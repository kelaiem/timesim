## 242. The faster beat rates re-gear only the escape mesh, so the train cannot start or fully swing the balance at 21,600-36,000 — CLOSED

Found fixing the stopped balance (TODO 238). A reader who picked a faster beat
rate from the panel saw the movement lock up after the boot sync: the hack
stopped the balance and nothing restarted it.

`RATE_TABLE` (layout.js) changes one mesh, the fourth⇄escape, so that the
fourth wheel keeps one turn a minute (§22). Everything upstream is the
18,000 movement's: the same mainspring, fusee and wheels. The hairspring is
re-solved to the rate (k ∝ F², TODO 25), so the spring the escapement has to
push grows fourfold at 36,000 while the escape torque falls by the escape
mesh's ratio. Measured on the TODO 238 drive (nominal corner, hanging):

| rate | escape torque | starts from rest | sustained swing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18,000 | 3.14e-7 N·m | yes | 199° |
| 21,600 | 2.62e-7 N·m | no (rests at 11.4°) | 161° |
| 28,800 | 1.96e-7 N·m | no (rests at 6.1°) | 112° |
| 36,000 | 1.57e-7 N·m | no (rests at 3.6°) | 85° |

The panel now says so (the spec verdict row, and a "Balance stopped" row
with a swing), which is honest, but the faster rows are not watches anyone
would build: a real 36,000 movement has a stronger mainspring and a train
cut for it.

**Fix path.** Make the rate a design input upstream of the escape mesh:
re-solve the going mainspring's torque (TODO 192 step 3's proportioning, run
at each rate) and the train's ratios so the escape torque delivers the energy
`AMPLITUDE_TARGET_DEG` needs at each rate's k, then re-check the fusee cut,
the reserve and the ribbon's stress gate. Acceptance: every `RATE_TABLE` row
reaches the target swing hanging at the nominal corner, and
`BALANCE_DRIVE.selfStart.starts` is true at every row.

**21,600 and 28,800: DONE, by the balance rather than the mainspring.** The
fix path above asked for a bigger mainspring, and the movement has none to
give: the drum is as large as the plate carries (`DRUM_R_ACTUAL`) and the
ribbon is proportioned to its stress limit (TODO 192 step 3). A faster rate
needs k ∝ I·F², so what a real high-beat calibre does instead is carry a
LIGHTER balance, and that is what each row now does. The arithmetic that
decides how much lighter: at 18,000 the balance spends 71% of each beat on its
pivots (μ·m·g·r, which goes with its MASS) and 29% on everything else
((π/2Q)·k·θ², which goes with its INERTIA), so a lighter rim cuts both, and
the hairspring a lighter balance needs at a faster rate comes out at about
the 18,000 balance's stiffness.

- **One dimension law** (`balanceWheelDims` in `layout.js`): the rim keeps its
  width (the stop work's pad annulus) and loses height with `rimF`, as TODO
  207 did; the arms and the timing screws' heads scaled with it, and below
  rimF 0.317 that would cut them under §50's stock floor, so they are held at
  the floor instead. A screw head at the floor stays inside the rim's band
  exactly while the rim is at or above the floor, so all three floors meet at
  rimF 0.2305, the lightest this wheel can be. The builder, `OSC_I` and the
  stop work's `HACK_SCREW_DROP` all read the one law.
- **Per rate** (`RATE_TABLE`: `rimF`, `claimDeg`, `peakDeg`): the rim is the
  heaviest, in `BAL_RIM_STEP` steps, that reaches `AMPLITUDE_TARGET_DEG` at the
  nominal corner hanging AND is started by the escapement from rest (TODO
  238's `selfStart`). `equalisation` row 15 now weighs the one-step-heavier
  balance by the same law (inertia, mass, the spring re-solved to it, the
  pivots' friction with the mass, the start by the same drive) and requires it
  to fail one of the two. That replaces `AMPLITUDE_TARGET_SLACK_DEG` (1.5°),
  which was one step's measured cost at 18,000 only. `probe-power-budget`
  recomputes the heavier balance's swing itself and agrees.

**36,000: DONE, by a smaller balance.** The lightest rim the metal allows

Three things the smaller balance moved, each fixed where it lives:

- **TODO 147's stud pitch.** The spiral packs into a smaller annulus, and at
  eight coils its pitch (0.6375) fell under the stud post's 0.65. `HS_COILS`
  is now the most coils, up to eight, whose pitch carries the post:
  `floor((HS_OUTER_R − HS_INNER_R) / 0.65)`, seven at 36,000 and still eight at
  r 9.
- **The balance cock had no seat.** Its slab ends 0.1 inside the cut edge,
  which on the balance's own bearings is its swept radius plus the cut margin,
  so the T-bar stands just outside the balance and the legs straddle it. At
  r 7.5 the bar came in, the legs splayed out of the 75° cut wedge on every
  bearing the stop work left open (best −0.582 at φ 25°), and the fallback
  bearing grew the plate's cut until it reached the escape pivot. The seat
  scan now lengthens the slab in 0.25 steps, the least that seats, which moves
  the feet in toward its axis: a position change of a fixture, with the jewel
  on the staff. Zero added wherever the cut edge alone seats, which is every
  other rate.
- **The arrest window owed the governor's a land.** The smaller balance moved
  the alarm arrest's station beside the governor's plate window. A LATE window
  (§201) was solved against holes, slots and keeps, but not against the windows
  already cut, so `checkPlateWindows` read a 0.017 land between them. A late
  window's rays now keep `TQ_LAND_MIN` from earlier windows' edges, held on the
  chords the outline emits rather than only its vertices
  (√(L² + (r·sin 0.5°)²), since the outline samples at most every half degree;
  without it the land read 1.900 against 1.900, under by less than the third
  decimal). The arrest solve's window filter asks the same question, so it
  picks a station whose window can open. Holding the window alone had left it
  NOT CUT, because a bossless window must open all the way round.
  `tqWindowEdges` and `tqEdgeLand` serve both.
