## 271. Form builds: the pillar seats are solved inside the round plate's rim

**What is wrong.** The four pillars are placed by an obstacle scan (`src/main.js`, the `// --- Pillars.`
block). For each ideal bearing (45°, 135°, 225°, 315°) it steps out 1° at a time, up to ±60°, and tries radii
from `plateR − 4` in to `plateR − 16`. A spot holds when `seatClearance(x, y) ≥ CLEAR_MARGIN`. One term of that
clearance is the round plate's rim, `plateR − |p|`, and the search ring is `plateR`'s too. Neither has a FORM
branch. Under `?case=` (§307/§308) both plates are cut to the form's outline, which reaches past that circle,
but the scan still reads the circle. That is TODO 270's coupling again, this time in the frame rather than a
window.

Measured on `main` at 3.6.10 (Chromium, each case at its seat's `?formrect=` from `src/gong-seats.js`), the
pillars stand at the same four points on the round build and on every form build:

| Pillar | Seat (x, y) | r | Bearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (32.49, 18.76) | 37.52 | 30° |
| 2 | (−32.38, 17.95) | 37.02 | 151° |
| 3 | (−24.05, −24.05) | 34.02 | −135° |
| 4 | (5.48, −34.59) | 35.02 | −81° |

`plateR` is 41.516. Every form outline reaches past that circle, so a form plate carries metal outboard of
every seat that the scan never considered.

**Why it matters: pillar 1 is in the strike corner.** Each pillar's screw seat is a hole in the three-quarter
plate (`tqHoles`, `PILLAR_SEAT_R`). On form builds the strike group's opening (TODO 266) owes that hole a land of
`TQ_LAND_MIN`. Pillar 1's seat is the "bore (32.49, 18.76)" in those records:
- TODO 266's lands for the cushion and tonneau bars (2.086 and 2.081) are to it;
- the tonneau's earlier bar seat was refused at 1.007 of plate to it (§310, "Since TODO 266/267");
- TODO 267 named it, beside the governor's window, as one of the two lands refusing the spiral stations.

The seated hammers' pivots stand at (44.40, 16.68) on the rect, (41.13, 24.05) on the cushion and
(40.82, 24.38) on the tonneau. So the strike group works in the corner beyond pillar 1, and pillar 1 stands
between that corner and the train. A pillar 1 sited against the outline, not the circle, can leave that
corner: further along the outline, or out of the strike group's bearings.

**Read the expected gain carefully.** Since TODO 270, `tools/gong-seats.mjs --write --modes spiral` refuses
every spiral seat that fits the first cut on one of three grounds (TODO 269's status). The land ground now
binds at bores (24.90, 20.89) on the rect and (22.22, 18.64) on the tonneau, and neither is a pillar seat. Freeing
pillar 1 gives the generator's maps more room in the strike corner, but it is not shown to close a spiral
seat. Measure that, and don't assume it.

**Fix path.**
- **Under FORM, read the plate that is cut.** The rim term becomes the outline's inset, `FORM.inset(x, y)`, the
  same swap TODO 270 made in `tqOpeningClearance`. The search radii run out to the outline along each bearing,
  not to `plateR − 4`. The cap still needs its land to the outline and the case.
- **Keep the round build bit for bit.** It takes neither branch, so its four seats and the fingerprint do not
  move.
- **Re-check what the frame carries.** The pillars hold the three-quarter plate and set the plate screws'
  spread, so a pillar moved to an outline corner changes the plate's support span. On the support check, the
  frame joints (`Pillar ⇄ base plate`, `FRAME_JOINTS`) and §184's joint bound, the move must read at least as
  well as the round seats do.
- **Order.** The pillars are solved before the strike opening is cut. A pillar that yields to the opening, the
  way TODO 270's windows do, is a separate decision and must be stated in the item. The frame's members are P1
  structure, and the opening is a clearance.
- **Then re-run the seat generator.** `node tools/gong-seats.mjs --write` (both modes) gives the result. Record
  what moves: the bars' lands, and which spiral refusals remain.

**Why no gate saw it.** Form builds are UNVERIFIED (TODO 158). On the round build the rim term is right.

**Done when.**
