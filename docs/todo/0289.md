## 289. The setting fold's shafts run in no bearing: the rise, stub and cap arbor stand on their bevel meshes alone — PART DONE

Filed from the owner's report (2026-11-06): "four spiders floating" by the
keyless works — the bevel blanks at the setting cap and the setting rise,
standing in the air with nothing holding the shafts they are keyed to.

**What is wrong.** The §234 fold carries the setting path from the keyless
minute arbor to the motion works through five bevel corners and five rods
(`buildSettingMetal`). Every rod is a `makeRodSegment` cylinder from one
apex to the next, and every blank is keyed to a rod — but no rod runs in
anything. A bevel mesh can hold no shaft: it is a line contact between two
cones, and the blank it belongs to is free to leave it in every direction a
bearing would forbid. `support` cannot see this because it is declared per
UNIT (`['Keyless works', 'plate']`, the stem bushing's foot) and one floating
member inside a seated unit passes it in silence — roadmap §180 names that
exact gap. §54's slenderness report knew it too: the Keyless works row it
retired at §234 read "supported by nothing over 28.84 u between its two bevel
corners", and the fix was to SHORTEN the bar, not to bear it.

### Built — the tail: the rise, the stub and the cap arbor (this item)

Three plain bushes and one jewel, every number derived, the fold's own solve
re-run with the bearings' room as a constraint, the solved record published
at `__clock.settingFold.bearings` and held by `tools/probe-fold-bearings.mjs`
(an acceptance test now, with a control):

- **Where a bush can stand.** A rod's two blanks leave no run between them,
  so a bush goes OUTBOARD of an apex on the rod's own extension — through
  the empty cone-distance sphere every bevel pair leaves round its apex
  (no metal inside ρ = `coneRi` of the apex) — or the rod is lengthened for
  it. The rise extends past E, the cap arbor past D, and the stub is
  lengthened so its bush sits between its two blanks (the one rod whose length
  is a free solve variable, `stubSolve`, which takes the floor
  `zWebHi_foot + m + L_bush + m + zWebHi_cap` beside its envelope scan). The
  stub's ends stop a rod radius plus the margin short of the two extensions
  that now pass through E and D, so the rods no longer meet in a lens at the
  apex (they did, by one radius, on every corner; the tail's two are cut
  clear now, the other three are untouched).
- **The cap arbor's upper bearing is a jewel in the base plate**, the
  dial-side pivot idiom (`addDialSidePivot`: shaft up to the stone, setting
  set into the dial face), set FLUSH in the cap pocket's floor rather than
  proud of it, because the cap's top face stands exactly one margin under
  that floor and a proud rim would eat it. The arbor runs up through the
  cap's bore, the margin and the stone.
- **Bush length is the overhung-bevel rule.** Where a bush is the only
  bearing a load has, the bearing spread is at least twice the overhang
  (Dudley, *Handbook of Practical Gear Design*, overhung bevel mounting:
  2×, 2.5× preferred); a straddled load (the cap pinion and its blank,
  between the jewel and the bush) asks nothing of the bush's length beyond
  a plain journal's own L/D ≥ 1. The overhang is from the bush's end to the
  blank's load point, the mean cone distance's axial station
  `(coneR + coneRi)/2 · cos γ`.
- **Bore and wall.** `PIVOT_BORE_CLEAR` over the rod (the repo's one
  running fit for a pivot in a bore; the alarm link's 0.02 was flagged at
  §137 as inherited and is not copied), and `STOCK_MIN_U` of wall — the
  bush carries the hand-set's separating force, which no section at §50's
  floor can fail.
- **Hangers.** Each bush hangs from the base plate's dial face on an arm and
  a column (the alarm link's hanger idiom, §121/§202: bush socketed in its
  arm, column planted in the plate), square section `STOCK_MIN_U`, the arm
  leaving the bush OUTWARD from the motion works' axis — inward is the
  minute wheel, which fills the plate's underside to r 10.3 — at a standoff
  SOLVED as the least that clears every fold blank's swept envelope, the cap
  pinion's swept tip, the two motion-works discs and the plate's opening
  (the merged cap pocket and rise bore) by the margin, and foots the column
  on plate metal. The cap-bearing scan builds the hangers into every
  candidate's scratch metal, so the reserve train's swing solve judges them
  with the rest of the fold.
