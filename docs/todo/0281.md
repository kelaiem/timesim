## 281. The free outline's port faces are raster staircases, not flats — CLOSED

**What's wrong.** §325's fit (`layout.js fitFreeCase`) gives each PORT — a part that passes through the wall and seats
on it, the alarm pusher's flat button above all (TODO 275) — a block whose face stands square to the port's line, so
the closing "leaves the outline flat there". It leaves it flat only to the raster's resolution. The lug ends are
SNAPPED exactly straight after the contour (`snap`, the strip's vertices set on the reach line); the port faces are
not, and a slanted face read off a `FREE_H` (0.1 u) field is a staircase: on the 19 mm row the pusher's face runs
(45.906, 4.780) (45.843, 5.180) (45.806, 5.480) (45.743, 5.880) … its x steps alternating −0.063 and −0.037, a line
only to about a tenth of a cell.

**Where it shows.** main.js's TODO 275 guard holds every outer-loop vertex within the button's reach of the exit
behind the seat plane at 1e-9, the plane taken off the local edge at the exit. Whether a neighbouring step stands in
front of that plane is then a matter of where the raster falls — and the case's grown loops (form-case.js
`formCaseLoops`) re-raster from the outline's own bounds, which the lug ends move with the strap. Measured on the
generated rows (`?case=free&strap=W`, the movement with the alarm): 14 mm warns `the case wall stands 0.0412 proud
of the pusher button's seat plane at (51.228, 4.810)`, 15 and 16 mm 0.0408 at (51.215, 4.910); 17, 18, 19 and 20 mm
are silent. The outline itself is IDENTICAL near the exit between 14 and 19 mm (vertex for vertex, y 1.7–9.4): the
19 mm row is silent by the raster's alignment, not by construction. 0.041 u is 0.016 mm — the button's rim buries
that far into the wall on one side.

**Fix path.** Snap the port faces as the lug ends are snapped, in `fitFreeCase`: for each port, the outline's
vertices inside its strip (|t| ≤ half) whose distance along u is within the raster's noise of the face's reach set
ON the face line, collinear runs collapsed, the fillets beside it left as contoured (the lug ends' own reasoning
about kinks). Then regenerate every row (`tools/free-outline.mjs --write --spec …`, the three movements and the
picker's strap widths: probe-325-free-case.mjs S names them) and re-boot each: the guard must be silent on every
row. Add the face's straightness to probe-325's F3 beside the lug ends', with a control (an unsnapped row fails).

**Closed (same branch, filed and fixed in one landing).** `fitFreeCase`'s `snap` now snaps each port's face as it snaps
the lug ends: inside the port's strip, a vertex within half a cell (`FREE_H / 2`, the cell-centre transform's worst
miss) of the face is moved OUT along u onto it, and where two strips overlap (the pusher and the winding stem, 8°
apart) a vertex goes to the face it is NEAREST, never onto a neighbour's line. The grow that cuts the case's rings
(`freeOffset`) contours an exact distance field, so a straight run stays straight out to the outer loop the guard
reads. All nine rows re-derived with `tools/free-outline.mjs --write --fresh` (without `--fresh` the tool settles on
the committed row when the axis and area agree, and the first pass kept every staircase): the with-alarm movement's
seven straps 897.09–950.33 mm² (each +≤ 0.02 mm², the outward snap) and the two alarm-off movements 849.57 and 877.33
mm², every row holding its metal at the rim margin and booting with only its standing rows — TODO 275's guard silent
on 14, 15 and 16 mm. `probe-325-free-case.mjs` W1 holds it: every port-strip vertex ON its face (the table's 1e-4
quantum: the writer rounds every point, which moves a point off a slanted line by up to 0.71e-4) or more than half a
cell behind it; it fails on the pre-snap table (0.0448 u) and its control (a face vertex moved in 0.02 u) fires.
