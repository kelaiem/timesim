## 286. The fusee cock's foot carries the arm's twist through one screw's clamp, unmodelled — CLOSED (§336 landing 4)

Filed by §336, which built the fusee cock.

**What is wrong.** The fusee cock carries the fusee arbor's top bush at the end
of a 20-unit arm, and the bush's reaction is SIDEWAYS: the chain's pull, the
great wheel's mesh force and, at the stall, the stop's force and the winding
input all lie in the plate's plane. §336 sized the arm (in-plane bending), the
post (bending and twist) and the leg (bending over its height, twist from the
arm) against the plate metal's yield. What it did not size is the JOINT at the
foot: the arm's moment about the leg's axis, R·L = **35.1 N·mm** at the stall
bound on §47's build (R 4.64 N, L 20 u), and **60.6 N·mm** on the default build since §336 landing 3, where the
stop-work's stud puts its stall force into the crank as well (R 5.24 N and 3.23 N at the seat, L 20.25 u), has to pass from the leg into the base plate, and the
only thing there is one foot screw (`COCK_THREAD_D`, sized by TODO 184 for the
plate it grips, not for a torque). A screw's clamp carries a torque only through
the friction under its head and pad, μ·F_preload·r_eff, and no preload is
modelled anywhere in this frame.

The other cocks have the same construction and do not have the load: the
escape and fork cocks carry jewelled pivots at milli-newtons.

**Why it is not fixed in §336.** It needs a quantity the movement does not
carry yet (a screw's preload, from its torque and thread, under `FRICTION`'s
rule for a coefficient: a band with its source), and the fix that a workshop
would make is a part, not a number: **steady pins**. A cock is located and
keyed by two steady pins pressed into the plate beside its screw, which carry
the torque in bearing and shear while the screw only holds the cock down.

**Fix path.**
1. Add two steady pins to the fusee cock's foot, on the diameter perpendicular
   to the arm, pressed into the base plate: each carries R·L / d in shear,
   sized against the plate metal's shear yield (`NICKEL_SILVER_SIGMA_Y_PA`/√3)
   and the base plate's bearing; their holes join `BACK_PLATE_LATE_HOLES` with a
   stock floor of land.
2. Declare each as a `FRAME_JOINTS` row of its kind (a pressed pin, not a
   screw) and hold it to the plate in `plateSeats`.
3. Then the escape and fork cocks for consistency, whose torques are small but
   whose construction is the same.

`tools/probe-336-fusee-cock.mjs` reports the twist and does not gate it; when
this closes, it gates the pins.

**Closed by §336 landing 4 (2026-11-06): the steady pins.** The foot is a FLANGE as deep as the base plate, bored for the
screw and two steady pins pressed through the plate and up into the flange, so each pin engages the cock as deep as it
engages the plate. The pins stand on a diameter of the foot, each in a lobe of the flange (the pin and a §50 floor of metal,
a floor from the leg's foot too), and carry the whole twist as a couple, F = Tq / (2·r_p): the screw's clamp is not counted
on. Each pin's radius is the largest of its shear in the one steel (`SPRING_TAU_Y_PA`), its bearing in the nickel silver of
plate and flange alike (F/(d·t)), and the §50 floor. The pins' azimuth about the foot is a freedom of the cock's foot scan: a
seat is feasible when the flange's core and both lobes clear the column. (A round flange big enough for the pins found no seat
at the foot's 40° and moved the cock to 232°, hiding the cone back to 75%.)
