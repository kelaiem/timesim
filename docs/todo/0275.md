## 275. The alarm pusher's cap buries in a form case's oblique wall: a flat button seated on the wall's tangent plane — CLOSED

Reported by the owner (2026-10-27), on the form shapes: the alarm pusher's head
collides with the case wall on the rect and the tonneau.

**What was measured.** The head was a cylinder, 2 mm across and 0.62 of its
radius deep, with its flat inner face SQUARE TO THE PRESS LINE. TODO 241 solves
that face to land on the wall at the end of the carry, exactly, but only ON THE
LINE. §170 runs the line through the movement's centre, which is the round band's
normal, so on the round case a face square to the line is the wall's tangent
plane and clears the band everywhere off the line. A FORM wall is not a circle.
The line leaves it obliquely, so the face touched at its centre and its rim on
the high side stood in the band. Rays cast along the line across the head's face,
from far outboard, at full press (`alarmPress` f = 0.5):

| case | wall's tilt to the line | head's rim in the band |
|---|---|---|
| round | 0° | 0.02 (the designed seat sink) |
| `?case=cushion` | 4.2° | 0.086 |
| `?case=tonneau` | 5.6° | 0.253 |
| `?case=rect` | 18.3° | 0.846 |
| `?case=rect&barrelstep=0&rsvaz=36&alarmmod=80` | 38.3° | 1.545, inside the band from f 0.3 |

The flush bore's sleeve had the same defect from the other side. Its outboard
end was square to the line at the exit point, so on the rect it stood up to
0.78 u proud of the wall, and the head landed on that.

**Why no gate saw it.** The `Alarm switch ⇄ Case` floors row and
`probe-case-relief`'s STOP set excuse `alarmPusherCap` against `caseMiddle` and
`casePusherBoreSleeve` BY NAME, because TODO 241's stop is a contact between
exactly those bodies. The excuse covers the pair, not where on the pair it
touches. The one form build the battery sweeps is the arrangement, where the
buried rim reads as the stop.

**The fix: a flat button cut to the wall, and a wall that faces it.**

- **One law for which way the wall faces.** `caseWallNormal(u, off)` in
  `main.js` sits beside TODO 268's `caseWallAlong`. Under FORM it is the outer
  loop's edge where `rayExit` crosses it (`rayExitNormal`, `form-case.js`, on the
  loop `makeFormCase` cuts on). On the round case it is the exit point's radial,
  returned as `u` itself on a radial line.
- **The button.** A turned disc (`LatheGeometry`) on its own axis, the wall's
  normal, centred on the press line. Its inner face is the seat plane through
  the line at the stem's published outer end. The stop is unchanged: at T = 1 the
  face crosses the line `ALARM_SEAT_SINK` past the wall's exit point, the
  stroke's own coordinate. Its radius is §43's 1 mm ergonomic floor, with a 45°
  edge break inside it. Two styling inputs are declared in the head radius's
  standing: FLAT is the owner's word, a 0.40 mm button, and a 0.10 mm edge
  break. The thickness is the larger of the style and the structural floor
  below.
- **The stem's spigot.** The stem's end is square to its line, so it reaches
  past the seat plane by the plane's fall across it, r·tanθ (`PUSHER_SPIGOT`),
  and the whole end is inside the button. The spigot's deepest point is
  2·r·sinθ into the button along the normal, and the button carries §50's stock
  floor over it. That floor governs on the arrangement (1.596 u, 0.605 mm). The
  style governs everywhere else.
- **The sleeve and the liner end on the tangent plane.** `cutEndOnPlane`
  (`geometry.js`) is the one law, read by `makeCase`, `makeFormCase` and the
  §202 liner. A slope of 0 returns the geometry untouched, so the round case's
  bytes do not move. The stop's seat assert reads the sleeve's end through the
  same plane, each vertex's height along the normal over the normal's component
  along the line.
- **The stem's bar is the stem.** It read "the stem and the cap, one piece of
  stock". §203 cut the head from case metal and the stem from steel, and a head
  as wide as it is deep is a disc, which `turning` refuses to cluster. So the
  bar is the stem and its spigot, and the stem's radius is that bar's fixed
  point. On the round case the stock still governs, bit for bit. On the
  arrangement the bar is 37.19 u and asks r 1.0331, where stem-and-cap asked
  1.0559.
- **Asserted at build, with the numbers.** The case wall stands behind the seat
  plane wherever the button reaches. That is the claim the cut rests on, read off
  the outer loop's vertices; every shape in the library is convex there. The edge
  break leaves a land and a face, and the spigot keeps the floor of metal over it.
