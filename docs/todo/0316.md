## 316. The maintaining spring's pin is seated in the ring's root land with no real wall — CLOSED (§339)

Filed 2026-11-16, found while landing §339 3f, which chooses the maintaining ring from its click's worst recoil
instead of a whole pitch.

**What is wrong.** The maintaining spring's pin (TODO 219) is let into the ring's underside to mid-thickness at
r_P ± its radius. r_P is the least radius the great wheel's crossing allows: the hub land, plus the margin, plus the
pin's own radius. The ring's teeth keep the 24-tooth cut's proportions, so its root is R·(1 − 4.8/N). Nothing relates
the two.

- **The default (25 teeth):** the pin's outer edge stands at r 3.552 and the root at 3.588. The hole's wall to the
  tooth gaps is 0.036, against §50's STOCK_MIN_U of 0.317.
- **`alarm=0&barrels=2` (25 teeth, landing 3c):** the doubled going torque thickens the pin to r_out 3.698. The hole
  already breaks out of the root land by 0.11, into the gaps where the detent's beak rides. Only the crossing's phase
  keeps the beak off the exposed pin.
- **The 20-tooth ring the recoil rule first chose:** the hole breaks out by 0.178, and the beak passes the pin at
  0.145 against the 0.15 floor (`expectedContacts`, train f=0.5417).

No gate holds the pin's seat to the ring's metal. The pair's clearance floor reads whatever phase the crossing gives.

**Fix path.** The pin needs a seat with a real wall, derived from §50's floor, wherever the ring's tooth count puts the
root. The options are a position-space move, never a thinner pin:
- seat the pin in a collar under the toothed band;
- bring r_P inward through a re-cut hub land;
- or stop scaling the tooth depth with the pitch, and re-derive the beak's valley.

§339 3f carries the no-break-out condition (wall 0) in the ring's choice, with its witness in the record. That is the
floor of the fix, not the fix.

**Acceptance.** A row in the equalisation record holds the wall between the pin's hole and the ring's root (and the
pin's top and the beak's band) to §50's floor on every build. The beak ⇄ pin floor holds at every phase of the
crossing, not only the one the build cuts.

**Closed by §339 landing 3f, 2026-11-17.** The pin moved inward rather than the ring growing. The great wheel's crossings
began at twice the bore (r 2.8), a proportion of `makeGear`'s, not a constraint. Cut to the hub's own term
(`landToHub`: the hub ring plus 0.35 module, r 2.41), the crossing seats the pin 0.44 nearer the arbor. Every ring's
choice now requires its root to stand §50's floor outside the pin's hole. The record carries the seat
(`maintainingHold.spring.pinSeat`), and the equalisation gate re-derives its wall from the radii and holds it to the
floor; its two controls (a short wall, a wall that disagrees with its radii) both fail. Measured:

| build | teeth | wall |
|---|---|---|
| default | 22 | 0.361 |
| `alarm=0&barrels=2` | 25 | at least 0.317 |
| `alarm=0&barrels=2&vph=36000` | 25 | at least 0.317 |

The beak, which cannot enter the root land, stands at least that wall off the pin at every phase of the crossing.

A collar under the ring was measured first and refused. The daylight between the great wheel's top face and the ring
gives 0.294 after the margin, under §50's 0.317. The beak hangs to 0.022 over the ring's underside, so the click would
have had to lose 0.128 of its 0.392 to clear a collar. That thinner arm bends at 1113 MPa at the doubled torque, and
the 36,000 beak no longer fits its tooth. Keeping the click whole would have raised the fusee sandwich by about 0.15 u.
