## 296. The escape wheel's club teeth taper to a 10.83° point, and nothing prices the tip against the load it locks and impulses with — DONE

Reported by eye (the owner, 2026-11-08): the escape wheel's teeth "look a
little thin at the ends". They are, and the number is worse than it looks:
measured off the authored outline, the club's last 0.038 mm is 0.010 mm wide,
and not one instrument in the repo asks whether that metal survives the
escapement's own load.

**What the cut says.** `makeEscapeWheel` (src/geometry.js) walks one ring of
15 teeth, 24° of pitch each, at `radius` = `ESCAPE_WHEEL_R` = 4.5 u (1.70 mm)
and `thickness` 0.8 u (0.303 mm). The club is three authored points plus
TODO 226 step 3's undercut: the heel `H` at r 4.05 (0.03 of a pitch ahead of
the tooth centre), the tip `T` at r 4.5 (0.22 ahead), the locking foot `L` at
r 3.6 (0.17 ahead), and `U` leaving the tip along the inward radial raked
`TOOTH_RAKE_DEG` = 24° back against the wheel's travel.

So the two faces that meet AT THE TIP are the impulse face (`H`→`T`, 0.564 u
long) and the undercut's locking face (`T`→`U`), and the angle between them
is **10.827°** — fixed by `rakeDeg` and the heel's offset, and unchanged by
the undercut's depth (measured identical at every depth from 0.05 to 0.4 u).
The club is a wedge of that angle, so its width perpendicular to the radius
is:

| back from the tip | in-plane width | section at 0.8 u (0.303 mm) deep |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 u (0.019 mm) | 0.0134 u = **0.0051 mm** | 0.0015 mm² |
| 0.10 u (0.038 mm) | 0.0268 u = **0.0102 mm** | 0.0031 mm² |
| 0.20 u (0.076 mm) | 0.0537 u = **0.0204 mm** | 0.0062 mm² |
| 0.45 u (0.170 mm — the heel circle) | 0.2216 u = 0.0840 mm | 0.0255 mm² |

The width first reaches `STOCK_MIN_U` (0.12 mm) at about r 3.86 u — 0.64 u
(0.242 mm) back from the tip, which is BELOW the club heel. The whole club is
thinner in the sheet than the floor for sheet metal, and its last 0.038 mm is
thinner than `PIVOT_MIN_U` (0.07 mm), the floor under the most slender
feature the movement admits anywhere.

**And the tip is the working surface.** TODO 226 step 3 cut the undercut
precisely so that only the tip touches: the stone's corner stands
`undercut − CLEAR_MARGIN` inside the tip circle, raked behind it, so the
lock, the draw and the impulse all bear on that wedge. The movement prices
the FORCE already (`going.energy`'s escape torque, the per-beat gross, each
stage's η at all three `FRICTION` corners; §238 traces the contact and reads
the torque off the metal by virtual work), and it prices every PIVOT's
bending stress at its service load against `SPRING_SIGMA_Y_PA` (TODO 192
step 2). It has never multiplied the two here. A tooth that yields at its tip
loses its lock depth and its drop; a tooth that breaks stops the watch.

**Why nothing has said so.** Four instruments pass it, each for its own
reason:

- `stockFloor` (§50) reads a mesh's geometry-LOCAL bounding box, whose
  smallest side for this extrude is the 0.8 u depth, so the in-plane taper is
  invisible — TODO 225's mechanism exactly. The wheel's existing §50 waiver
  (`Escape wheel` → TODO 12) is a RADIAL BAND row: a different dimension.
- TODO 225's probe (`tools/probe-137-hidden-thin.mjs`) CAN see in-plane
  sub-floor metal, and its screen was deliberately set not to flag this —
  "a gear tooth tapers to a point by design", so the threshold was raised
  until the wheels dropped out of a cut that first returned 39 rows. Its two
  flagged `Escape wheel` rows are the escape PINION's leaves and an annulus,
  not the club.
- TODO 131's guard holds the cut metal's REACH to `radius` (which is what
  lets the stones be seated on a circle the wheel has) and says nothing about
  the section behind the tip.
- `outlines` (TODO 100) holds the ring simple, and `slenderness` (§54) reads
  the same bounding box `stockFloor` does. Neither is a strength claim.

So this is not a measurement that came back green. It is a question nobody
has asked of this part, which is the class TODO 225 named and left open on
purpose.

**What is owed.** Price the tip the way TODO 192 step 2 priced the pivots,
from the load the energy column already computes — P1, structural truth
inside the escapement group:

1. Take the tooth load at the lock, through the draw and over the impulse
   from `going.energy` / §238's traced contact (escape torque ÷ the contact
   radius, at all three `FRICTION` corners), and say WHERE on the face each
   bears — the lock is at the tip, the impulse slides down the face, and they
   are different stations with different sections.
2. Re-derive the club as a cantilever off the heel circle at the wedge's own
   section (b = the measured width at the bearing station, h = the 0.303 mm
   thickness) and hold σ under a declared limit for the wheel's steel: a new
   constant with its alloy band's source, on `SPRING_SIGMA_Y_PA`'s and
   `MAINSPRING_SIGMA_Y_PA`'s pattern, not either of those by reference.
3. Add the HERTZIAN side. The lock bears a stone's corner on a wedge, so the
   contact stress at the tip is what decides whether the tip deforms before
   it bends, and a tip that deforms re-cuts its own lock.
4. If the metal does not carry it, the fix is the TOOTH FORM, not a thicker
   wheel. A real Swiss club has a flat impulse plane and a locking face that
   leaves a tip LAND, not a point; `radius`, `teeth` and the undercut are
   each a change with its own clearance chain (the stones' seats, the fork
   blank's reach, `escapeAngle`, §136's conjugate cut). Rule 1 either way:
   whatever lands, the number derives from the load, not from how it looks.

**Scope.** This item is the escape wheel's own teeth. The same question
stands over every pinion leaf in the train and is NOT claimed here — TODO 225
lists those rows, TODO 255 and 256 own the mesh law.

---

**Landing 1 — the tip is priced, and the tooth form stands.**

`EQUALISATION.going.energy.escapeTooth` is the row, beside the pivots' (row 12)
and the maintaining hold's (row 16), and `equalisation` row 20 gates it. The
answer: **the club tooth carries the escapement at ×4.96, and first yields at
24.6× the escape torque.** The owner's eye was right that the metal is thin; the
LOAD is 201 µN.

**One bearing station, not three.** Step 1 asked for the lock, the draw and the
impulse as "different stations with different sections". They are not: TODO 226
step 3 cut the undercut so that only the tip touches, and `deltaOnImpulseFace`
puts the tooth CIRCLE on the stone's face, so through the whole beat it is the
TIP that bears and the club's own impulse face never meets the stone. That is
the metal's answer to the item's guess, and it makes the question one question.

**The wedge, re-measured, and the trap it carries.** 10.8266° — the item's
figure, confirmed, with the locking flank leaving the tip at exactly
`TOOTH_RAKE_DEG` 24.000° and the impulse flank at 34.827°. But both flanks lie
on the SAME side of the tip's radius, so the club's width is their DIFFERENCE:
0.25048 u per unit of radial depth, where the item's table used 0.268 and the
first draft of this measurement used their SUM, 1.14094, reading the wedge as
58.83° and every section 4.56× too fat. A gear tooth's flanks straddle its
radius, which is what makes the sum the reflex. Corrected widths:

| back from the tip | in-plane width |
|---|---|
| 0.05 u | 4.75 µm |
| 0.10 u (0.038 mm) | **9.49 µm** |
| 0.20 u | 18.98 µm |
| 0.3004 u (the undercut's root, where the wedge ends) | 28.51 µm |

So the item's qualitative point is right and stronger than it said: the whole
club wedge — every section from the tip to the undercut's root 0.114 mm in — is
under 0.0285 mm, thinner than `PIVOT_MIN_U` (0.07 mm) as well as `STOCK_MIN_U`
(0.12 mm). (The item's "reaches STOCK_MIN_U at about r 3.86" was wrong twice
over: on the corrected taper the extrapolated wedge would need 1.264 u of depth,
which is past the valley; `PIVOT_MIN_U` would need 0.738 u, also past the
wedge's end.)

**The load, off the metal.** The contact normal's moment arm about the wheel's
axis is what turns the escape torque into a tooth load, and it is NOT the tooth
circle: the locking face leans only by the draw plus the fork/wheel radial
difference, so its arm is 4.251 and 4.473 (of a 4.5 wheel), while the stone's
IMPULSE face is inclined 24° to the tooth circle and its arm is only **1.847** —
so the impulse's forward load is 2.4× what τ/R would say. Sampled over each run
the tip makes and kept at its smallest.

**The worst load is the UNLOCK, and the corners invert there.** A forward run
carries the corner's delivered T·η; the unlock carries T/η, because the balance
drives the train BACKWARD through the draw and every stage's friction opposes it
(§238's own reading). So a forward run's load FALLS toward the adverse corner
and the recoil's RISES — 201.1 µN adverse against 56.1 µN for the same corner's
locked tooth, 3.58× — and row 20 gates both senses, because a record that priced
the unlock forward would still look monotone.

**The tip is an ARC, because a point is singular.** The beam's stress goes as
1/s into the apex and the Hertz pressure as 1/√ρ, so a mathematically sharp club
has no finite strength and the only honest price is on the radius the METAL
carries. This is TODO 221's treatment of the maintaining beak's apex applied to
the one other wedge in the movement that bears a load on its point, including
its `binds` field. Two new constants, each with its own source rather than
either neighbour by reference:

- `ESCAPE_STEEL_SIGMA_Y_BAND` 1.5–1.8 GPa — the escape wheel's own hardened and
  tempered carbon steel. The figures coincide with `CLICK_STEEL_SIGMA_Y_BAND`
  today and are declared apart on purpose: a blade is drawn back soft enough to
  come back from its working stress, a click is tempered for a hold, and a wheel
  is hardened for WEAR at a tip that slides on ruby five times a second, so a
  measurement of either part must move one band and not both.
- `ESCAPE_TIP_R_BAND` 0.1–2 µm — the edge radius of a polished corner on
  hardened steel. A finish, not a cut, so it cannot be read off the outline; a
  BAND for FRICTION's reason, and every gate reads the SHARP end, because a
  sharper tip is the loaded one.

At that sharp end, against E* 138.8 GPa for steel on ruby and an allowable of
1.79 × 1.5 GPa:

| | worst row (adverse, the unlock) |
|---|---|
| Hertz p0 | 0.541 GPa, ×4.96 |
| the arc's root section in bending + axial | 12.43 MPa, ×120.7 |
| ρ at first yield | **4.07 nm** (Hertz binds) against the band's sharp 100 nm |

**So step 4 is not triggered.** The tooth form stands: nothing in the load asks
for a tip land, and rule 1 cuts both ways — a land added because the teeth look
thin would be exactly the number that "appeared because it looked right". Worth
recording that the verdict is ROBUST to the geometry error that wrecked the
first measurement: the arc's section is 2ρ·cos α and the Hertz pressure does not
see α at all, so reading the wedge as a sum changes σ by 16% and p0 not at all.
The club's thinness and the club's strength turn out to be different questions.

**Held at both ends.** `tools/probe-296-escape-tooth.mjs` is the second reader:
it measures the wedge off the CUT MESH's own side-wall triangle normals where
the record reads the authored outline (they agree to 1e-6, which is TODO 131's
`bevelOffset = -bevel` claim tested at the tip), re-solves the escapement and
recomputes every arm, and re-prices all 18 rows from layout.js's bands. Five
controls fire: the sum-vs-difference trap, the recoil's inverted corners, a tip
ρ/100 (which goes over in contact, so the criterion is not vacuous), an arm
taken square to the face instead of along it, and twice the load (p0 must move
by exactly √2). `tools/probe-296-tooth-gate.mjs` watches the GATE fire, on a
cloned record rather than a patched tree: eight sabotages, all refused and named
— including the escape torque raised to 27.1×, just past the 24.6× the tip first
yields at, which is derived from the margin rather than picked.

**The control that mattered most was virtual work.** An arm read off the wrong
edge, or a normal taken square to the wrong vector, comes back a plausible
number — and nothing else in the record would notice if BOTH arms were wrong
together. So every arm is held to `arm_fork / arm_wheel = dδ/dφ` against the
escapement solve's own gearing, at boot and again in the probe: 8.9e-10. The
sabotage that takes the arm square to the face reads 1.368 instead of 4.251 and
virtual work reads 8.6.

**Still owed: the DROP's impact.** The reflected wheel carries
I 1.345e-11 kg·m²; free through the 1.5° drop the train gives it 4.48e-9 J (a
whole beat delivers 3.58e-8 J), against **6.12e-16 J** stored elastically in the
tip at the working load — so the landing arrives with some 7.3 million times the
contact's elastic energy, and THAT is the load a thin tip really fears. It is
not priced here, and the reason is not shyness: its path runs through the stone
into the fork and out at the banking pin, and none of the fork's impact, the
bank's compliance or the stone's seat is modelled. A number produced without
them would be an invention in the unsafe direction. `probe-296-escape-tooth.mjs`
REPORTS the energy and the ratio so this paragraph has a source.

**Landing 2 — the drop's landing does not break the tooth. It runs in.**

Landing 1 named this load and left it: the wheel arrives from the drop with some
7.3 million times the elastic energy the tip's contact stores at the working
load. It is priced now (`going.energy.escapeTooth.landing`, `equalisation` row
21), and the answer is that the question was the wrong shape. **The tip does
yield at the first landings — and then it stops: it blunts to 0.299 µm, the
contact shakes down, and that radius is inside the finish band the wheel is
declared to have.** It costs the tooth 0.00504 u of reach, 3.4% of a
`CLEAR_MARGIN`.

The peak swing is deliberate: TODO 192 step 4's rule — a peak load is priced at
the largest swing the balance can REACH — one mechanism along. No restitution
coefficient appears anywhere, and the fork is taken AT REST, which is the bound:
between the let-off and the landing it coasts toward its banking, which is the
way the landing tooth will push it, so any speed it has only opens the gap. The
model PACES the drop across the run, so the instant the tip touches is not
resolved by it; the arm is the largest over that whole window.

**Where the energy goes, and this is the finding.** The series compliance is not
the Hertz contact — it is the TOOTH, by three orders of magnitude:

| | |
|---|---|
| the club as a tapered cantilever loaded at its tip arc (EI y″ = F·s, I = t(k·s)³/12, integrated from the arc's tangent station to where the wedge ends) | 21,491 N/m |
| the Hertz line contact at the band's sharp end | 22,618,265 N/m |
| the series | 21,471 N/m — **1052× the tooth's** |

That matters for a reason beyond interest. A 2-D line contact's approach is only
defined against a reference length (Johnson §5.2), so the contact's compliance
is the one figure here that a reasonable person could write down differently —
and because the tooth governs, it cannot move the answer. Measured rather than
asserted: ×10 on the contact's stiffness moves the run-in by 0.086% and ÷10 by
0.844%. The gate holds the series within a percent of the tooth's and the
contact at over 100× it, so a stack the contact governed would be a different
claim and would fail.

**And the criterion is SHAKEDOWN, not first yield.** A contact that repeats ten
times a second for the life of a watch is not held to the elastic limit: past
first yield the apex deforms a little, residual stresses build, and the contact
becomes elastic again at a higher load. `LINE_CONTACT_SHAKEDOWN_P0_PER_K = 4.0`
is Johnson's shakedown limit for a line contact (Contact Mechanics (1985) §9.1 /
Table 9.1), converted with k = Y/√3. It is the same table as the first-yield
constant already in the tree — whose 1.79·Y IS that table's elastic limit 3.1k,
since 1.79·√3 = 3.100 — so the two are held to ONE source and the gate asserts
that identity before using either. First yield stays the criterion for a hold
that must not yield at all (TODO 221's maintaining beak) and for the
escapement's quasi-static load (landing 1).

**It also vindicates landing 1's reading.** Landing 1 priced the quasi-static
load at the band's SHARP end on the grounds that a sharper tip is the loaded
one. Landing 2 says that end is only ever the NEW tooth's: after the first
landings the tip stands at 0.299 µm, where the static margin is ×8.57 rather
than ×4.96. The conservative choice was conservative in service too.

**Four controls decide whether the number means anything**
(`probe-296-escape-tooth.mjs` §6, all firing):

- the contact's stiffness ×10 / ÷10 → 0.086% / 0.844% (above);
- **one more series member as soft as the tooth → the run-in falls to 0.1859 µm.**
  That is the load-bearing control, because the fork's arm, the stone's seat and
  the banking pin are NOT modelled and each is a further series member. Softening
  the stack runs the tip in LESS, so leaving them out makes 0.299 µm an UPPER
  bound — proved by direction rather than asserted in prose, and the gate fails a
  record that does not NAME the three it leaves out;
- the design swing (200°) instead of the peak → 0.2350 µm, so pricing at the
  peak is the conservative reading;
- the drop's acceleration removed → 5.53 nJ of the 10.00 and a run-in of
  0.2088 µm, so the drop contributes 44.7% of the energy and the tip would blunt
  even without it.

An earlier draft of the first control was a no-op — it reassigned the exported
function while the closures read the local const, and reported a 0.000%
sensitivity that was its own patch doing nothing. It now reads the multiplier as
a parameter and asserts the sensitivity is non-zero as well as small, which is
the shape a sensitivity control has to have.

**What this is not.** It is MODELLED, not simulated: nothing integrates a
collision or flows metal. The run-in radius is where the contact stops yielding,
not a wear process advanced in time, and the build still cuts a sharp apex —
the blunting is a service state, so TODO 131's reach guard and landing 1's
static price both stand unchanged.

**Closing the item.** The question it was filed to ask — does the club tooth's
tip carry what the escapement puts on it — is answered in both currencies, the
quasi-static load (×4.96, first yield at ×24.6 the escape torque) and the drop's
impact (it runs in, inside its finish band). The tooth form stands. The
conservatisms that remain are named and pointed the safe way: the three
unmodelled series members, the fork at rest, the frictionless drop, the worst
arm over an unresolved landing window, and the peak swing. Each would make the
answer kinder.
