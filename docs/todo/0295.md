## 295. The cocks' jewel bores are not cut: the balance cock's slab has no bore at all, and both escapement bridges run an unbored arm across the one they do cut

Reported by eye (the owner, 2026-11-08): "the bore holding the stones mesh
around the cock shaft". Two construction defects, one per builder, and in
both the stone is set into metal that was never opened for the pivot that has
to reach it.

**1. `makeCock` cuts no bore and no counterbore** (src/geometry.js). The
slab's outline carries exactly three holes — the spy hole toward the head,
the hairspring stud hole, and the tail spy hole — and then
`makeJewelSetting(...)` is ADDED at `(0, length·jewelAt, depth/2)`. Nothing
is removed where it lands.

Measured at the balance cock's own dimensions (`COCK_W` 6, `COCK_T` 0.8,
`COCK_JEWEL_AT` 0.5, so the setting is r 0.96 with `jewelStone`'s stack):

| | cock-local z |
|---|---|
| slab, bevels included | −0.400 … +0.400 |
| slab core (the extrude) | −0.240 … +0.240 |
| the setting's collar (r 1.104 … 1.536) | −0.196 … +0.340 |
| the ruby (hole r 0.1457) | −0.057 … +0.260 |

So the collar — a solid lathe ring — is buried 0.436 u into the slab with no
counterbore under it, stopping 0.044 u above the core's own underside; the
ruby's lower two thirds stand inside the slab's metal; and on the staff's
axis there is **0.343 u = 0.130 mm of uncut nickel** between the slab's
underside and the stone's bottom face.

That last number is the one that matters, because the pivot is built to run
through it. `BALANCE_SHOULDER_TOP_Z` is derived as
`COCK_MID_Z + cockJewelStone(…).bottom − CLEAR_MARGIN` (src/main.js), so the
balance staff shoulders one margin under the stone and its pivot
(`BALANCE_PIVOT_R`, ⌀ 0.07 mm) is drawn up into solid metal. The upper pivot
of the movement's most friction-loaded bearing runs in nickel, not in its
ruby, and the shock setting on the face above caps a tip nothing bored a path
to. `jewelHoleR` is passed in correctly (TODO 192 step 2 turned the stone for
the pivot that runs in it) — the stone's hole is right and the host's is
absent.

**2. `makeEscapeBridge` bores its boss and then fills the bore with the
arm.** The escape and fork cocks come from the bridge builder, which does the
boss honestly — a `bore`-carrying node is a lathed tube, counterbored from
the top for its chaton, and its comment says why ("a chaton lying on a
surface is not a setting"). But the bar between two nodes is a `BoxGeometry`
of the FULL centre-to-centre length, centred on the midpoint, so its end face
lands exactly on the boss's axis; its half-width is `min(a.r, b.r)·0.8` and
it spans the slab's whole thickness:

| cock | bore r | counterbore r | bar half-width | stone dish outer r |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Escape cock | 0.600 | 1.600 | 1.257 (2.09× the bore) | 1.500 |
| Fork cock | 0.400 | 0.950 | 1.200 (3.00× the bore) | 0.850 |

The arm's metal therefore re-fills the whole foot-side HALF of the bore and
of the counterbore, over the slab's full depth, and the ruby dish sits inside
it over about half its own area. The boss is bored and the arm is not, which
is defect 1 arriving by another route: in real metal the arm is bored WITH
the boss, in one operation, because they are one piece.

**Why nothing has said so.** Each cock is one rigid unit, so every pair here
is a FIXTURE pair inside a unit:

- the pair sweep cannot see inside a unit at all (CLAUDE.md's first blind
  spot);
- `intraUnit`'s FF tier (TODO 5 / §121) measures them but GATES only inside
  `INTRA_TIER_SCOPE`, which is the alarm complex — `Balance cock`,
  `Fork cock` and `Escape cock` are not in it, so their rows sit in the
  reported, untriaged residue;
- `assembly` (§107) asks whether a rigid group is ONE connected body, and
  interpenetration makes that MORE true, not less;
- `plateSeats` (TODO 202 step 4) and `support` reach the cock's legs and
  screws, not its own interior.

places — it is the cut that is missing:

   pivot + `PIVOT_BORE_CLEAR`), the counterbore at the collar's `wallR` down
