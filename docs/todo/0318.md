## 318. The crown turns backward as the watch runs down: a crown-wound movement's crown stands still

Filed 2026-11-18 at the owner's request: "the keyless works currently turns the winding crown as we slowly
unwind the spring. The crown should not move as we unwind if that's not normal in a regular watch. There should be
a todo for that." The fix is already scoped as a feature (roadmap §334, claimed in `docs/item-numbers/BUILT-0334.md`,
nothing built). This item exists so the debt is counted where honesty debt is counted: until §334 lands, the movement
shows something a watch of its kind does not do.

**What is wrong.** On a crown-wound watch the crown does not turn while the watch runs. On this movement it does.
Measured on the default build for §334 (stepping `setPose({ tension })` from 1 to 0 and unwrapping the `windStem`
mesh's world angle): the stem turns **4.2 times backward over the 30 h reserve**, about one turn every seven hours.
It is slow enough to miss by eye and it is still wrong twice over: the crown is the wearer's input and it moves on its
own, and turning the winding train takes going torque (whether the energy column prices that load was not checked
when this was filed; §334 treats the drag as a row still to be written).

**Why it happens.** It is deliberate in `main.js`, not a slip. Every winding wheel is posed from the banked wind
(`windStemRot`, from `barrelWindTurns`), and the `restoring` audit declares the keyless works `two-way`: the crown
drives the winding wheels forward, and the mainspring back-drives the same teeth through the fusee arbor and its spur as
the watch runs down. That back-drive is true of a KEY-wound fusee, whose winding square is on the fusee arbor and turns
with the cone. A CROWN-wound fusee has to break the path, and this one has nothing that does. TODO 50's saw coupling is
a one-way, but the wrong way round for this: the running fusee turns the winding pinion in the sense that bears on the
coupling's DRIVING faces, so it carries the stem instead of slipping past it.

**Fix path: §334's one-way cannot do it (2026-11-18).** §334 proposes a second one-way "placed so that
winding drives through it and running overruns it". No one-way placed anywhere in the winding train can, and the
reason is the same one §334 gives for the saw coupling, applied to every station. The chain pulls the fusee arbor
in the pay-out sense ALWAYS: while winding the crown overpowers it, and while running the arbor turns in that same
sense at the train's rate. So the torque at every mesh of the winding train has the same direction in both
regimes, and only the motion reverses. A one-way that carries winding stops its crown side running ahead of its
fusee side in the winding sense, which is the same constraint as stopping the fusee side running ahead in the
pay-out sense, and that is exactly the running motion. It locks and carries the crown, wherever it sits: at the
stem (TODO 50's saw coupling, as §334 found), at the transfer or crown wheel, or between the spur and the arbor
(§334's candidate (a), which fails the same way). A torque-sensing swinging idler fails too, because the torque
does not change sign.

**What a keyless fusee does instead.** It DISENGAGES: the winding wheels are carried on a rocking bar held in a
neutral position by a spring, out of mesh with the fusee's winding wheel, and turning the crown swings them into
mesh. Search snippets (2026-11-18; the pages themselves were not reachable from the session that wrote this) gave
this from vintagewatchstraps.com's keyless-work page ("with a fusee watch the wheels must be on a rocking bar which
is held in a neutral position by a spring") and an NAWCC thread ("keyless fusees need a disengagement device").
Both should be read in full, or checked against a printed text, before a design is cut to them. The fix is
therefore a REDESIGN of the winding branch, not a part added to it, and the choice among the ways to disengage is
the owner's (roadmap §334 carries it). A spring-centred bar has a force WINDOW in TODO 16's format: the spring
must return the bar against the running load (the winding train's own friction, small) and lose to the winding
load (the mainspring through the fusee, large), and both ends must be measured, not posed.

**Progress.** The owner chose the rocking bar on 2026-11-18. §334 landing 1 (`docs/built/0334.md`) designs it in a
line: `src/keyless-rocking-bar.js`, held by `tools/probe-keyless-rocking-bar.mjs`; landing 2 (2026-11-19) cuts it as
metal there (the bar, its pins, the banking slot, the return blade and the friction clip, the window re-solved with
them). Landings 3a–3c (2026-11-20/21) plan the fold against the built movement and the plan holds
(`tools/probe-keyless-rocking-bar-fold.mjs`, all rows green): corner K's plate bore is cut blind, and the bar carries
11 + 10T wheels at a bend derived against the mainspring barrel, which the line's 18T bar lay over. The crown still
turns in the watch; this item stays open until landing 3d cuts the fold.

**What still stands from §334.** The acceptance below, the instrument, the arrest's stall under the hand, Harrison's
maintaining power unchanged, and the audits it lists (`restoring` splitting the keyless works by member,
`transmits` holding the disengaged mesh, the new cuts in `tools/probe-direction-guards.mjs`, the energy column).

**How to verify.** §334's acceptance: over a full run-down at the default build the stem's measured world angle moves
by under a degree (the click's own lift); a Wind press from any reserve still banks turn-for-turn up to the arrest and
stalls there; the battery is clean at the default. The measurement above becomes a probe whose control leaves the
one-way out and must read the 4.2 turns.

**Until then.** No waiver is needed: no gate measures the crown's rest while running, which is part of the finding.
`explain.html`'s keyless entry should state this as open debt when it is next refreshed.
