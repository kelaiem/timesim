## 272. Form builds: the gong seat sets the pillars, not the other way round — DONE

**What was wrong.** On a form build the pillars were solved first and the gong seat after, so the gong
had to fit round them. The pillar scan never saw the strike group: the gong and the hammer are cut
about 11,000 lines after it, and the hammer's metal about 30,000 lines after. The seat generator
(`tools/gong-seats.mjs`) counted the pillars as obstacles in the gong's band. So a pillar always beat
the gong for room, though the pillars are the freest part on the plate (the balance cock's precedent
in the pillar scan: "The pillar is the freest part on this plate, so it yields"). After TODO 271 that
cost the cushion its one spiral seat: its strike opening kept 1.775 of plate to pillar 1, against
`TQ_LAND_MIN` 1.900. Before TODO 271 it was worse: the cushion's and the tonneau's hammers ran
through pillar 1, unseen, because nothing told the pillar scan where the hammer was.

**What was built.** The gong chooses first, and the pillars yield to it.
- **The seat lookup is hoisted ahead of the pillar scan** (`GONG_SEAT_LOOKUP` in `src/main.js`).
  `GONG_SEAT` solves its strike from the same lookup, so a build reads which row it stands on once.
- **Each seat row declares `keep`**, the strike group's plan footprint as its seated build measured
  it: the convex hull of the hammer's swept metal over the pillars' height, `[PLATE_BACK_FACE,
  TQ_BOT_Z]` with a margin either side, sampled across its whole draw; its opening in the three-quarter
  plate (TODO 266); and the post's radius. This is `ALARM_UNDER_FOOTPRINT`'s idiom: a footprint
  declared before the metal exists, for a consumer that runs first.
- **The pillar scan yields to it.** A pillar keeps `CLEAR_MARGIN` to the hammer's hull, to the wire (read
  off the row's own curve) and to the post at its foot. It keeps `TQ_LAND_MIN` to the opening, as it does
  to a window, because the screw seat is a hole in that plate.
- **The generator no longer counts the pillars** in the gong's band map (`bandParts` excludes `pillars`
  beside the hammer). It measures `keep` off a first seated boot and judges the second boot, in which
  the pillars yield. The hammer does not depend on where the pillars stand, so one measuring boot is
  the fixed point, and the build holds that.
- **Declared against cut** (§115's idiom). Where the strike opening is cut, the build measures the same
  footprint (`STRIKE_KEEP_MEASURED`, published as `__clock.gongSeat.keep`). It warns when a row has no
  `keep`, when the cut metal stands outside the declaration, or when a pillar stands within
  `CLEAR_MARGIN` of the hammer's swept metal. The last of these is the gate that would have read the
  hammer through pillar 1 before TODO 271.

**And a rim land TODO 271 got wrong.** TODO 271 held a pillar's cap to `TQ_LAND_MIN` of the outline. A
pillar is counted metal, though, and the case's fit (§308, `FORM_FOOTPRINT`) holds every counted
vertex `PLATE_RIM_MARGIN` (5) inside the outline. The first run of this item found the cushion
spiral's pillar pushed to the rim, 3.238 inside the outline, and the fit widened the case round it:
its `binding` read `pillars`. The land is now `max(TQ_LAND_MIN, PLATE_RIM_MARGIN)`. On that build the
fit is bound by the dial, at 5.05, and on every build here the pillars stand at least that far in.
