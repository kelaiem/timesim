## 291. The going mainspring is three to five times too thick for its arbor: r/e 3.4 against a floor of 10 — DONE

Filed 2026-11-08, from BACKLOG §339 landing 0 (a twin-barrel going train), which reads the ribbon's packing to compare two
drum couplings. Measured by `tools/probe-twin-barrel-room.mjs` (row (e)) off the spring's own record
(`scene.getObjectByName('spring').userData.mainspring`). The value is the same on all seven builds it booted: the round
default, the validated `form-rect` point, `case=cushion`, `case=tonneau`, `case=fitted`, `alarm=0&case=fitted` and the
lands preview.

**What is wrong.** The going ribbon is a strip **e = 0.440 u (0.167 mm) thick** (`section.a` 0.2201, the half-thickness)
wound on an arbor collar of **r = 1.5 u** (`MS_COLLAR_R`). So its arbor-to-thickness ratio is

| | r | α = r/e |
|---|---|---|
| on the collar (the strip's inner face) | 1.500 | **3.41** |
| at the innermost coil's centreline (`innerR`) | 1.720 | 3.91 |

The watchmaking rule this is measured against is the one that sizes a mainspring's thickness to its arbor, because the
innermost coils' bending round the arbor is what sets or breaks a spring. David Ichim (SJX Watches, "In-Depth:
Balancing Mainspring Dimensions Inside the Barrel", 7 November 2025,
<https://watchesbysjx.com/2025/11/in-depth-balancing-mainspring-dimensions-inside-the-barrel.html>) states it as α = r/e,
with r the innermost coil's radius. Defossez suggests 16, about 14 was the old lower limit, and "the lower limit today
is more in the vicinity of 10". The ribbon stands **three times** past the most permissive of those and nearly five
times past Defossez's. For α 10 on this collar the strip could be at most **0.150 u (0.057 mm)** thick. The same article
puts r at a quarter to a third of the barrel radius. Here r/R is **0.167**, with R the spring's outer radius `outerR`
8.975. The fill, 0.481, is inside the half-area rule the builder already uses, and is not the problem.

**Why every gate is green.** The thickness is SOLVED from the stress (TODO 192 step 3, `makeBarrel`'s `ribbonSolve`):
a = (σ_y/E)·L/θ_f, the half-thickness that puts the full wind on `MAINSPRING_SIGMA_Y_PA`. θ_f is the wind measured from
the DRAWN free coil (setup + sweep, about 10.0 rad), and the free coil is drawn as 8 coils already lying in the barrel
(`sweepFree` 50.27 rad). So the stress row in `equalisation` (TODO 193) holds a bending stress relative to a free shape
that is itself a tight spiral with its inner coil at 1.72 u, and nothing asks whether a strip that thick could take that
shape, or be wound onto a 1.5 u arbor, at all. The collar is not derived either: its own comment (`main.js`, at
`MS_COLLAR_R`) calls the 1.5 "the one dimension here that is inherited rather than derived; deriving it is a
spring-proportion question (arbor core against barrel bore)". This item is that question, measured.

**Fix path.** Make the packing a constraint of the solve, not a report.
- **A band with its source**, `FRICTION`'s and `MAINSPRING_SIGMA_Y_BAND`'s rule: `MAINSPRING_ALPHA_BAND` { low 10
  (today's limit), historical 14, Defossez 16 }, cited to the article above. The gate reads the LOW end, the
  permissive one, so a verdict does not rest on the strictest figure.
- **`ribbonSolve` takes it**: a ≤ r/(2·α), beside the stress law and the half-fill. With the stress law a = κ·L
  (κ = (σ_y/E)/θ_f, and the wind angles are the fusee's inputs, never outputs), the α floor caps the LENGTH as well, so
  the ribbon's volume, and with it the stored energy, falls unless the collar grows.
- **The collar is derived, not inherited**: the r that maximises the ribbon's volume under all three constraints. Rough
  estimates from this record's own κ (8.18e-4 /u) and R (8.975), to be re-derived by the solve, not quoted:
  - at today's r = 1.5, α 10 caps a at 0.075 and L at about 92 u, which is about **12%** of today's energy;
  - at r = R/3 (the article's upper band), a 0.15 and L about 183, about **46%**;
  - with r free, the optimum sits near r ≈ 4.1 (r/R ≈ 0.45, past the article's band) at about **85%**.

  Whole coils make each of these a step.
- **What it moves, re-derived on the existing rules**: `GOING_K` and the energy column; TODO 207's `BAL_RIM_F` re-solved
  to the lighter spring (`equalisation` row 15 holds it both ways, and `AMPLITUDE_TARGET_DEG` 200° is the claim at
  risk); the pivot sizes (`TRAIN_PIVOT_SIZES`) re-read off the lower torque; the ribbon morph re-cut. The fusee, the
  chain and the arrest do not move: the cone is cut against the torque law normalised to the wind angles
  (`springTorqueAt`), not to its size.
- **The alarm ribbon** (`makeBarrel` without `ribbonSolve`, TODO 201's legacy fraction) is measured on the same
  formula, and gets the same constraint or a row stating why not.

**Acceptance.** The going ribbon's α at the collar ≥ `MAINSPRING_ALPHA_BAND.low`, as a row on `equalisation` beside the
stress row. Its control is a ribbon at today's 0.440 u on the 1.5 u collar, which must fail. The collar is a solve
output with its constraint written in the comment (rule 1). The amplitude target and the balance rim are re-solved, not
re-targeted. The default boots silent and the battery is clean.

**Relation to BACKLOG §339** (the twin barrel). In a parallel twin each ribbon runs the full wind in a smaller drum, so
its shorter ribbon is thinner at the same κ and its α rises. In series each runs half the wind, so its strip is
thicker and its α falls. Closing this item first sets the packing law the twin is designed against.

**Done (2026-11-08).** Built on the fix path, with one constraint the path did not foresee.

- **The band.** `MAINSPRING_ALPHA_BAND` (layout.js) { low 10, historical 14, Defossez 16 }, cited to the SJX article,
  with `MAINSPRING_ALPHA_MIN` its low end.
- **The solve.** `makeBarrel`'s `ribbonSolve` takes `alphaMin` and solves the collar. `springArborR` became its floor,
  `MS_COLLAR_MIN`: the arbor's working section (`barrelArborR`, 0.964, which the inherited 1.5 had rounded to 0.9) plus
  the boss's 0.6. The first solve, with only the stress, the half fill and α, chose 5 coils on a 5.23 collar (71% of
  the energy) and broke the wind morph: its 226 u ribbon was too short to take the full wind packed on that collar (the
  frames came out 22% long). So the solve gained a fourth row, the wind. Wound to A_full = 2πn + setup + sweep, the
  ribbon must be at least as long as the tightest frame of `mainspringFrames`' own family. That law now lives in one
  function, `ribbonFrameLength`, which the frames and the solve both read (`RIBBON_K_MAX`, the k-solve's bracket). Each
  coil count takes the largest collar that keeps the fill and the wind, and the count that stores most at α ≥ 10 wins.
  The cost estimates above did not have that row: the answer stores **54.5%** of the old energy, not 85%.
- **The ribbon now**: 5 coils on a **3.497 u** collar, e **0.325 u** (0.123 mm), L 198.5, **α 10.76**, r/R 0.390,
  fill 0.301. The wind binds (`packing.binds`). r/R sits past SJX's ¼–⅓, a range drawn for springs designed for many
  developing turns; this one develops 1.6 (10 rad), where a large core is the optimum. It is reported, not gated. The
  stress row is unchanged: the full wind still lands on 1800 MPa.
- **The gate.** `equalisation` row 19: each ribbon's α re-derived from its section and collar (`packing`, both ribbons'
  records), the limit held to the band's low end, a ribbon under it waived by name in `RIBBON_PACKING_WAIVERS`, and a
  stale waiver failing. The control is the old going ribbon (a 0.22008 on the 1.5 collar), which the rule must refuse.
  The alarm ribbon measures **α 3.12** on its 0.594 collar and is waived against TODO 201, which re-proportions it.
- **What moved, re-derived on the existing rules.** The 18,000 balance rim went 0.645 → **0.36** (201.65° nominal
  hanging; 0.365 reads 199.43° and fails), the 21,600 rim 0.485 → **0.26** (200.98°; 0.265 reads 198.69°), and both
  rows' claim and peak held (126°/311° and 128°/311°). The hairspring followed the lighter balance on TODO 207's height
  law: 0.3726 → **0.2015** (k ×0.54081, read off both builds), keeping §218's 0.0239 mm ribbon. The stack above the
  balance keeps the height it was solved at (`HAIRSPRING_H_STACK`, `SPRING_STACK_TOP_Z`), the `TRAIN_CEILING_Z` rule a
  level up. Lowered with the spring, the plate came down onto the stop-work star over the cone (−0.057 a side, §329).
  The depth the shorter spring could buy is left for a plate-floor re-layout. The two fast rows have no balance this
  spring drives (TODO 292); they are refused, not shipped stale. The explainer's hack-corner plate re-quotes
  `HACK_SCREW_DROP` 0.0315 and `HACK_SCREW_STANDOFF` 0.1467 (the lighter rim's), and the overcoil plate's raise 0.3515,
  which had been stale since TODO 207.
- **Measured** (local, this container): the default boots silent; `equalisation`, `oscillator` and `restoring` pass;
  `probe-power-budget.mjs` AGREES on 62 figures; `explain-quotes.mjs` PASS; `explain-i18n.mjs --check` fails on the same
