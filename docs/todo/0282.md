## 282. meshClearance reads a positive clearance through real crossings when a mesh carries long sliver triangles

Filed 2026-11-03, found while profiling `form-rect`'s point sweep (§331).

**What is wrong.** On `main` (`6658165e`), `?case=rect&formrect=-45.72,45.72,-44.38,44.38`, at `tau 0`:
- An exact edge–triangle count (Möller–Trumbore over every `windStem` triangle edge against every `caseMiddle` triangle) finds **96 crossings**: the winding stem passes through the form band, which was not bored for its tubes.
- `meshClearance(windStem, caseMiddle)` returns **2.289 u**, a positive clearance.
- So `undeclaredClearance` read `Case ⇄ Keyless works` clean, and `form-rect` joined `src/validated-configs.js` on that reading.

The only property that distinguishes the case is its mesh. The case middle carried 420 triangles with an edge over 20 u, up to 90.5 u (the case's width). Once §331 cut those faces into short triangles, the same call read 0, and with the band bored, 2.507 u. The round case, which has no such slivers, reads the same pair 2.507 u with 0 crossings. So the long triangles are what blinds the query. Which step fails is not isolated: the BVH closest-point query, `sampledVerdict`'s parity raycast, or the `rigidFrame` path.

**Why it matters.** `meshClearance` is the exact tier of `inspection`, `clearances`, `undeclaredClearance`, `expectedContacts` and the point sweeps. A false positive clearance is the dangerous direction: a gate that reads clean through metal. Every other mesh with long triangles is suspect until measured. Candidates are any `ExtrudeGeometry` whose earcut caps join distant points of a long straight run, and any plate or slab with long straight edges.

**Fix path.**
1. **Reproduce in isolation.** Two meshes: a long thin box cut as two triangles per face, and a cylinder through it. Read `meshClearance` against the exact crossing count, then find which step returns the positive number. A control must read 0 at the same geometry triangulated finely.
2. **Fix the query, not the meshes.** The instrument must be right for any closed mesh; a mesh-side cure only moves the trap.
3. **Sweep the movement for other hidden contacts.** Run the exact crossing count over every unit pair the battery declares clear, on the default and every swept point, and list each pair where `meshClearance` reads positive through a crossing. That list is this item's real size. Each pair it finds is a defect in the metal, filed on its own.
4. **Gate the agreement.** Hold `meshClearance` and the exact count to agree on a fixture set that includes slivers, so the instrument cannot regress silently.

**Root cause, measured (2026-11-04).** It is not the BVH query: on the pre-§331 tree, in one world frame, the vendored
library's `closestPointToGeometry` reads the stem and the middle **0** both ways, `intersectsGeometry` reads **true**, and a
brute-force minimum over every triangle pair (`ExtendedTriangle.distanceToTriangle`) is 0 with 48 pairs intersecting. The
false clearance came from `_meshClearanceInner`'s arbitration of a near-zero, on the branch "the library read 0 and no
sample is contained → publish the sampled distance". Two conditions met there together:
1. **No point sample stood in the other body's metal.** The band's 90 u slivers left no band vertex or edge midpoint near
   the stem, and the stem passes THROUGH a wall thinner than its own sample spacing (TODO 95's case exactly).
2. **The pass-through witness was switched off.** `boundsASolid` demanded every position-keyed edge on exactly two
   faces, and the middle's 118 bad edges were all OVER-shared (count 4): the bezel sweep and the band share the seat
   plane, a face doubled inside the metal on purpose (§289). So the body was called open and the witness never ran.

The slivers made condition 1 likely; condition 2 is the instrument's defect, and it is what this item fixes. Across the
default and `form-rect`, the counts split cleanly: six meshes carry only EVEN over-shared edges (`caseMiddle`, `caseBack`,
`chainRun` with 1,276, two `star`s, two unnamed bodies), and nineteen carry ODD ones (real boundaries: the springs, the
hands' tips, the sub-dials' faces and walls, `fuseeCone` with 468, a plane and a shape geometry).

**Fixed (step 2), on `fix/todo-282-meshclearance`.** `surfaceEdgeCensus` gains `odd`, the edges on an odd number of faces
(the boundary mod 2), and `boundsASolid` reads `odd === 0`. The witness's argument needs only a surface with no
boundary: a doubled internal face can be reached only from inside the metal, so n ≥ 2 crossings still means inside.
`bad` and `over` are unchanged, so `meshIntegrity`'s held-fixture manifoldness gate is unchanged.
`tools/probe-282-witness.mjs` holds it against the rule as it was, rewritten in flight:
- a rod through a slab cut as two boxes sharing a face reads 0.5 under the old rule and 0 under the new;
- a rod through an open sheet is still refused (1.0 both);
- the census is monotone, and newly closes exactly the six meshes above.

`probe-165-witness` (its control now also restores the old line) passes.

**Open.**
- **Step 3**, the sweep for other hidden contacts. The witness now runs for those six meshes in every pair sweep, so the
  battery itself is that sweep for them. Any pair it newly reports is a defect in the metal, filed on its own.
- **Step 4** stays: hold `meshClearance` against an exact count on a fixture set in a workflow. The probe is acceptance
  but runs in none.
- Separately, `fuseeCone` is an OPEN mesh (468 boundary edges), and an open mesh reads as a colliding one to the parity
  raycast (CLAUDE.md's TODO 27 trap): its pairs deserve the same measurement.
