## §315 — LEGO going train, landing 1: the fusee's great wheel to the knife-edge balance, one framed train

Owner's item 1: the LEGO modules stood side by side — the fusee (landing H, §282, §285,
§286) with its centre pinion, the four `TECHNIC_FORK` rows, the recoil anchor and the
knife-edge balance (§287) — and nothing turned one from another. This landing joins them
into ONE kinematic train per generation: one framed LDraw build on one baseplate, its tooth
counts read off the rows, every mesh on the lattice and phased off the parts' measured
tooth phases, its step order cleared by §302's swept insertion, and every angle on the page
carried from one state through the teeth (standing rule 2). It is `src/technic-train.js`,
the ninth cell of `technic.html` (a carousel stop, a parts list, a step order, a booklet,
an `.ldr` and an `.mpd`), held by `tools/probe-technic-train.mjs`.

What it is NOT: the escapement is not driven. The balance's swing is POSED (§287's
cycle), the escape wheel's angle is read off that swing, and the train follows the escape
wheel back through the teeth. Landings 2 and 3 (below) are where the swing gets driven.

### The chain (`trainChain`, read, never typed)

The meshes are `FUSEE_CENTRE`'s (the great wheel's 40T on the centre pinion's 8T) and the
two `TECHNIC_FORK` rows: centre → fourth as declared, and fourth → escape with its two
meshes the OTHER WAY ROUND. The row declares (24:8)(40:16), and no 16T fits on the escape
arbor between the anchor's pallets and the knives (the lattice survey of §287 already
found no 16T escapes); turned round, (40:16)(24:8), the 8T driven gear IS the escape
wheel, driven from straight below by a 24T on a new DRIVE arbor (a 24:8 mesh is 16 mm,
five plates, so the drive sits a whole number of plates under the escape arbor). The
product is unchanged.

2250 = the centre pinion's 5 × the centre→fourth row's 60 × the escape row's 15/2: the
steel train's three targets. At the row's 1 s and the 8T escape wheel the fourth turns once
a minute, the centre once an hour and the great wheel once in five hours. The ratio the
probe gates is the product of the PLACED parts' tooth counts read off the build's mesh
list, not the chain's.

### The going time (POSED spring law)

A wind turns the great wheel `coneTurnTotal(run)/2π` = 1.3217 turns (§286's cone run, two
steps), so the escape wheel turns 2973.8 times, one tooth a period: **6.61 h a wind at
1 s** (6.62 h at the balance's solved period, which the page reads off the parts). That
number rests on `TECHNIC_FUSEE_SPRING`, the steel ribbon's stand-in law: a LEGO band's run
of the barrel is unmeasured, and the page and the parts list say so.

### The frame and the solve (`solveTrain`, `frameTrain`)

The train stands on the fusee's own frame and lattice (§219 tier three (b)): each arbor
one or two Technic columns, every mesh at its catalogue centre distance on the 4 mm grid,
whole plates where it climbs. The solve walks every placement: per arbor a lattice move,
a layer step for its driver (±8, ±16, ±24 mm), a column choice (`ends`, `front`, `back`,
`mid+front`, `mid+back`); the centre arbor's new driver in front of or behind its
columns; then the drive across from the fourth at the escape row's 28 mm, its driven gear
in any layer between the front knife and the wheel, the escapement either way round
(`o`), its drive threaded from in front or from behind. Laws it holds:

- **Two clearance laws.** Within the train a turning part may stand flush on a
  neighbouring arbor's column (the rows' own law, reported as contacts), never gear on
  gear; anything against the modules (fusee, escapement) keeps `TRAIN_CLEAR` = 0.4 mm in
  3D. An axle end may stand flush on a frame block; an arbor's own line's column is its
  bearing, not an obstacle.
- **One arbor to an axle line.** A train arbor coaxial with another body could only be
  threaded through that body's axle; the swept insertion found the classic's second arbor
  trapped on the centre's line that way, so taken lines are refused.
- **The drive's way in is clear.** The drive's knife-side end is under the knife's stack,
  so the axle cannot be lowered between the knives (the gap is shorter than it); it is
  threaded through a 1×2 Technic brick (3700) in a knife's stack, and the whole line beyond
  that brick must be clear of every body standing when it goes in (the escape wheel and
  the balance go on after it). In front (2026) the bearings are the front knife's 3700 and
  the front escape column; from behind (classic) the back knife's 3700 and both escape
  columns, a 7L axle. The classic's line in front runs into its fusee — barrel, cord hook,
  ratchet and great wheel — which the swept insertion found first; the solve then found
  NO classic train with a clear front line (every one reaches its fourth at x 36, 52 or
  68 mm, over the fusee), and every one with a clear line behind.
- **The knife line on a stud.** Every catalogue centre distance keeps x ≡ 0 (mod 8) from
  the centre arbor, so the escape arbor lands on the stud grid; a 1×2 knife stack centred
  there stands on a 1×2 jumper (3794b), the frame's own half-stud part
  (`frameEscapement`'s `x0`). The escapement is otherwise §287's, raised by whole plates
  (`zE`), so its period and cycle are unchanged — the probe holds both.
- **One baseplate.** 16 × 16 (3867), 32 × 32 (3811) or 48 × 48 (4186, vendored by this
  landing): the classic's 12 mm meshes climb a layer a stage and cannot fold back past the
  fusee's back columns, so it needs 34 studs along its axles.

Ranked: baseplate, face contacts, parts, footprint, path, escape-arbor height, §287's sense,
front before behind. **The declared train (`TECHNIC_TRAIN`) is the first ranked train whose
derived step order the swept insertion clears**, and the probe re-derives exactly that:
2026's #1 is flagged (its fourth's 16T meets a 40T on its way in), its #2
(the fourth's columns at both ends) clears; the classic's #1 clears. The insertion judge
needs the parts' solids and minutes a candidate, so it is the probe's, not the walk's.

### The step order (§302) learned three things

The train's recipe (`going train/<gen>` in `RECIPES`, `walk: false`) is the fusee cell's
three chapters, the centre pinion's own chapter, the rest of the train, then the
escapement's two. Building it found three ordering rules, each now in
`src/technic-steps.js`, and none moves any other cell's plan (every other cell's `.mpd`
hashes identical to `main`'s, both generations, every mode):

- the centre pinion is its own chapter, as the fusee cell has it: it slides on from its
  one open end past the maintaining ratchet, so it goes in before the line, while the rest
  of the train goes in after the line, whose great axle it would otherwise have to pass;
- a gear's MESH PARTNER is not in its corridor (their boxes overlap at rest because the
  teeth interleave; whether it slides into the mesh is the turned insertion's question);
- among the chapters ready to go in, the ones the earliest outstanding chapter is OWED go
  first, so the recipe's order bends only where an edge bends it.

### One state (`trainModel`, `trainPose`)

The page's state is the time t. §287's `cycleModel` gives the balance's posed swing and the
escape wheel's angle at t, recoil included; the great wheel's going turn is that angle
carried back through the teeth, dG = (θ − θ₀)/2250 (θ₀ puts the wheel's least angle over
the first period at the great wheel's zero, so dG ≥ 0); every arbor is its speed × dG off
its measured phase; and §282's fusee loop is put at dG, so the cord, the barrel, the
ratchet and both pawls stand where the great wheel's turn puts them, past a run-down
included. The probe poses the train at ten times and holds every mesh phased, the great
wheel read two ways (the train's speed and the loop's), the escape wheel one tooth on per
period and the great wheel 2π/(2250 × 8) on per period; its control poses one arbor by
itself, a third of a pitch off its law, and watches the phase check fire.

### Landings 2 and 3 (scope only)

**Landing 2 — the escapement DRIVEN (≈ two to three sessions).** Replace the posed swing
with a driven one: the train's torque at the escape wheel (the POSED spring law through
2250:1 and the meshes' tabled η, never a single coefficient) delivers impulse through the
recoil anchor's 8T pallets, and the knife-edge balance answers with its measured I and d.
Deliverables: a RATING NUT — a mass on a threaded axle whose travel changes d — with its
rate sensitivity DERIVED (dT/dd off the compound-pendulum law from `partSolid` inertias),
and the period against amplitude measured off the driven cycle, recoil included, so the
circular error is a number, not a claim. Honesty: still a MODELLED drive until landing 3
integrates it in time.

**Landing 3 — integrated dynamics (≈ three to four sessions).** The whole train as one
equation of motion stepped in time (the movement's `balance-drive.js` is the precedent):
the band's torque through the cone, the train's inertia reflected through the teeth, the
anchor's impulse and recoil, the knife's friction band; going time per wind and the rate
across the run-down then come out of the integration rather than the cone's arithmetic.
Gated against landing 2's driven cycle at its operating amplitude. Not in either: a Swiss
lever, a hairspring, wrist scale — the analogue stays an analogue.
