## §302 — LEGO build instructions, landing 2: a rule-derived step order, a swept-insertion validator and an .mpd with steps

Second landing of the private roadmap's §297 scoping (landing 1 is §300's parts
list). Every LEGO cell on `technic.html` now has a **step order** and a
**`download .mpd (build steps)`** link beside its `.ldr`. Not here: a viewer
(landing 3), a print booklet, LeoCAD/LPub3D in CI. Display and file only: no
placement, `.ldr` line, colour or build probe moves, and `index.html`'s module
graph reaches `layout.js` and nothing of `src/technic-*.js`, so the battery
cannot see this landing.

**MODELLED, not built.** The order is derived from the placements and judged
against the part geometry; nobody has assembled any of it. The bands' and the
spring's forces stay POSED and each is stated on a step of its own that adds no
part ("a rubber band goes here; count, size and length are not in the model").
The escapement keeps its analogue caption on its step; the alarm's gong is "not
in the model".

### The order is derived (`src/technic-steps.js`, pure)

No per-placement list exists. The rules, each a constraint read off the
placements: the baseplate, then the columns bottom-up one layer across all
columns (a part rests on the one under it); a part on an axle that cannot be
slid in from either end because a frame part stands in its whole footprint is
**held in the gap** and lowered first, then the axle is **threaded** through
brick, parts, brick, then what has a free end **slides** on, nearest the bricks
first; two parts on one line whose extents overlap (the keyless ring over its
joiner) and an axle no brick holds (the fusee's maintaining axle on its two
joiners) are **sub-assemblies**, as is any body with no brick on its axle (a
pawl on its arm, the balance on its knife edge); a part that travels along its
shaft between modes (the ring) goes in last, at the neutral place, and is slid
to the mode's; shafts follow the build's mesh list (a shaft after the one that
drives it); chapters are then put in an order in which no part is added behind
one standing in its only clear corridor. The one hand-written table is
`RECIPES`: chapter names and order for the fusee, alarm and escapement (no mesh
list) and the keyless selector, by shaft key. A toothed part next to a meshed
neighbour carries the solved shaft angle φ as a "turn to mesh" note.

### The validator (`tools/technic-steps-sweep.mjs`, three-mesh-bvh)

Each part is moved along its declared directions (down for a stud-seated part,
along the axle for an axle, then its own axes and the six axis directions) from
outside the model into the parts already placed, sampled every 0.5 LDU. A
boolean "do triangles cross" is useless on LDraw parts, which are made to
touch, so the measure is a DEPTH per crossing triangle pair: one triangle is
clipped to the prism over the other and the lesser side of what is left through
the other's plane is read (a clip with no area is an edge touch and reads 0).
A pair may reach the depth it **ends** in (its fit, measured at the placed pose)
plus the tolerance; a friction pin or bush may reach its deepest measured fit
anywhere, but only along its own axes. Failing that, a meshed gear is tried with
the chain turned through a tooth (24 steps), and any part in two legs (lowered,
then slid 8, 16 or 32 LDU, or by the travel the model itself declares).

**The tolerance is derived: 0.4 LDU (0.16 mm)** = twice the sagitta of the
library's 16-gon at the largest circle two surfaces share here (10 LDU): two
tessellations of one tangent circle can disagree by that. The probe measures the
exact-fit floor (an axle in a brick or a bore, 0.199 LDU) and requires the
tolerance above it; a brick pushed 10 LDU into its neighbour reads 4.0 and one
set beside it reads 0. **Honest history:** the first runs used 0.25, read off
that floor, and left a string of rows over by 0.02 to 0.2 LDU (a pulley boss,
a pair of axles, a gear flank). The tessellation derivation replaced it for
that reason, and the rows it cleared are the margin cases, not collisions;
the deep ones (0.5 to 1.2 LDU over) are untouched and listed below.

### The file

`toMpd` writes a main file with `0 STEP` after each step and a `0 FILE` per
sub-assembly, placed at the identity (sub-assembly parts keep their world
matrices, so flattening is exact). The probe parses it back with `src/ldraw.js`
and requires its flattened part lines to equal `toLdr`'s, as text.

### Instruments

`tools/probe-technic-steps.mjs` (pure Node, about five minutes): coverage and
byte replay (controls: a part dropped, a part twice), topology against a support
graph read off the pack's bounds and hole faces (controls: a Technic brick put
ahead of the plate under it, an axle threaded before its gear), insertion
(control: the same swap, where the gear can no longer be lowered), the file
(controls: two steps swapped, a matrix changed, a sub-assembly's file removed),
the words (no "works", "tested" or "simulated"), and both inventories judged
both ways (a new row fails, a cleared row is stale). `tools/smoke-technic-steps.mjs`
(Chromium) clicks every link and holds each download to the pure module's text:
32 checks, 0 failing under SwiftShader. The Chrome strings are English in a page
that is not localized, so `probe-chrome-coverage` is untouched.
