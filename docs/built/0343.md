## §343 — The chronometer's beat: a 14,400 A/h rate carrying the largest balance the case admits — PARTIAL: landing 1 (the rate and its balance) shipped; landing 2 (making it the shipped watch) open

Asked 2026-11-12 by the owner: a larger balance, the way old chronometers carry one, at whatever beat allows the
largest. The question is BACKLOG §76's ("the balance cannot grow"), and the answer moved since §76 last measured it:
TODO 291 cut the going spring's stored energy to 54.5% of the old ribbon's, and TODO 207 / 242 had already lightened
the balance to what that spring sustains. So the binding question was no longer only the layout.

### What sets a balance's size, per rate

The spring and the fusee deliver one POWER at every rate: the train is the same up to the fourth wheel, which turns
once a minute at every `RATE_TABLE` row, so the escape torque rises exactly as the escape mesh's ratio falls. The
balance spends (πk/2Q)θ² per cycle on everything but its pivots, with k = I·ω², and 2θ·μ·m·g·r on its pivots. At a
fixed power the inertia a rate can carry therefore goes roughly as 1/f³ (18,000/14,400 cubed is 1.95), and a larger
radius at the same inertia is a lighter rim, so its pivot loss falls too.

Measured with the build's own law (`atBalance` in main.js's amplitude block: the swing at the nominal corner, hanging,
and the self-start), the heaviest grid rim that reaches 200° and starts, per radius:

| rate | largest wheel the spring drives | what stops it first |
|---|---|---|
| 18,000 | r 11 on rim 0.245 (r 11.25 at the lightest rim, 0.235: 199.5°) | the spring |
| 14,400 | r 13.5 on rim 0.25 (r 14: 198.1°) | the CASE, at r 11.75 |
| 10,800 | r 18 and more | the hairspring: 0.0170 mm at r 9, under real stock's 0.02 |
| 7,200 | r 18 and more | the hairspring: 0.0130 mm at r 9 |

The case is the wall at 14,400, and it is a layout wall, not an energy one. The balance stands clear of the train in
plan (`solveLayout`'s swept keep-outs), so its station moves outward as it grows and the plate's rim moves with it:
plateR 41.52 at r 9, 44.03 at r 11.5, 44.62 at r 11.75, where the case body reaches 40.22 mm against the 40 mm owner
cap. A search over every escape and balance step angle (2° × 3° grid, the layout's own solve, warn-free answers only)
found no station that lets a larger wheel stand nearer the centre: at r 11 and up, the shipped station is already the
nearest the keep-outs allow. At r 9 a station 1.6 u nearer exists (escape step −50°), which is not this question.

### Landing 1 — the 14,400 row

`RATE_TABLE[14400]`: 8/64 (fourth = 1 rev/min at 1800/14,400), cut at module 0.2625 = 0.21 · 80/64 so the 64-tooth
wheel keeps the 80-tooth wheel's pitch radius (8.4) and the escape arbor stands where it stood (§204's rule for the
36,000 row). At the mesh's own 0.21 the wheel shrinks to r 6.72, and measured, the reset rod's elbow fouls the low
corridor by 0.87 and the maintaining spring's tip leaves its contact. The balance is r 11.5 on rim 0.345 (0.35
sustains 197.84°), 2 Hz, its hairspring solved to 0.0282 mm, and **2.08× the inertia** of the shipped 18,000 balance
(3.49e-10 against 1.68e-10 kg·m², each read off its own oscillator record). `claimDeg` 126 and `peakDeg` 328 are the
solve's (the peak is the knock at 327.06°, since the favourable dial-flat corner would carry the swing to 445.86°).

A row larger than `BALANCE_R_REF` used to fail `equalisation`'s radius rule ("never larger than the shipped wheel").
That rule was written for faster rows, whose balance can only shrink. The new row carries `caseBound: true`
(`BALANCE_R_CASE_BOUND`): the case, not the spring, sets its radius. The gate still holds it to the grid, and
`tools/probe-343-chronometer.mjs` holds the cap from both sides on booted builds:

```
declared  r 11.5 rim 0.345: plateR 44.03, 200.09° nominal vertical, 2 Hz, ribbon 0.0282 mm, I 3.486e-10
larger    r 11.75 rim 0.33: plateR 44.62, 201.49°, case: body Ø40.22 mm breaks the 40 mm owner cap
shipped   r 9 at 18,000: I 1.680e-10 — the 14,400 wheel is 2.08× its inertia
PASS  the declared balance fits the 40 mm case
PASS  the declared rim is the heaviest that reaches the target and starts
PASS  the hairspring beats 2 Hz inside real stock
PASS  control: the patch took (the larger build is r 11.75)
PASS  one BAL_R_STEP larger breaks the 40 mm case (the wall)
PASS  one step larger still reaches the target and starts (the spring is not the wall)
```

The probe boots the larger wheel through `tools/native/source-patch.mjs`, a native-host load hook that replaces one
string in one module's source for one process, and throws unless the string occurs exactly once. A patch that matched
nothing would boot the declared build and read as the larger one; the third claim is that control.

The row is offered in the rate menu (`SPEC_RATES`) and is a CI spec point (`vph=14400`, `expect: 'any'`), as 21,600
is. The shipped build is unchanged: identity fingerprint 2229004767, boot silent. Like 21,600 (six), the row does not
yet CLOSE: `?vph=14400` boots with eight structural warnings, all of them consequences of the larger plate and the
higher peak swing, and the panel says so. They are landing 2's list.

### Landing 2 — making it the shipped watch (open)

The owner chose 14,400 / r 11.5 as the shipped movement. That is `DEFAULT_VPH = 14400`, and it needs, measured on a
default-14,400 tree:

1. **The stop work**: the crank sweeps its own bracket (−0.160 against 0.15). The hack brake is priced on the
   hairspring's torque at `AMPLITUDE_PEAK_DEG`, which rose from 311° to 328°. The §184 alarm tier-split triple's
   joint bound (click against the anchor ring, −1.438) arrives with the same peak and is unexplained yet.
2. **`DRUM_R_ACTUAL`** (TODO 207's rule, the plate's bound rounded down): the plate grew, so the drum may reach
   12.06. That is MORE spring energy, so it re-proportions the ribbon (TODO 291), re-cuts the fusee and re-solves
   every row's rim. Re-measure the balance's wall after it: the case still caps the radius, but the rim gets heavier.
3. **D4** (§125's closed form), **the gong's arc** (aesthetics `gong.arcDeg` to the C7 derivation, `tools/gong-bench-data.mjs`),
   **§187's glass step** against the grown aperture.
4. **Every generated, config-keyed table**: the identity key becomes `vph:14400`, so `ALARM_PINB_SEAT_MN`
   (TODO 206's warning), the linkage lines, `strike-keep-round.js`, `form-fits.js`, `free-outlines.js`,
   `gong-seats.js`, `validated-configs.js` and the booklet flags are each re-run by their own generator, and every
   form build re-checked against its cap.
5. **The records**: `explain.html`, `primer.html` and the docs quote 18,000 A/h and 2.5 Hz as the shipped beat.

Beyond r 11.5, the balance has to stop standing clear of the train in plan: a chronometer's balance overlaps the
train in its own stratum, under the cock. That is a layout question (P3), filed in the roadmap with §76.
