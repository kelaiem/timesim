## §347 — The round plate's station term is the measured metal

**The ask** (the owner, out of a case-volume study): the round plate was sized by a term nobody had derived. `solveKeyless`
took the larger of the keyless works' floors and, for each going-train station, the station's distance plus its OUTLINE
plus `PLATE_RIM_MARGIN`'s 5 u. The outline was a pitch radius for each wheel, `balanceR × 1.35` "+ cock" for the balance
and a bare 4 for the fork, and the 5 u was "the literal plateR carried as `+= 5` since §13". Standing rule 1 says a number
that looked right is a bug in waiting, and this one sat inside the term that sets the case.

### The law (`layout.js` solveKeyless, `main.js` KEYLESS_INPUTS)

- **A station's plate term is its own metal**: the arbor's distance from the centre, plus the swept radius `main.js`
  measures off the built part (vertex max, so screws and bevel corners count, the same measurement `LAYOUT_INPUTS.swept`
  hands the balance solve), plus `CLEAR_MARGIN`. The fork is measured before `forkGroup` places it, so its radius is about
  its own pivot. The barrel station's widest metal is the great wheel.
- **Nothing else needed the old allowance to carry it.** The balance cock's seat search holds its slab and leg pads inside
  the plate and warns when no bearing does; the escape cock's foot scan the same; the drum is held one margin inside plateR
  (TODO 207); the pillars carry their own rim land (`PILLAR_RIM_LAND`, still `PLATE_RIM_MARGIN`); the stem bushing is one of
  the keyless floors. `PLATE_RIM_MARGIN` keeps every one of those duties, and the form rectangle's.
- **The round case only**, on §325 landing 10's precedent. A form case's plate is its fitted outline, and plateR there is
  the working circle its generated outlines, gong seats and fits were cut against (`src/free-outlines.js`,
  `src/gong-seats.js`). Measured with the law on every kind, the free case lost its balance cock's seat and the fitted
  case's metal stood 4.916 u inside its outline against the 5 it needs. So `reach` is passed only when `SPEC.caseShape` is
  null, and `solveKeyless` keeps the old term when it is absent (which also keeps `tools/form-layout.mjs`'s older dumps
  readable).

### What the smaller plate exposed, fixed here

1. **The escape cock's foot was judged by its node, the reveal by its pad.** On the round plate the foot scan held the
   node inside the cut's rim (`rimR − nodeR`) while the form branch beside it held the pad, the widest metal the foot puts
   on the plate. The pad's reveal then stood past the rim and the three-quarter plate's cut clamped at bearings 283–286.
   The round term now reads the pad.
2. **The balance cock's seat ignored the reveal's rim.** Its slab and pads were held `CLEAR_MARGIN` inside plateR, but the
   reveal opens the plate to the cock's silhouette plus `TQ_CUT_MARGIN` and clamps that edge at plateR − 0.2, so an
   accepted seat could force a clamped reveal (84 bearings over the spec points). The plate term is now that rim, with the
   acceptance margin carried back so "accepted" means exactly "the reveal fits".
3. **§186's case-sector tripwire read the keyless levers unposed (TODO 311).** It walks the case annulus before boot's first
   `tick(0)`, and the setting lever and yoke take their angle only from `tick()`, so it judged them at rotation 0. Once the
   bore came in to 44.16 it fired on the designed train. The two levers now stand at each end of the crown's pull for the
   walk (`settingLeverAngleAt(0|1)`, `yokeAngleAt(YOKE_A_SEAT|YOKE_A_FULL)`) and go back to what the build gave them. The
   other members only `tick()` poses are TODO 311's remaining part.
