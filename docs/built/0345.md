## §345 — A printed Graham dead-beat for the LEGO going train — PARTIAL: landing 0 (the calibration prints) shipped

§323 refused a dead-beat built from LEGO parts. Its limits were the catalogue's:
- the lattice puts the nearest arc centre on the crossbar, 2.4 mm off the pivot;
- a keyed part turns only by quarter turns;
- no catalogue arcs pair at the radius the 8T needs.

The owner asked whether the clock could be printed in PLA, then chose the hybrid: "Scope the hybrid printed
escapement first, weight drive later." The scope is in the private roadmap's §345.

A printed pallet keys on the crossbar through a printed cross hole, but its lock arcs can be centred on the
knife's APEX itself. That gives a true dead-beat on the knife that already ships, with every other part still LEGO.
The shipped `drawnGraham` escapes there through §323's own model and judge. Of the straddles tried, only the
widest escapes at the 2–4° swings the LEGO drive reaches:

| locks apart | arc radius about the pivot | widest pallet that escapes | swings it escapes at |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 pitches | 3.33 mm | 3 mm | 6–10° |
| 2.5 pitches | 7.45 mm | 4 mm | 4–10° |
| 3.5 pitches | 25.04 mm | 8 mm (every width tried) | 2° (2–4° at 1 mm) |

Nothing printed may be quoted until the printer has been measured, so **landing 0 is the calibration prints**:
`src/technic-printed.js` builds them, `tools/printed-stl.mjs --out DIR` writes them, and
`tools/probe-technic-printed.mjs` holds them.

### The printer

As the owner gave it (2026-11-14): a 0.4 mm nozzle and glossy PLA, with white, black, orange and grey on hand
(`PRINT_SETUP`).

**The printed parts are WHITE.** The pallet sits on the build's black crossbar beside its orange arms, and it locks
against the light-grey 8T. The grey filament would read as that wheel.

The friction and the fit are properties of the filament, so every reading is taken on the white spool the parts
will be printed from.

`WALL_MIN`, the thinnest wall anything printed may carry, is two nozzle widths: 0.8 mm.

### The three pieces

**The fit strip** (`coupon-fit.stl`, 64 × 8 × 8 mm). Eight cross holes on the stud pitch.
- **The section.** Each hole is the LEGO axle's section as the vendored 3705 draws it: arms 1.6 mm wide, ends on
  r 2.4 mm. The section is read off the part (`axleSection`) and held to the frame table's `axleR`.
- **The steps.** Each hole is grown by one more clearance step, 0–0.35 mm by 0.05 (`CLEAR_STEPS`). Holes print up
  to about half a line width small, and a moulded arm may be wider than LDraw's 1.6 mm, so the strip brackets both
  with a step to spare.
  - If the owner's hole is the last one, the bracket was too narrow. The strip is then reprinted further out,
    never extrapolated.
- **The notch.** A V notch marks the tightest hole. Its depth is whatever the cell leaves with `WALL_MIN` to the
  hole: 0.8 mm.
- **The thickness** is one layer of the build (8 mm): the gear face the pallet will be as thick as.

**The ring** (`coupon-ring.stl`).
- **The bore** is ⌀50.07 mm: the 3.5-pitch layout's lock radius, read through `drawnGraham` rather than typed.
- **The wall** is 8 mm, the most material behind a face that layout was seen to escape with, so it is ⌀66.07 mm
  outside.
- **The facets:** 64 of them, a sagitta of 0.04 mm, under a tenth of the nozzle.
- **What it reads.** Calipers inside and out read the print's error on a concave face and on a convex one, at the
  radius the pallet will carry. The scale reads its density: 14.45 g at PLA's nominal 1.24 g/cm³.

**The ramp** (`coupon-ramp.stl`, 100 × 30 × 4 mm).
- **Orientation.** It is exported standing on its long edge, so its big faces print as perimeters, the texture of a
  lock face.
- **The sled** rides on it and is made of the build's own parts (`SLED_PARTS`): two 6632 thin liftarms, two 3L
  axles (4519) keyed through their end cross holes, and an 8T on each axle beside each liftarm. The gears cannot
  turn, so the sled SLIDES along the layer lines on its tooth tips, as a tooth slides along a pallet.
- **The reading.** The angle at which the sled starts to slide gives μ = tan θ for a LEGO tooth on printed PLA:
  the lock's rest friction.
- **These dimensions are a jig's**, chosen for handling and not derived: four sled lengths of run, and thick enough
  not to flex standing up.

The scope's draft had a 16 mm arc and a row of round holes. Both were replaced in this landing:
- the ring reads the radius the leading layout actually needs;
- no printed part turns on a pin, so round holes measure nothing it uses.

### What the probe holds

- **The section:** read off 3705, its radius the frame table's.
- **The holes:** every one is its step, span 2(r + c) and slot 2(halfArm + c) off the outline.
  - Linearity control: two steps apart, the slot moves by exactly two steps.
- **The walls:** every wall at least `WALL_MIN`. Between holes it is 2.50 mm, hole to edge 1.25 mm, and notch to
  the tightest hole 0.80 mm.
  - Control: a notch 1.2 mm deep fails.
- **The meshes:** every coupon closed and manifold (each directed edge once, its reverse once), every cap triangle
  wound the right way, and the volume equal to the outline's area × height at float noise.
  - Controls: a triangle dropped, a triangle flipped, a cap reversed.
- **The files:** every one round-trips through binary STL.

The winding check earned its place on the first run. The cells were first stitched as a fan by angle, which cut
across the cross's re-entrant corners. The mesh still read closed, and its volume matched to within the notch.

They are now ear-clipped, each with a BRIDGE from the hole's +x arm to a midpoint every cell carries on both
vertical edges, so neighbouring cells meet vertex for vertex.

### The readings

`PRINT_MEASURED` is empty (`AWAITING`), and nothing printed is quoted until it is filled:
- **The fit:** the first hole, counted from the notch, that takes a 3705 with no play.
- **The ring:** the bore and outside diameters, and its mass.
- **The slide:** three runs each, the angle the sled starts at and the angle at which it keeps going once nudged.

Each row arrives with its date and printer.

### What stays posed or unmodelled

- Every printed dimension and the PLA-on-LEGO friction, until the readings arrive.
- The knife's loss, until its free-decay test. Every swing in §345 is an upper bound.
- PLA's creep and wear. Neither is modelled.

The remaining landings (1: the printed Graham solved and driven on the 8T; 2: the part, listed, drawn and built;
3: a printed escape wheel, only if the margin asks) are scoped in the roadmap.
