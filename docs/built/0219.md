## §219 tier one — the technic fork as data: the catalogue, the solver and four exact rows in `layout.js`

**PARTIAL.** Tier one has landed. Tier two (`?catalogue=technic`, the trains
cut to the rows) and tier three (a printed bill of materials) have not. The
plan, including the scoping note it came from, lives in the private roadmap.

### What it is

The movement's going, motion and reserve trains re-toothed to a public parts
catalogue: LEGO Technic, module 1 on a 4 mm half-stud lattice. Nothing in
`TRAIN` survives verbatim (120:7, 75:10, 80:10 and 80:8 name counts the
catalogue does not have). What survives is the INVARIANT each ratio serves, and
every one of the four re-derives as an EXACT rational product of catalogue
meshes. That is a fork in §70's sense: the constraint that forced each changed
row is the catalogue, and it is written in the row.

| row | steel | technic_classic | technic_2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| centre→fourth, 60 | 75:10 × 80:10 | 16:8 × 16:8 × 24:8 × 40:8 | 60:8 × 64:8 |
| fourth→escape, 15/2 | 80:8 on a 15T wheel at 2.5 Hz | 60:8 on an 8T wheel at 1 Hz | 60:8 |
| motion works, 12 | 30:10 × 32:8 | 16:8 × 16:8 × 24:8 | 24:8 × 56:14 |
| reserve reduction, 21/10 | 28:8 × 6:10 | 24:16 × 28:20 | 24:16 × 28:20 |

### Where each half lives, and why

- **`CATALOGUES`** (`src/layout.js`). `steel: null` plus two technic
  generations, because the catalogue moved in 2026 (14T 7785, 18T 7786, 64T
  7861) and a fork should not silently mean one year. A count is a row,
  `{ teeth, part, kind }`: `part` is what a BOM orders, and `kind` is what makes
  a centre distance mean anything. The 18T is a clutch (`slips: true`) and
  `catalogueMesh` bars it from a going train, by rule rather than by omission.
  The rows were read from BrickLink on 2026-09-12. The roadmap's
  feasibility note carries the part table.
- **`solveRatio`** (`src/layout.js`). The shortest exact chain, ties broken by
  fewest total teeth. It is a breadth-first search to depth 6, so the build
  never CALLS it: it is exported for tier two and for the probe.
- **`TECHNIC_FORK`** (`src/layout.js`). The four rows, each chain written as
  pairs `[driver, driven]`. No row carries its target. `technicForkTargets()`
  derives three of them from `TRAIN`, the motion-works counts and RATE_TABLE's
  law at the catalogue's own 1 Hz, 8T escapement. The fourth, the reserve's, is
  derived in `main.js` beside `RESERVE_SWEEP_DEG`, because the reduction is a
  consequence of the sweep and `layout.js` cannot see it.
- **Boot asserts**, one in each file. Each declared chain must name catalogue
  counts, every pair must be an admissible mesh, and the product must equal the
  live steel invariant. Re-gear a steel train and boot warns that the fork
  describes a ratio the movement no longer has. The `main.js` assert also holds
  the steel reserve counts to the scale at every reserve, which is TODO 18's
  assert seen from the fork's side.
- **Optimality is the probe's job.** A boot cannot afford the search that says
  a declared chain is the BEST one. `tools/probe-219-catalogue.mjs` now imports
  everything above rather than keeping a copy, and its new claim 6 fails if any
  declared chain differs from what `solveRatio` returns.

### One rule that is easy to break

**The fork is solved at the identity spec.** The reserve target is 7h/100, so
at any other `reserveHours` it is a different fork, and the chains are checked
only at `TECHNIC_FORK.solvedAt`. The other three rows do not depend on the
spec. `rsvTeethW2` is fractional at four of the five menu reserves (TODO 134,
open). `BigInt` refuses a fraction, so the assert reads the count as a decimal
rational rather than restating `reserveHours / 5`.

  refused under both generations, 120:7 held as the documented non-refusal.
- Every control fired:
  - **Boot assert:** silent on the clean tree, and it fired on each of three
    mutations:
    - a declared chain whose product is wrong (105/2 against 60);
    - a pair off the lattice (24:12 at 18 mm);
    - a re-geared steel hour wheel (target 27/2).
  - **Claim 6:** an exact, admissible but suboptimal chain (40:8 × 24:8 ×

## §219 tier two (a) — the technic fork drawn: `technic.html`

**PARTIAL**, like the entry it extends. Tier two (b) is still unbuilt: the
movement booted at module 1 under `?catalogue=technic`. So is tier three's
LDraw/Studio export.

### Why a page and not a boot

LEGO gears are module 1, three to four times the watch's 0.24–0.30. A movement
re-solved at that module grows 5–8× (the scoping note's F4), and every part
with no LEGO row (the escapement, the balance, the fusee) would stay steel
beside it. A boot like that is tier two (b), and its sweeps are a report until
a bar is set for it. The four rows tier one declared can be seen and checked
without any of that, so they get their own page first.

### What the page claims, and what it only draws

**Claimed, read from `layout.js` and never restated:**
- every tooth count;
- each pair's kind and mesh class (`catalogueMesh`);
- every parallel centre distance, half the sum of the counts, drawn over the
  4 mm lattice;
- every shaft's speed, the running product of the pairs before it. The angles
  travel the gears, so the shafts are not posed from the output.

Each row prints its exact rational product against `technicForkTargets()`.
The reserve row is held by `main.js`'s assert, and the page says so. Each row
also prints a MEASURED output ÷ input read off the shaft angles.

**Drawn, and said to be drawn:**
- the involute outline (20°, module 1);
- the cross bore;
- a 4 mm face (half a stud, so two layers never touch);
- an 8 mm step between a shaft's gears (one stud, so each mesh is coplanar);
- the 60T turntable's face teeth;
- the colours.

**Not drawn:** beams and frames. Whether a chain can be BRACED is tier three
(b)'s question.

**Phasing:** a parallel pair is phased exactly. With the driver's tooth `pa`
off the line of centres, the driven gear's gap faces it when
`Na·pa + Nb·(pb − π) ≡ π (mod 2π)`, and since the driven gear turns `−Na/Nb`
as fast, that holds at every angle. The 90° meshes on the bevel top were
placed but not phased when the page shipped; they are phased now (below).

### What it measured on its first run

Every row in both generations reads its declared product:

| row | product |
|---|---|
| centre→fourth | 60.0000 |
| fourth→escape | 7.5000 |
| motion works | 12.0000 |
| reserve reduction | 2.1000 |

The console was silent and the page logged nothing. The drawing makes two of
tier one's choices visible:
- **technic_2026's centre→fourth** is two meshes only because the first is a
  90° mesh on the turntable's bevel top. Seen in 3D, that is a different build
  from the parallel-axis chain the backlog first wrote down.
- **technic_2026's motion works** puts its 56T on a turntable's own frame at
  35 mm, off the lattice. The page flags it.

### Shipping

The page is stamped and precached as an ADOPTED document (`stamp-release.mjs`),
on the primer's terms. Release tags cut before it have no such page, so a
missing page is tolerated and said, and `offline-check.mjs` builds from the
source tree, where a mis-listed seed fails. `build-pages.mjs` marks it noindex
in every environment, like `test-geometry.html`. `battery.yml`'s `paths-ignore`
lists it: it imports `src/layout.js`, but nothing on `index.html`'s graph
imports it, and the battery's own check holds that.

### The 90° mesh, phased

The page shipped with its one right-angle mesh — the 60T turntable's face teeth
driving an 8T pinion, in both generations' fourth→escape row and in 2026's
centre→fourth — phased at 0. Measured, that is the worst index there is:
**exactly tooth on tooth**, half a pitch out, in all three places it appears.

It is the parallel rule in another frame. The contact is the pinion's point
nearest the face, `−u` from its centre. There the face moves along the
turntable's tangent `v` at `R·ω_A`, and the pinion's rim along `u_B × (−u)` at
`r·ω_B`. Those two directions are parallel, and `σ` is the sign between them.
`σ` is read off the frames rather than written down, and it sets the pinion's
sense: `ω_B = σ·ω_A·Na/Nb`. Measured along `v`, the driver's tooth arc is
`R·pA` and the pinion's is `σ·r·(pB − βc)`, where `βc` is the contact's angle
in the pinion's own frame. The pair meshes when the two arcs differ by half the
module-1 pitch `π`. Both arcs advance at `R·ω_A`, so a phase that holds at rest
holds at every angle:

    pB = βc + σ·(Na·pA − π)/Nb

`σ` came out `+1` for every placement the page makes, so no shaft's speed
changed. Only the pinion's index moved.

**`tools/probe-technic-phase.mjs` measures it off the drawn gears**, never off
the phase the page computed. For each mesh it finds:
- the contact point;
- the tangent the two surfaces share there;
- each gear's tooth-0 direction.

It converts both tooth offsets to arc along that tangent. It reads the result
at five input angles, set the way the page's loop sets them. A phase solved at
rest but turned the wrong way reads ½ at angle 0 and drifts at every other
angle, so the spread across angles is what catches a sign error.

Controls:
- **must-hit:** the 14 parallel meshes, which the page's closed form already
  phased, read ½.
- **must-fail:** half a pitch injected into every driven gear reads 0.

**17/17 meshes read ½ at every angle**, worst `2.9e-13` of a pitch, and every
injection reads tooth on tooth. The bar is `1e-6`: both sides are closed forms
in float64.

## §219 tier three (b), landing A — the frame that would hold the technic fork, as millimetres: `src/technic-frame.js`

**PARTIAL.** This is the frame's topology, solved and checked in millimetres
from a table of LEGO dimensions that is UNVERIFIED. Landing B reads every one
of those dimensions from the LDraw part files and fails on a mismatch, then
renders the build with real parts. It needs `library.ldraw.org`, which this
landing's container could not reach.

### The construction

One idiom serves every parallel row:
- each axle is held at both ends by a COLUMN on a baseplate: a bottom plate,
  1×1 plates and bricks to height, then a 1×1 Technic brick whose hole the axle
  passes through;
- an axle on the stud grid stands on a 1×1 plate. One half a stud off it stands
  on a jumper plate, LEGO's own half-stud offset;
- the column height is the lowest stack whose hole clears the row's largest tip
  over the baseplate's studs (`columnFor`).

1×1 columns are 8 mm wide, so even the 12 mm spacing (16:8) leaves a gap
between neighbouring columns. A 1×2 or a beam could not do that.

### The step between stages is derived, and it is not technic.html's

`technic.html` steps every stage one 8 mm layer along the axle. A frame cannot
always do that. With a one-layer step:
- the previous shaft's back column shares a layer with this shaft's driver;
- the next shaft's front column shares a layer with this shaft's driven gear.

In both cases the column stands one centre distance from the gear. The first
run framed the rows that way and failed two of them. In classic centre→fourth,
the 40T's tips reach 21 mm against a column face 12 mm away.

So `rowLayout` steps one layer only when both gears' tips clear the neighbouring
column's near face (`tip ≤ cd − stud/2`). Otherwise it steps two layers, and the
columns stand in the empty layer between. On today's rows:
- classic centre→fourth and motion works step two layers at every stage;
- the reserve reduction steps one.

`technic.html` still draws one layer everywhere. When landing B renders the
build, that page should consume `rowLayout`, so the placement law is written
once.

### Not framed, by name

`rowLayout` refuses two kinds of row and gives the reason:
- a right-angle mesh on the 60T turntable's face teeth. This is classic
  fourth→escape and both of technic_2026's going rows;
- a turntable on its own frame off the 4 mm lattice: technic_2026's motion
  works, 56:14 at 35 mm.

Both need the turntable's mounting, which is LDraw geometry.

### `tools/probe-technic-frame.mjs`

This is a pure-Node acceptance test. Per framed row it checks that:
- every hole stands at its axle's height;
- every axle has a part LEGO makes;
- no two columns' parts overlap;
- no gear's swept disc (tip radius over its face width) reaches the baseplate's
  studs, another shaft's axle, a column it does not stand on, or a gear it does
  not mesh;
- every mesh stands at its catalogue centre distance, in one layer.

Its controls:
- a column slid toward its neighbour must fail, on every row;
- every column a brick short must fail, on every row;
- every gear folded into one layer must give a gear-against-gear finding
  somewhere in the fork.

Forcing the one-layer step back in turns both two-layer rows red. That is how
the step rule was found, and it stays the first thing to try if the rule is
ever loosened.

| row | axles | axle height | base | axles (studs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic centre→fourth | 5 | 25.0 mm (3 plates + 1 brick, then the Technic brick) | 9×9 | 3/5/5/5/3 |
| classic motion works | 4 | 15.4 mm (3 plates, then the Technic brick) | 6×7 | 3/5/5/3 |
| reserve reduction (both) | 3 | 18.6 mm (1 plate + 1 brick, then the Technic brick) | 7×4 | 3/4/3 |

These are 4 rows framed, 4 refused with their reason, and 0 failing. Every
figure in the table moves when landing B corrects a dimension, which is why the
probe holds the topology and not the numbers.

## §219 tier three (b), landing B — the LEGO build from real parts: measured dimensions, a placed build, and `technic.html`'s LEGO view

**PARTIAL, like the entries it extends.** What is still unbuilt:
- the turntable rows (four of the eight, the same four landing A refused);
- moving the drawn view onto `rowLayout`'s derived step.

### The parts are vendored, and measured

`tools/ldraw-vendor.mjs` reads the part list from `layout.js`' catalogues and
the frame's tables. It fetches every part and every sub-file it references from
library.ldraw.org and packs them into `vendor/ldraw/technic-parts.mpd`: 28 parts
in 246 files, 472 KiB. The library allows 60 requests a minute, so the fetcher
starts one every 1.05 s.

**The first pack carried the server's HTTP headers, cookies included, at the
head of all 246 files.** Behind the container's proxy, `curl -D -` prints the
proxy's CONNECT response before the server's headers, and the fetcher split
that stream at its first blank line. The parser skips lines it does not know,
so every measurement read correctly and nothing looked wrong. Re-running the
vendor tool to check its own determinism found it, because the dates differed.
The fetcher now reads the one header it needs through curl's write-out and
dumps none. The vendor tool refuses any file that does not start as LDraw, and
`probe-technic-ldraw.mjs` fails the committed pack if any section is not LDraw
or any line is an HTTP header. That check fails 247 times on the leaked pack. The parts are CC BY 4.0, with the attribution and the
library's README in `vendor/LICENSE-ldraw.txt`. Both deploys now require that
licence wherever `vendor/ldraw/` ships. The 2026 gears and two primitives come
from the library's unofficial tracker and say so in their own headers.

`src/ldraw.js` is a small pure parser. It reads the line types the parts use,
composes placements, walks a part down to its triangles, and finds where a
sub-file (a hole, a stud) is placed. The probes and the page share it. It
replaces three.js' `LDrawLoader` for two reasons. The probes needed a parser
anyway, and this way the only third-party content is the part data itself.

### What the parts corrected

`tools/probe-technic-ldraw.mjs` re-reads every entry of the frame's tables off
the parts. Landing A's figures were wrong in five places:

| quantity | landing A | the parts |
|---|---|---|
| stud height | 1.7 mm | 1.6 mm (4 LDU) |
| hole centre above a Technic brick's bottom | 5.8 mm | 5.6 mm (the pin hole sits 10 LDU under a 24 LDU top) |
| gear face width | 7.2 mm (the 16T 3.6) | 8 mm for every gear but the 24T, which is 7.7 |
| gear tip radius | teeth/2 + 1 | measured per part, `GEAR_TIP_MM` |

The tip is the one that mattered most. The 12T stands 0.084 mm and the 20T
0.045 mm outside the module-1 tip, so sweeping the formula swept less than the
metal. The tip is also the surface's farthest point from the axis, not its
bounding box's extreme. The box reads the 8T's tips at 12.43 LDU because no
tooth points along a box axis; they stand 12.45 out.

The probe's first run also found the jumper with two "studs". The second was

The 24T's library file is `3648`, now an alias that points to `3648b`; the

Every axle came down 0.2 mm, and the frame probe still frames the same four
rows.

Controls: each table entry is perturbed in turn (each dimension by 0.1 mm, a

All 11 must fail, and do.

### The build: `src/technic-ldraw.js`

This module turns a framed row into LDraw placements:
- the baseplate, its centre snapped to its own stud grid;
- every column part, at its top face;
- every axle, turned from its own X onto LDraw Z;
- every gear.

The frame-to-LDraw map is `(x, −z, y) / 0.4`. That is a proper rotation, never
a mirror.

**Each shaft turns as one keyed body.** A gear on a cross axle can be re-seated
only by a quarter turn, which is a whole number of teeth for every count
divisible by four. So a shaft has one free angle, and it is solved so that its
driven gear's gap faces the driver's tooth:

    Na·A + Nb·(B − π) ≡ π (mod 2π)

The tooth angle of each part in its own frame is MEASURED off its geometry: the
argument of the sum of e^{iNθ} over the tip points, divided by N. Every gear's
tips cohere at its own count (0.74–1.0). The shafts then turn at the running
product of their pairs, with alternating signs.

`toLdr` writes the same placements as a model file, coloured by role with
LDraw's codes. Studio or LDView opens it with its own part library.

`tools/probe-technic-build.mjs` measures the placements against the real part
geometry, never against the tables:
- **seated:** every column on the baseplate's studs (a jumper on the half stud
  between two), every part's underside on the top face of the part below it;
- **through:** both hole faces of every Technic brick on the axle's line, and
  the axle filling each hole to within the half LDU the library trims off each
  axle end;
- **on the axle:** every gear's bore on its axle's line, within the axle;
- **meshed:** each pair's tooth angles measured off the placed tips, at five
  input angles;
- **the file:** the `.ldr` parsed back gives the same placements.

Its two controls are a half pitch injected into each driven shaft in turn, and
a Technic brick lifted one plate. The first version shifted every driven shaft
at once. On an 8T driven by a 40T, the shift comes back 6π on the next mesh,
which reads meshed, so the control missed it. The control now shifts one shaft
at a time and reads that shaft's own mesh.

| row | parts | meshes, worst |
|---|---|---|
| classic centre→fourth | 64 | 1.0e-13 of a turn |
| classic motion works | 43 | 1.2e-14 |
| reserve reduction (both) | 26 | 2.4e-14 |

## §219 tier three (b), landing C1 — the turntables measured, the 56T held to the lattice, the drawn view on the frame's step

**PARTIAL.** The three rows that drive the 60T turntable at 90° are measured

### The 56T turntable is held to the lattice; 2026's motion works re-solved

Tier one exempted the 56T turntable from the 4 mm lattice ("it mounts on its
own frame"), and the 2026 solver took 24:8 × 56:14 for the motion works, with

- 35 mm is not 4·√(a² + b²) for any whole a and b, so not even a diagonal

### The turntables measured

`probe-technic-ldraw.mjs` now gates each turntable's tooth count against the
catalogue. The count is read off the tips: the smallest N at which the
outermost points cohere, with no count that is not a multiple of it cohering
at all.

| part | catalogue | tips repeat | coherence | best rival |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2855 (56T) | 56 | 56 | 0.706 | 0.003 |
| 18938 (60T) | 60 | 60 | 0.786 | 0.000 |

The first version took the strongest N. It read the 60T's own second harmonic
(0.240) as a rival count, and once its range was widened it let 224 = 4 × 56 win
outright. A control claims two teeth more than each part has, and must fail.

The 60T's base, 18939, joined the pack (now 29 parts, 257 files) through a new
`TURNTABLE_BASES` table. The probe reports the assembly's profile as landing
C2's inputs:
- the toothed band stands 3.08 mm below to 2.00 mm above the face top and base
  share;
- the tips are at 30.70 mm, and the gaps open to 27.72 mm through the whole
  band;
- the body above stays within 27.60 mm of the axis, the base beside it within
  26.80 mm;
- six pin holes sit 8 mm below the face, 16 mm out from the axis.

**What that means for the 90° mesh.** The 60T's teeth are radial bars. Their
gaps run through the whole height, with a chamfer about 1 mm deep on the lower
edge, so a pinion at 90° meets them as it would a rack of bars. That
engagement is plausible, but the ring fixes no depth for it, so C2 has to take
the depth from module-1 proportions. Its window runs from one addendum (1 mm)
to the whole depth (2.25 mm). The mount the lattice reaches is:
- the turntable pinned through its base holes into Technic bricks, 3 studs
  from its axis;
- the 8T on a radial axle, cantilevered from two columns outside the ring,
  engaging from above;
- the lattice arithmetic putting the 8T's tips 2.18 mm into the ring, inside
  the window. From below it gives 0.98 mm, just short.

C2 builds that mount. It needs pins, Technic bricks turned a quarter turn, a
90° phase solve, and clearances measured between the parts' own surfaces: the
pin columns' studs come within a few tenths of a millimetre of the base's ring,
and the frame probe's boxes cannot judge that.

### The drawn view on the frame's step

`technic.html` now stands each shaft's driver at `rowLayout`'s derived step (8

control fails if every step is 8, because then the comparison could not tell
the two laws apart. 17/17 meshes still mesh.

### Not changed

The catalogue's 24T stays `3648`. That is the number a BOM orders, and the
library resolves it through its `~Moved to 3648b` alias. The probe prints the
alias beside the part.

## §219 tier three (b), landing C2 — the 60T refused on its measured mount; every row a LEGO build

### Why the mount fails, measured

The base's underside has a recess 1.6 mm deep, exactly a stud's height. Its
ceiling stands 2.4 mm below the face the top and base share, so LEGO evidently
meant Technic bricks to sit under it, studs up. But a 1×1 Technic brick pinned
into the base's lug is placed by the parts themselves:
- the lug's face is 20 mm from the axis;
- the brick's centre is half a stud beyond that;
- the stud's edge is a stud's radius further, at 26.40 mm.

The recess rim starts at 26.08 mm, so the stud cuts **0.32 mm** into the rim.
The brick's top also sits flush under the rotating ring.
`probe-technic-ldraw.mjs` prints all of this from the parts, each run.

Two other mounts were weighed:
- **A longer pin, putting the brick at 4 studs.** Its stud then rises into the
  ring's teeth.
- **Studless liftarms.** These would need parts outside the frame's
  vocabulary, and their ends still touch the ring flush.

So the 60T is out of going trains. In `catalogueMesh`, `_UNMOUNTABLE` refuses
it beside the clutch gear's `slips`, and the comment there carries the

## §219 tier three (b), landing D — the keyless works as a LEGO transmission

The steel stem carries one sliding pinion, which a pull moves between the
winding and setting wheels. LEGO has its own version of that idiom, and the
landing builds the keyless works from it:
- two **16T clutch gears** (18946) idle on the stem;
- a **ridged joiner** (18948) is keyed to the stem between them;
- a **driving ring** (18947) slides on the joiner's ridges into one gear's
  recess or the other's;
- a **wedge belt wheel** (4185a) is the crown, outside the stem's front column.

The first plan used the older ring (6539) and its extension (32187). Measured,
those parts do not say what keys the ring to the axle, so the landing uses the
current system, whose joiner carries an axle hole.

### The rows

Each branch is a chain whose first driver is the clutch gear. `solveRatio`
gained a `first` option for that: the first mesh's driver is a part outside the
catalogue, meshing as a parallel member of its own count. The targets are
derived from the steel counts, so a re-geared steel keyless chain fails the boot
assert:

| branch | target | classic | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| winding | pinion → crown wheel → spur = 10/24 = **5/12** | 16:24 × 8:16 × 8:16 × 40:16 | 16:64 × 20:12 |
| setting | pinion → setting wheel → minute wheel, cap → cannon = **1/3** | 16:24 × 8:16 | 16:24 × 8:16 |

Each target is a magnitude, as in the fork's rows. Which way each branch turns
belongs to the build.

### The frame

`frameKeyless` lays out:
- the stem as shaft 0, carrying the crown, the setting clutch, the joiner and
  ring, and the winding clutch;
- each branch with `rowLayout`, moved so that its clutch gear stands against
  its own end of the joiner. The winding branch leaves along +x and the setting
  branch along −x. Only positions are mirrored, never a part.

`frameShafts` (factored out of `frameRow`) frames any set of parallel shafts.
It takes an explicit mesh list and non-gear bodies: the joiner, the ring and
the crown. The crown is OUTBOARD, so it sets the axle's length and not where
the columns stand.

The frame probe found one thing on its first run. Swept as one 7.2 mm disc over
its stroke, the ring reached both 24T gears. That reading was false: the
flanges stop 6.8 mm from the ring's middle, and only the 5.43 mm end tube runs
past them. The ring is now framed in its two measured sections, and it clears.

### What the parts measured

Every figure in `KEYLESS_DIM_MM` is read off a part's SURFACE (triangles and
quads), to the micron, never its bounding box: the box put the joiner's
radius at 3.51 mm, where its middle collar stands at 3.80 mm.

The ring's TRAVEL is derived: joinerHalf + recessDepth − ringHalf, which is
12 + 3.2 − 11.2 = **4 mm**. At that travel the ring's end seats on the web at
the bottom of the gear's recess. Centred, each end stands 0.8 mm short of its
gear, so neutral is a real third position.

The parts also draw the **dog clutch**, which the first plan had expected to
be missing:
- four lugs on each end of the ring reach out to 5.43 mm;
- four lugs in each recess's mouth reach in to 5.32 mm;
- the ring's tube (4.75 mm) passes the gear's lugs, and the ring's lugs pass the
  gear's wall (5.89 mm).

So the only thing that keeps the two sets of lugs apart is their angle.
`dogPhase` reads both sets the way `toothPhase` reads a gear's teeth. It sets
each clutch gear's angle on the stem so the ring's lugs sit midway between the
gear's own. That angle is 0: the parts' own orientations already interleave
them. Each branch is then phased FROM its clutch gear.

### The build and its modes

A build now has BODIES: one per shaft, plus one per clutch gear, since a clutch
gear idles on the stem. Speed is per MODE:
- the stem turns in every mode;
- a clutch gear turns only in its own mode;
- the ring SLIDES ±4 mm with the mode.

Phases are solved from the line of centres, because a branch can leave along
−x. `placedParts` and `toLdr` take the mode as an argument. The four rows'
`.ldr` files are byte-identical to before this generalisation.

### Instruments

`tools/probe-technic-keyless.mjs` (new) checks:
- **rows**: each equals the solver's answer from the clutch gear;
- **stroke**: read off the placed parts' bounds, the ring stands 3.20 mm into
  the engaged gear and −4.80 into the idle one, and −0.80 / −0.80 in neutral;
- **dogs**: engaged 3.12 mm deep with ±32.2° play, axially clear when idle;
- **keyed**: the ring's ribs sit in the joiner's slots;
- **ratios**: the engaged branch's output turns by exactly ±target × input, and
  the idle branch stands still.

It has five controls, all caught: a changed mesh, the ring 0.8 mm short, the
ring turned 45°, a clutch turned 45°, and the idle branch turning or the engaged
branch at twice its ratio.

The other probes:
- `probe-technic-build.mjs` judges the keyless build in all three modes,
  walking the build's own mesh list: 10 builds, 0 failing;
- `probe-technic-frame.mjs`: 10 rows framed. Its overlap control now finds the
  nearest pair of columns rather than assuming shaft 1's;
- `probe-technic-ldraw.mjs` holds every `KEYLESS_DIM_MM` figure, the clutch
  gear's tip and width, and the lugs' fourfold repeat, with three new
  perturbation controls (16/16).

The pack grew to 33 parts, 273 files.

### What it does not claim

The mode is POSED. No lever moves the ring yet: a changeover catch or a shift
fork in the ring's groove is the next landing's. The ring is posed with its
lugs in the middle of their play, where the parts allow anywhere across it.
`technic.html`'s LEGO view draws the keyless works as its fifth cell, with
wind, set and neutral buttons. A shift restarts the works from its posed angle,
because the lugs only pass each other interleaved.

## §219 tier three (b), landing E — the selector: a changeover catch carries the ring

Landing D left the keyless works' mode POSED: a page button slid the ring.
Landing E adds the part that moves it. It is LEGO's own changeover catch
(6641):
- a rocker with an axle hole along its X through a boss;
- below the boss, a blade whose rounded bulb rides in the driving ring's
  groove.

The catch is keyed to a horizontal SELECTOR axle over the stem, and a wedge
belt wheel on that axle's end is its knob. Turning the knob swings the bulb
along the stem and carries the ring.

### The catch was drawn for the older ring, so it was measured, not assumed

6641 belongs to the older transmission (6539). Against the 3L ring (18947)
the parts give:
- the groove's floor is 4.80 mm from the axis;
- the flanges stand 7.20 mm from the axis;
- the bulb's edge is 10.40 mm below the pivot.

The library has a newer catch with a two-tooth axle hole (51149). Its body is
the same 6641 subfile, so the fit is the same.

### The pivot's height is derived

Both axles stand in Technic bricks on one baseplate, so the pivot rises over
the stem by a whole number of plates. The bulb must clear the groove's floor,
because the ring turns under it, and it must stand inside the flanges, or it
cannot push them. `selectorRise` finds exactly one plate count:

| plates | bulb from the axis | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2.40 mm | inside the floor |
| **5** | **5.60 mm** | **seated** |
| 6 | 8.80 mm | past the flanges |

So the pivot stands 16 mm over the stem. Its columns stand on the +x side,
because the setting branch's 8T sits at y = 0 on −x. The first column is one
gap clear of the ring's flanges, the second beside it, and the knob one gap
outside them, as the crown is. The selector axle is a 5L, held by Technic
bricks turned a quarter so their holes run along x.

### The ring's position is read from the catch, not from a table

- `catchInRingFrame` samples the catch's SURFACE, subdividing every face,
  and hangs it from the pivot at an angle. The subdivision matters: the
  bulb is a single quad whose corners stand 4 mm off the stem's line, and a
  vertex-only reading put the bulb 1.28 mm shallower than its middle really
  is.
- `catchCentre` is the middle of the catch's surface that stands inside the
  flanges. That is where the bulb holds the ring.
- `selectorAngle` solves the angle that holds the ring at each stroke end:
  **±26.02°**.
- The build poses the selector at that angle and reads the ring's slide back
  through `catchCentre`. The mode is now the selector's angle, and the ring
  follows from it.

### What the lever measures

| mode | angle | bulb into the flanges | floor clearance | axial play a side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wind / set | ±26.02° | 0.98 mm | 1.42 mm | 0.54 mm |
| neutral | 0° | 1.60 mm | 0.80 mm | 0.42 mm |

The engagement is shallow at the stroke ends: under a millimetre of plastic
pushes the ring onto its dogs. This is the measured fit of a catch drawn for
a larger-flanged ring. It is reported every run and gated only at greater
than zero. No floor was invented to sit under it.

### Instruments

- `probe-technic-keyless.mjs` adds LEVER checks, per mode:
  - the catch's sampled surface stands clear of the ring, the joiner and both
    clutch gears, each taken as its body of revolution;
  - the bulb reaches inside the flanges and clears the floor;
  - the placed ring stands at the catch's centre, re-derived from the samples
    rather than read from the build.

  Two new controls fire: the selector a plate lower (the bulb in the floor),
  and the selector left at neutral while the ring stands at its wind stroke.
  Controls 6/6.
- `probe-technic-build.mjs` holds the selector as a body turning about X: two
  turned bricks on its line, the axle filling both, and the catch's pivot hole
  and the knob on the axle. 10 builds, 0 failing, in every mode.
- `probe-technic-frame.mjs` checks each column's hole against its own axle
  height, and sweeps the selector's columns against every gear: 10 rows,
  0 failing.
- `probe-technic-ldraw.mjs` holds `KEYLESS_SELECTOR_DIM_MM` to the parts:
  the pivot from the axle-hole primitive, the bulb, the catch's extents, and
  the groove floor. It also checks that the rise is still a single plate
  count. Controls 18/18.

The pack grew to 34 parts, 277 files.

### What it does not claim

The selector's three angles are posed. No detent holds it there, and nothing
yet ties it to a crown pull. The ring is posed with its lugs mid-play, and the
bulb is centred in the groove, though the parts would allow anywhere across
either.

## §219 tier three (b), landing F — the pull and the detent: a rack turns the selector, a wheel holds it

Landing E left the selector's three angles posed. Nothing held them, and
nothing tied them to a pull. Landing F adds both:
- **the pull** — a 1×14 rack (18942) under a 28T (46372) on the selector
  axle, sliding on tiles between two walls. Pushed in it winds, drawn out it
  sets;
- **the detent** — a small gear on the same axle (the DETENT WHEEL) with a
  round half bush (32123a) on a 1×3 thin liftarm (6632) banded onto it. A mode
  is one of the wheel's gaps.

### What was measured first, and refused

The plan's detent was an 8T keyed on an arm, clicking into the rack's own
teeth. Two measurements ruled it out before any of it was built.

- **The keyed 8T is a ratchet, not a detent.** It meshes the rack as a gear
  does. Traced continuously out of its notch, pushing the rack toward the
  arm's pivot locks it: the 8T would have to climb a whole 2 mm tooth. The
  other way it rides up about 1 mm per mm of travel. Every pivot height on
  the lattice did the same on one side.
- **Any detent on the rack clicks at every rack tooth, and a mode is two of
  them.** Through the 28T one tooth is 12.86° of selector, so the click
  between modes holds the ring 1.96 mm into its 4 mm travel. That is 1.16 mm
  past the clutch gear's face (it enters at 0.8 mm): half engaged.

So the detent moved to the selector axle, one wheel tooth per mode.

### The wheel is surveyed, not chosen

One tooth of an N-tooth wheel turns the selector 360°/N. `catchHold` reads
where that leaves the catch's middle against the ring's web, and the catch's
PLAY at that angle: how far the bulb's middle may stand off the ring before a
flange meets it. A wheel seats the ring when the web lies within the play.

| wheel | one tooth | catch's middle off the web | play | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8T | 45° | (the bulb leaves the groove) | — | ✗ |
| **12T** | **30°** | **+0.46 mm** | **0.91 mm** | **classic** |
| **14T** | **25.71°** | **−0.05 mm** | **0.53 mm** | **2026** |
| 16T | 22.5° | −0.57 mm | 0.51 mm | ✗ |
| 18T, 20T, 24T, 40T, 64T | | short by 0.95–3.12 mm | | ✗ |

The play opens with the angle, because the tilted bulb spans less of the
groove, which is why the 12T's 0.46 mm overshoot seats. The 14T is the 2026
catalogue's alone.

### The follower's placement is a search over the lattice

The half bush hangs from the arm's far hole. Its pivot stands on a column pair
of turned Technic bricks, so the pivot is on the stud grid across and the
plate lattice up. For a pivot two studs toward the rack's side, every plate
height and both band senses were searched, plus both mounts of the 14T on its
axle (a quarter turn is 3.5 of its teeth). Each candidate was:
- sampled for its follower's height over a tooth;
- refused unless its notch's two flanks agree within 30% (a detent, not a
  ratchet);
- scored by the worst of three ring errors, each a fraction of what that
  error may be. At neutral the ring must stay off both gears' faces (0.8 mm);
  in wind and set it must lie within the catch's play of the web.

| | wheel | pivot | rests (wind/neutral/set) | ring at neutral | lift | flanks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | 12T at 0° | 2 plates up, sense + | 28.89° / −1.11° / −31.11° | −0.18 mm | 0.53 mm | 0.076 / 0.091 mm/° |
| 2026 | 14T at 90° | 3 plates up, sense − | 26.06° / 0.34° / −25.37° | +0.05 mm | 0.43 mm | 0.085 / 0.079 mm/° |

A first search ran on 0.8 mm heights and found notches exactly at neutral.
The bricks step by a plate (3.2 mm), so none of them could be built. On the
real lattice the 1×2 arm (8 mm) left classic's notch 4.5° off, which moved the
ring 0.7 mm. The 1×3 arm (16 mm between its end holes) brings it to 1.11°.

The half bush is centred on the wheel's outer face. Its inner flange, the
part that bears, lies wholly on the wheel, and the arm outboard of it stands
2 mm clear of the turning wheel's face.

### The rack follows the rests

The pinion rolls the rack tooth for tooth: one rack pitch (3.2 mm) per pinion
tooth, so a selector angle θ is θ·28·3.2/2π of rack. The rack is phased so
the pinion stands in the middle of its MEASURED backlash at neutral: the
angles either side at which its teeth first enter the rack's, about ±1.25°.

| | wind | neutral | set |
|---|---|---|---|
| classic rack | +7.19 mm | −0.28 mm | −7.74 mm |
| 2026 rack | +6.49 mm | +0.09 mm | −6.31 mm |

The rack's height is derived. It slides on tiles, so its underside is a whole
number of plates, and `pullRackLift` finds the one count that puts its pitch
line 0.4 mm short of the pinion's (a shallow mesh, inside the window between
tip-on-root and teeth parted).

The rack is not symmetric. Its 32 teeth run from −44.8 to +54.4 mm along it,
and its other end carries a round boss 0.6 mm proud of the tips. PULL holds
the pinion to the toothed span, which it never leaves (+2 to +46 mm).

### The ring is pushed onto the web

Driven into wind or set, the bulb's leading side pushes the ring ahead of the
bulb's middle by up to the play, and the web stops it. So the ring stands on
the web whenever the web lies within the play of the middle, either way. In
2026's set the middle stands 0.11 mm short, and the ring still seats. At
neutral the ring is posed at the middle.

### Instruments

- `probe-technic-keyless.mjs`:
  - **PULL** — at each mode the posed angle stands strictly inside the pinion's
    backlash (both edges measured), its teeth reach 1.29–1.32 mm below the
    rack's tips, it works only the toothed span, and the rack rests on its
    tiles.
  - **DETENT** — re-runs the survey and the placement search and fails if
    either answers other than the table. In the build, every rest is a notch

  - **CLEAR** — the pull's moving parts, sampled every LDU, stay over one LDU
    from every placed part they do not bear on, in every mode. The nearest
    approaches are the rack to a support's plate (1.60 mm) and the follower's
    axle to the 14T (1.69 mm).
  - **LEVER** now holds the ring on the web within the play at wind and set.

  Four new controls fire: the rack half a tooth along, the selector half a
  wheel tooth off its rest, the wheel mounted half a tooth round (the notch and
  the ring move into a gear), and the arm slid onto the wheel's face. Controls
  10/10.
- `probe-technic-ldraw.mjs` holds `KEYLESS_PULL_DIM_MM` to the parts:
  - the rack's pitch, tip, root, underside, length, width and toothed span off
    18942's tooth primitives and surface;
  - the arm's hole spacing and thickness off 6632's axle-hole primitives;
  - the half bush's radius and width;
  - every new part's title, and the 32×32 baseplate where a frame outgrows the
    16×16.

  Controls 22/22.
- `probe-technic-build.mjs`: 10 builds, 0 failing, in every mode. The rack has
  no axle and the follower rides off the pivot's line, so both are left to

- `probe-technic-frame.mjs`: 10 rows, 0 failing.

The keyless frame is 21×17 studs (classic) and 19×13 (2026), on the 32×32
baseplate (3811). The pack grew to 39 parts, 302 files. `technic.html` draws

### What it does not claim

- **A force.** The band that presses the follower is drawn posed. Nothing

- **A motion.** The rack's position per mode is the detent's rest read

- **The ring's exact place within the play.** Its lugs are posed mid-play,
  and at neutral the bulb is centred in the groove. Which side it actually
  lies on depends on the mode it came from.

## §219 tier three (b), landing G — the motion works: the hour wheel idles on the minute axle, and the crown sets the hands through the gears

The keyless setting branch ends on the minute hand's axle. Its row's target is
the steel's stem-to-cannon-pinion ratio, 1/3, so its last shaft is the cannon
pinion's. Landing G builds the motion works on that axle:
- the **hour wheel** is a round-bored gear idling on the minute axle, LEGO's
  own clock idiom;
- four meshes carry the train from the minute axle back to it;
- in `set` the crown turns the hands through those gears (rule 2).

### What the fork row already did, and what it did not

`TECHNIC_FORK`'s 'motion works' row frames the 12:1 as a straight chain:
- classic 16:8 × 16:8 × 24:8, on four axles;
- 2026 24:8 × 64:16, on three.

Its hour wheel and its minute pinion stand on different axles, and nothing
drives it. A clock needs the hour hand on the minute hand's axle, which a
straight chain cannot give. The row stays as the RATIO fork, and its build is
unchanged. Landing G is the coaxial build beside it, joined to the keyless
works.

### Two meshes cannot do it, and three turn the hour hand backwards

- **Two meshes.** The steel motion works is two meshes on equal centre
  distances: cannon 10:30 and minute pinion 8:32, both 20 mm. Two meshes
  between the same two axles must have equal tooth sums. No two catalogue
  pairs with equal sums multiply to 12, in either generation. The solver's
  two-mesh search closes no chain at all.
- **Three meshes.** An odd number of external meshes turns the hour wheel
  against the minute axle.
- So **four** is the fewest.

The feasibility note's "(8:24)(16:64) = 3·4 — two meshes, the sim's own count,
on 2- and 5-stud centres" is the fork row. Its two centres differ, so it
cannot come back to its own axle. That note lives in the roadmap repo and is
not corrected here.

### The hour wheel is the keyless works' clutch gear

It is the 16T clutch gear (18946), so the landing adds no part to the
catalogue or the pack. Its bore is a pin hole, read off the part:
- every surface point near its axis stands 2.4000–2.4001 mm out;
- the cross axle's farthest surface stands 2.4000 mm out.

LDraw draws a pin hole and an axle's arm tips at the same nominal 6 LDU, a
running fit at zero drawn clearance. A keyed 16T (4019) reads its cross from
1.44 mm. That is the IDLES control.

### The solve: chain and placement together

Whether a coaxial chain is a BUILD depends on where its shafts stand, so
`solveMotionWorks` (technic-frame.js) searches both at once.

The chain:
- an even number of meshes;
- each mesh an admissible going-train mesh of the catalogue's spurs and
  double bevels. A turntable's top runs on its own base, not on an axle, and
  the 18T clutch-on-both-sides slips;
- the last mesh drives the hour wheel;
- the product is exactly 1/12, pruned by the set of products the remaining
  meshes can still make.

The placement:
- every shaft is on the frame's 4 mm lattice in its one plane, and the signed
  centre distances close on the minute axle;
- the layers rise along the axles, one to four layers per stage;
- an intermediate shaft may carry ONE gear as an IDLER, meshing both its
  neighbours in one layer;
- the first mesh is either a new gear keyed to the minute axle, or the setting
  branch's own (the minute axle's 16T into the minute wheel's 8T), which is how
  the steel shares its minute wheel.

Each candidate is framed with the keyless works and kept only when
`frameFindings` is empty. The ranking is:
1. fewest meshes (backlash and friction are per mesh, solveRatio's first rule);
2. fewest new gears;
3. fewest new axles;
4. fewest teeth (solveRatio's second rule);
5. the smallest baseplate footprint;
6. the lowest top layer.

| | 4-mesh chains that close | sharing the minute wheel | placements tried | frame clear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | 64 | 0 | 18,176 | 5 |
| 2026 | 138 | 2 (both on the 64T) | 38,528 | 15 |

Sharing the minute wheel closes nothing in classic. Its 16:8 speeds the train
up ×2 and leaves 1/24 for three meshes, which classic's counts cannot make.
2026's two chains that share it (on the 64T) do not frame.

Both generations answer **8:16 × 8:24 × 8:16 × 16:16**, from the minute axle:

| shaft | off the minute axle | gears (layer along the axles) |
|---|---|---|
| minute axle | — | a new 8T (32 mm); the hour wheel, idling (64 mm) |
| 1 | 12 mm | 16T (32), 8T (40) |
| 2 | 28 mm | 24T (40), 8T (64) |
| 3 | 16 mm | one 16T (64): an IDLER meshing shaft 2's 8T and the hour wheel |

The product is 8/16 · 8/24 · 8/16 = 1/12, since the idler's count cancels. The
landing adds 7 gears (96 teeth) and 3 axles.

Shaft 1 stands on the setting minute wheel's line, so the two are separate
axles end to end, 0.79 mm apart. Every gear sits a pitch circle from its mate.
The closest part-to-part approach is shaft 2's 24T, 0.67 mm from shaft 1's axle,
which is any 8:24 mesh's own geometry.

The probe prints the leading framed candidates beside the answer.
Second-placed are the same chain at a higher top layer, and the 12:36
variants at 120 teeth.

### Joining it to the keyless works

`frameKeyless(gen, motion)` frames the motion works with the keyless works by
default; `null` frames the keyless works alone.

The pull's reach is computed without the motion works. So landing F's derived
rack, wheel and pivot do not move, and the motion works must clear them. It
does: the frame is held to the rack's SWEPT box (its whole stroke, every gear
and column against it). The keyless frame grows from 19×13 to 19×17 studs in
2026. Classic stays 21×17, on the same 32×32 baseplate.

Three instruments changed to read the new construction:
- **The frame probe's judge moved into the module as `frameFindings`**, so the
  solver and the probe read one law. It gained two rules: the rack's swept box,
  and two axles on one line allowed only end to end.
- **`probe-technic-build.mjs` gives each Technic brick on a line to the NEARER
  axle.** Before, it counted four bricks on shaft 1's line and failed it.
- **`probe-technic-keyless.mjs`'s branch walk stays on its branch's own
  meshes.** Its first run walked on through the motion works and read the
  setting output turning 0.37/36. That was the crown-to-hour ratio, showing
  through a check that was not asking for it.

`ldrawBuild` makes a round-bored gear its own body, as it does a clutch gear,
turned only by the mesh that drives it. It also gets its own copy of the
axle's origin: a shared array, kept shared by `structuredClone`, let the
COAXIAL control move both bodies at once and read clean. Found because the
control did not fire.

## §219 tier three (b), landing H — the fusee module: a cord on a stepped cone, its barrel and hooks, and Harrison's maintaining power

The feasibility note's module 2 (its §3.2 and §4) is a fusee with a cord, a
rubber-band barrel and maintaining power. Landing H builds it from LDraw parts
in `src/technic-fusee.js` and draws it as the LEGO view's sixth cell. The build
is MODELLED: every part is placed and measured, and each law that turns one part
from another is geometry read off the parts. Nothing is integrated, and the one
law the build runs on, the spring's torque against its wind, is POSED.

### The spring is a posed table

LEGO has no spring steel. The barrel is a rubber band or a pull-back motor, and
its torque law can only be measured, with a spring scale on the cord at a known
radius. So `TECHNIC_FUSEE_SPRING` is a table of rows `[barrel turns from
run-down, torque]`, labelled `status: 'POSED'`, its source "to be measured".
Until someone measures it, the rows are a STAND-IN: the steel going spring's own
law, M = k·u (§104), at the steel's set-up and band.

- set up 23 clicks of the 24-tooth set-up ratchet (`SETUP_CLICKS`,
  `SETUP_RATCHET_TEETH`);
- run-down torque 0.58986 of full (`SPRING_TQ_EMPTY`, a solve output in
  `main.js`, quoted at the five figures its own comment records);
- so the band is (23/24)·(1/0.58986 − 1) = 0.6663 barrel turns, and the torque
  is scaled to 1 at full wind. The scale cancels: torque × radius is levelled as
  a shape.

The probe reads all three numbers back off `main.js`' TEXT and fails if they
part. The solve reads the spring through this table and nothing else, so a
measured table replaces `rows` and re-solves with no code change. The probe shows
that: a made-up rubber-band-like table (0.30 → 1 over 3 turns) re-solves to a
different barrel and different steps.

### The cone is a staircase of grooved pulleys

No LEGO part is a helically grooved cone, so the cord climbs a stack of
pulleys, one wrap a step. The step catalogue is every keyed round part with a
cord GROOVE, read from the library's titles. A flat rim lets a wrap walk off.
The cord is LEGO's braided string (section ±1.5 LDU, 0.6 mm radius).

`grooveSeat` measures where that cord comes to rest in each groove: the part's
outermost radius R(z) along its axis, the groove between its two outermost rims,
and the cord's circle lowered until it touches. The capacity is how many cords
lie side by side at that seat.

### The solve

`solveFusee` tries every barrel and every descending set of steps.

- Winding takes the cord off the barrel onto the cone, base first. The barrel's
  wind rises by (cord laid)/(2π ρ_b), and the cord's arm is the step's seat.
- The base wrap is PARTIAL, f of a turn, because the hook stands where the run
  begins. Every step above it carries one whole wrap.
- Between two steps the cord crosses on the tangent from the larger circle to
  the smaller: √(ρ_j² − ρ_{j+1}² + Δy²) long, its arm the smaller seat.
- The run spans the whole table, which fixes f. A set whose f falls outside
  (0, 1] is refused, never stretched.
- A step must stand a cord diameter below the one before it, or the wrap beneath
  could not guide the next. A barrel must hold the run in ONE layer, or its
  radius would change mid-run.
- Ranked by the SPREAD of torque × radius over the run, (max − min)/mean, then
  by the fewest steps.

The answer: barrel **3736**, base **4185a** (a 0.209-turn wrap), top **3482**
(one wrap). 35 runs fit the table.

|---|---|---|---|
| 4185a | 11.599 mm | θ 0–0.1336 turns | 11.357 → 9.967 mm |
| 3482 | 8.199 mm | θ 0.2153–0.6663 | 9.273 → 6.699 mm |

**The level product spreads 40.13%** over the run (5.510–8.199 of full-wind

### Maintaining power needs two one-way parts

measured" (wind the band a counted number of barrel turns, count the strikes to
run-down, time them). Until then it stands in the steel's own numbers, read back
off `main.js`' text: `ALARM_BARREL_TURNS` 1.75 and `ALARM_STRIKE_GAP` 0.42. So
**28 strikes per wind** (the steel's 28), 11.76 s of ringing, both POSED. A
measured table replaces `standIn` and the counts follow with no code change; the
probe shows it.

**There is no LEGO gong.** The hammer strikes a tile on a stack of plates, and
the record says so.

### The frame

`frameAlarm` stands the module on an 11×18-stud base: 22 columns and 3 stacks
(the driver's rest and bank, the hammer's struck stack), the lever's axle at
50.4 mm. The axles are lever 4L, disc 6L, fly 9L, middle 7L, barrel 4L, S2 8L,
S1 5L, hour 4L, column wheel 8L, detent 7L and hammer 5L. A greedy allocator
puts each column on a free stud slot along its axle; the column wheel, detent and
hammer columns stand in front, the rest behind. Parts that turn apart stand an
LDU (`AX_CLEAR`, 0.4 mm) off each other.

### Refused, with the reason

- **A stock notched disc**: no part in the library has one (the survey above).
  The notch is three pins.
- **Wheel rims with six holes as a six-column wheel**: their holes sit inside a
  44 mm drum, where no beak reaches them. The rotors are six columns instead.
- **"Two flat plates" for the fly**: a plate has no axle hole at its middle, and
  a beam's end holes carry the cam. The fly is two crossed beams, so it has FOUR
  vanes.
- **A pawl of two 6632s, or two 5Ls**: each rides both ways (above). An 8 mm
  driver with a 16 mm pawl locks, but folds over the column wheel's axle.
- **Knobs on the barrel and the hour axle**: the first build placed a wedge belt
  wheel on each, and CLEAR measured both against neighbouring axles and columns.
  They are removed; nothing here turns either axle but the record.

### Instruments

`tools/probe-technic-alarm.mjs` (new, acceptance, pure Node, about 5 minutes,
most of it the solve):

- **PARTS**: every `ALARM_DIM_MM` figure, the driver's and the pawl's arms read as

- **COLUMN WHEEL**: six columns 60° apart.

- **RELEASE**: the rim, the drop, the fly caught and passed, the push into the

- **INDEX**: the detent's rests, near and far crests and flanks; the pawl's lock

- **STRIKE**: the trace at two steps, the falls extrapolated, the lock's

- **TRAINS**: both ratios, every mesh at its centre distance.
- **BUILD**: the columns at their axles' heights and through their bricks; every

Its controls, 14/14, each seen to fail:

### What it does not claim

- **A force, a torque, a rate in strikes per second, how long it rings.** Every
  band is posed, and a lock is a direction, not a holding force.
- **The cadence.** A fly has no period; the 0.42 s is the steel's number, posed.
- **How a brick sounds.** No part rings.
- **The barrel's winding.** No click is built: winding the barrel back would turn
  the train back with it.
- **A going train.** The hour axle is turned by hand, and nothing drives the
  pusher but a finger.
- **A motion.** The three states are posed; the press, the drop and the strike
  are the laws' arithmetic, not a placed sequence.

## §219 tier two (a), retired — `technic.html` shows only the LEGO build

Tier two (a) drew the fork's rows as procedural module-1 involute gears, before
any row could be built from real parts. Since landing C2 every row frames in
LEGO, and landings D–H added the keyless works, the motion works and the fusee
only to the LEGO build. The drawn view had stopped being updated, so it now
showed less than the build beside it. It is retired, and the build is the one
view:

- **Removed from `technic.html`:** the drawn/LEGO toggle, the procedural gear
  outline, the drawn chain placement and its live ratio meter. The `?view=`
  parameter is ignored, so old links still open the build.
- **Kept:** every count, kind and ratio is still read from `layout.js`. Each
  train row now shows its product against the steel invariant beside the LEGO
  build, the verdict the drawn view used to carry. A row the frame refused
  would be listed in the panel with its reason and not drawn; none is today.
- **`tools/probe-technic-phase.mjs` is deleted with its subject.** It measured
  the drawn gears' tooth phase. The LEGO build's meshing is held by
  `probe-technic-build.mjs` (every mesh within 1.6e-13 of a turn), and the
  frame's stage step (`rowLayout`) by `probe-technic-frame.mjs`.
