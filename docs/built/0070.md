## §70, steps 1–2 for one group — LINE SPECS: the switch's drive line measured off the metal, landed for the fold §261 is about to make

> **PARTIAL.** §70 (roadmap) is the instrument behind CLAUDE.md's "design in a
> line, fold to fit". This landing builds its first two steps, scoped to the
> one group §261 step 4 is about to fold under the dial, so that the fold has
> a reference to measure back to BEFORE it moves a part. Step 3 (declared
> forks) and step 4 (the §35 control-case seeds) remain. A second landing added
> the LOCK that reads the wheel (§183's seed, below). A third built **step 3,
> FORKS**, with the selector link as the first reference that needs one (the
> last section below); step 4's seeds are what remains.

**Why.** Every battery gate before this was SPATIAL: it asked whether the
packaged movement fouls itself. None asked whether the packaged mechanism is
still the one that was designed. §35 fell through exactly that hole:
packaging pressure grew a beak tail to a 36.5× displacement gain and a stall
force a thirtieth of its detent window, with every gate green.

**`LINE_SPECS`** (`src/inspect.js`) is movement-independent declared data.
Each reference names its group and its drive path, and carries rows of the
form `{ key, kind, value, unit, derivedFrom, measure }`.
- **`value`** is a literal, or a function of the rows above it. A derived row
  is derived in the table, from its constraint, and never quoted from the
  build.
- **`kind: 'spec'`** rows are what the line design chose. A fold that must
  change one will declare a fork, once step 3 exists; until then a moved
  spec row fails.
- **`kind: 'envelope'`** rows are windows the mechanism must live inside
  whatever the integration, and they are never forkable. With `bound: 'max'`
  or `'min'` an envelope is one side of a window and fails only on crossing
  it; without one it is an equality, like `carry` below.
- **`inherits`** names an earlier reference whose DERIVED rows are in scope
  for this one's value functions. They are not measured again, because their
  own reference already holds them to the metal.

The first reference is the alarm switch's drive line, pusher → pin in the
driver's radial slot → driver on the wheel's arbor → pawl → column wheel:

| row | kind | derived from | measured off |
|---|---|---|---|
| `columns` 6 | spec | §25 D rev 2, the owner's column-wheel architecture | rays round the castellations, in the wheel's frame |
| `teeth` = 2·columns | spec | one saw tooth per half pitch, because a press steps half a pitch | rays round the saw |
| `baseR` 5.7 | spec | §68's real chronograph scale | the base disc's vertices |
| `rootR` = 0.9·baseR | spec | the saw's root circle | the skirt's cut outline |
| `step` = π/columns | spec | half a pitch per actuation | the DRIVER's sweep over the press (§163: its angle is the pin's azimuth) |
| `travel` = (2π/teeth)·rootR | spec | one tooth pitch at the root circle | the pin's posed positions, cycle 0 → 1 |
| `offset` = travel / (2·tan(step/2)) | spec | §163's coupling | the pin's line against the wheel's axis |
| `straddle` 0 | spec | the stroke straddles the foot of the perpendicular | the same |
| `straight` 0 | spec | a pusher in a bored tube | the pin's deviation from its line |
| `carry` = step | envelope | §163's equality: TODO 87 measured 117 % when it was a floor | the wheel's rotation over a whole actuation |

**The `lineSpec` check** gates every row within float noise of its derived
value (1e-6 u or rad, exact counts), 0 unwaived, 0 stale waivers and 0
malformed, and a control. On the shipped tree all ten rows (twelve since the
lock's landing) measure within
3e-15, except `baseR` at 2.5e-7, which is the float32 vertex store.
- **The control:** the pin's line is shifted a known 0.05 and the measured
  offset must move by exactly that much. A reader that quoted a constant would
  not move.
- **`tools/probe-70-line-spec.mjs`** (acceptance) holds the GATE where the
  control holds the reader. It boots copied trees built wrong on purpose:
  - the wheel grown 5.7 → 5.8 fails exactly `baseR`, `rootR`, `travel` and
    `offset` (and `tipR` and `colInner`, the two rows the lock's landing added);
  - the offset hand-tuned ×1.02 fails exactly `offset` and `step`.

**Two findings from building it.**
- **The vertices trap, again.** The first run read the pin's line 0.0104 u off
  its derived offset. It looked like a 4 µm defect in the coupling, and it was
  the reader: the mean of a cylinder's vertices is pulled off its axis by the
  seam's repeated vertices. The centre of the pin's world box is exact,
  because a 10-gon is centrally symmetric.
- **`step` is the row that sees a mis-set coupling, and `carry` is not.** The
  first draft predicted otherwise. With the offset wrong, the driver sweeps
  29.44° of the 30° a tooth needs, which is §163's own finding reproduced. But
  the whole actuation still carries the wheel one exact step, because the click
  banks it the rest of the way. A reference that held only the wheel's carry
  would have passed the very defect §163 removed.

### The second reference: the alarm lock, §183's seed

§70 named §183's fold as the seed that would land GREEN beside a §35 seed that
would land red: a fold whose line spec was derived first and written at the
constants, and checkable by a reader and by nothing else. It is also the second
link out of the switch §261 folds, and the one most likely to fork, because the
lever, its finger and the stop wheel stay on the striking arbor wherever the
wheel goes. Its path is column wheel → rocker beak on the columns' outer wall →
rocker → pin in the lever's radial slot → lock lever → finger in the stop wheel.

It `inherits` the drive reference for the wheel it reads, which grew two rows
to carry what the lock needs: `tipR` = 1.12·baseR (what a rod crossing the saw

the wheel, so the rocker, the lever and the finger are posed by the build's own

### Step 3 — forks, and the third reference: the selector link

The link is the first reference whose numbers are mostly the OUTPUT of solves
rather than literals, and the first the movement could not keep. Its path is
column wheel → beak lever (nose on the columns, tail on the rod) → selector rod
→ rim finger on the lay shaft → centre pin in the fork → selector ring.

**A reference may call the build's laws.** Its value functions take a second
argument, the laws `main.js` publishes as `__clock.lineLaws`: the beak's seat
solve, the fork pin's arm and the rim finger's top. They are the functions the
build itself calls, refactored out of the IIFEs that held them with the
arithmetic unchanged, so the reference evaluates the law the metal was cut by,
at its own inputs, and no law is written twice.

**A fork moves an input, never a law** (`LINE_SPEC_FORKS`, `inspect.js`). A
fork names one reference and overrides some of its LITERAL spec rows, each with
the constraint in the movement that forced it (`forcedBy`). Every derived row
then re-derives from the reference's own laws at the forked inputs, and the
gate measures the build against those values and lists what the fork moved,
and by which route. Four things are MALFORMED rather than trusted: overriding a
law, overriding an envelope (inherited whole, so §35's tail fails as a fork
too), an override with no forcing constraint, and a second fork of one
reference. An override equal to the reference is a STALE fork and fails, on the
waivers' rule.

| row | reference (§229's fold) | as built (the fork) | how |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wrLen`, the rod's distance from the wheel | 16 | **9** | INPUT — the §112 site solve (TODO 174's corrected guard; TODO 190's next parity ray) |
| `pinD`, the groove's offset from the shaft's axis | 0 (§51's level groove) | **−0.161475** | INPUT — §234 Landing 5 raised the shaft's stratum |
| `ratio`, beakLen / tailLen | 5.7906 | 5.3108 | law: the seat solve at the run |
| `beakLen` / `tailLen` | 9.6743 / 1.6707 | 3.6565 / 0.6885 | law |
| `pinArm` | 0.554101 (T / sin S) | 0.549798 | law: the closed form at D |

The other nine spec rows are the reference's own values: the nose's landing
radius (4.655), the lever collinear and its pin square to it, the shaft's span
(0.35 rad), the finger's rest 60° off its zenith, the rod's foot over the axis
(0.263513) and its stroke (0.100198), the nose's fall onto the gap floor (the
whole column tier, 0.616667), and the ring's travel (0.19). The sixteenth, the
stall, is the envelope: what the chain delivers must COVER the 5–50 mN detent window,
`bound: 'min'` at 50 mN, read as TODO 16's arithmetic (§70 step 2 allows forces
so), from the §137 lay-shaft row, which is itself asserted against TODO 82's
posed probe. It reads 1348.7 mN. The drive reference grew `colH` (the column
tier, measured off the castellations' z extent) for the seat to read.

Every row is measured at `alarmToggle`'s two states off world-box centres of
centrally symmetric meshes, which in 3D as in plan are exact at any rotation.
The shaft's axis is the line through two hanger bushes, which are rings on it,
and the stall is the one row read from a computed record. **The control moves
metal**: the selector rod is carried 0.05 out along the lever's line, the
measured run must follow it to 1e-9, and the rod goes back.

**What the reference found, all three fixed in this landing:**

- **The seat solve was discontinuous in its input.** The bar-lift sweep's grid
  puts a sample exactly at the nose's landing radius (x = beakLen), and whether
  `x > beakLen` kept it was a float rounding. The build's run,
  8.999999999999998 − landR, dropped it (lift 0.2687); the reference evaluating
  the same law at a run of exactly 9 kept it (lift 0.2983). That is one lever
  with two answers 0.03 apart. The sample is now excluded by a margin, which is
  what the build had landed on and what §172 says: the bar clears the columns,
  and only the nose reads them. The metal did not move.
- **The rod's stroke was a rounded quotation.** `ALARM_LINK_ROD_TRAVEL_SPEC` was
  0.10020, while the rim finger's law gives 0.1001979 exactly (the registration
  solve measures the same to 4e-15). The beak's seat was solved on the
  rounding, while the tick tilts the lever by the rod's real stroke. So the nose
  stopped **1.27e-5 above the gap floor** it is declared to stand on, inside the
  1e-4 the boot assert allowed. The spec is the law's own answer now. The beak's
  arms moved by 1.2e-5 to re-seat it, and the two asserts that judge the pair are
  tightened from 1e-3 and 1e-4 to 1e-9 and 1e-6.
- **TODO 174's fork record held its ratio "HELD" and computed its reference
  with the fork's own ratio.** That was true until TODO 206 made the seat a
  contact whose corner dip scales with the arm. Since then the ratio is solved
  per run, so forking the run moves it: 5.791 at wr 16, 5.311 at 9. The
  record's reference columns now come from the law at the reference run. Its
  first assert compared the fork's ratio with itself and could not fire, so it
  is gone. The fork itself now lives where the battery holds it.

`probe-70-line-spec.mjs` grew to eleven trees. The six new ones move only the
declaration, never the metal:

- deleting the fork fails exactly the six rows it carried;
- mis-declaring the run (9.5) fails the run and the three rows that derive from
  it;
- forking to the reference's own 16 fails them and reports the fork STALE;
- forking a law, forking the stall envelope, or forking with no `forcedBy` each
  come back MALFORMED, on the row named.

### TODO 222 — the selector link restated for the sideways drive

TODO 222 (the owner's "Full D") replaced the selector ring's axial drive. In the
axial drive the centre pin lifted the ring itself. Now the pin crosses the lay
shaft's DEAD CENTRE at mid-stroke and pushes the fork's walls sideways, and the
ring, guided on three helices, rises as it turns. Two of the reference's rows
described the axial drive, and a fork could not carry them: a fork moves an
input, never a law. The owner chose to restate the reference instead.

- **`pinD` and `pinArm` are gone.** The groove's offset over the axis no longer
  means anything: the pin rests half a stroke off dead centre, whatever the
  groove's height. The arm that spent the span on the ring's travel is no law of
  this drive, because any arm turns the ring through its whole lift once the
  helix is cut to its throw.
- **In their place:** `phase` (0: the mid-stroke arm stands square to the shaft
  along the vertical) and `arm`. The arm is a law, `L.arm(span, travel)`, the
  minimiser of what follower B's lift costs the column at the nose,
  W(α)·(1 + μ·r_J/(r·cos(S/2))), taken in the line at the tab's design reach. It
  reads 1.1306, where the axial drive's arm had been 0.5498. Then three more
  laws: `throw` = 2r·sin(S/2), `turn` = throw / r_eff and `helixTan` = travel /
  (R_helix·turn). One more input, `rEff`, is the distance from the ring's axis
  to the roller's line of push: 5.4 in the reference, 4.70697 as built, forked
  by the §112 site solve. `helixR` is `ALARM_SEL_HELIX_R`.
- **Measured off the posed metal.** The pin's arc gives the phase (its arms'
  bisector against the vertical, square to the shaft) and the throw (the chord).
  The roller's arc midpoint and that chord give r_eff. The ring's own x axis
  gives the turn, and one guide roller's radius and rise give the helix.

Restating the reference found two defects in the first landing's helix, fixed
with it: r_eff had been read at the pin's root rather than at the roller that
pushes, and by a one-sided difference 1.75e-4 short. The probe grew two trees
that corrupt the drive itself, the throw law and the arm hand-tuned past its
budget, and both fail the rows they should. TODO 82's probe now reads the pin
along the fork's wall normal; [TODO 237] files the reflection both stall paths
share.
