## §344 — The service-life view: the escape wheel's club tooth worn over a century of 5-yearly service, and the wear depth read radially

**The ask** (the owner, after TODO 299): show the escape wheel in its worn
shape, or better, model the wear over time — metal filings, grease
degradation. What shipped is a display-only page, `service-life.html`, that
draws the one wear the movement prices (TODO 299's Archard row on the club
tooth), magnified until it can be seen. The two other effects are named on the
page as not modelled, with the reason for each. And drawing it found a defect
in the record it draws, which is fixed here.

### Why a page, and not the 3D wheel

At true scale the century's worst case is 16.76 µm = 0.044 model units: under
the tooth's own 0.09 u edge bevel and inside the meshes' tessellation slack. TODO
299 landing 3 established that a bevelled extrude cannot carry a feature
smaller than its chamfer, and a mesh that changes with time would move the
geometry every gate in the battery measures. So the metal stays as cut and the
wear is drawn on its own page, at about ×40 (the field is 14 µm across).

### The page

- **The tooth tip, magnified**, drawn in the wheel's exact coordinates off the
  authored outline (`escapeWheel.userData.profile.poly`, the polygon TODO 296's
  wedge is read off): the sharp drawn point (dotted), the finished tip (an arc
  of TODO 299 landing 2's derived radius, 0.16 µm, inscribed in the outline's
  own two flanks), the tooth circle the stones are set to, the circle where the
  lock is down to its safety share (the allowance used up), and the worn
  surface at the chosen year for each of the three wear coefficients.
- **A year slider** (0–100, with the 20 services marked) and a play button.
  The readouts: services done, how far each tooth has slid (535 km over the
  century) and how many locks, the depth and its share of the allowance per
  case, the year each case runs out, and the steel each service cleans out
  (800 µm³ for the whole wheel per interval at the nominal rate, 6.3 ng, a
  speck 9.3 µm on a side).
- **A chart** of depth against years for the three cases, with the allowance
  line. The depth goes as √t: the volume Archard removes grows with the slip,
  and so with time, and each micrometre further down the wedge is wider.
- **The whole wheel at true scale**, with the tooth marked: the magnified field
  would be 1.2 px across on it.
- **What it does not claim**: the worn shape is the model's (the volume taken
  off as a cut square to the radius, the reading the allowance uses); oil
  ageing between services, debris left in the oil, the pallet stones' and the
  pivots' wear are not modelled; the page's time is a slider, not the
  movement's clock. The wear is MODELLED: the loads and slips are the
  escapement solve's, and K is a cited band.

**Every number is the build's.** The page fetches `src/service-life-data.json`,
GENERATED by `tools/service-life-data.mjs` from one native boot (§338): a copy
of the energy column's `escapeTooth` record and the outline, with no arithmetic
of its own, so `--check` means exactly "the build's record has not moved". The
tool refuses a loud boot, finds the wheel by its profile at the record's radius
(exactly one), holds the record's tip to a vertex of that outline, and counts
the teeth off it (the tips on the tooth circle) rather than typing 15. The page
applies only the record's own laws: depth ∝ √t, the year a case runs out as
T·(allowance/depth)², the volume per interval as V_life × teeth × interval/T.
TODO 279 (the gong bench's generated file went stale on `main` unseen) is why
this one is gated from its first day: `native-host.yml` runs `--check` on
x64 and arm64.

`tools/smoke-service-life.mjs` (Chromium, 1280 and 390 wide) holds:

- the finish the page DRAWS sets the tip back by exactly the cut-back the build
  recorded, which is where the page's geometry and the build's derivation could
  part. It reads 0.003499116531 u drawn against 0.00349911652 recorded; at 8
  significant digits the outline's rounding alone read 1e-5, so the data keeps
  12;
- the year-100 readouts are the record's recessions;
- the worst case runs out in the year the √t law puts it (58);
- the cases the record says hold, hold, and at year 0 nothing has worn;
- nothing scrolls sideways, and there are no page or console errors.

### And the record it draws was wrong: the depth was read along the bisector

A drawing has to put the worn surface somewhere, and the record allowed two
places. TODO 299 turned the worn-away area into a depth with √(A/tan a): the
depth along the wedge's BISECTOR, as for a wedge symmetric about the depth
direction. But the allowance it was compared with is RADIAL: `lockRunAt(dR)`
shortens the tooth circle. Both of the club's flanks lean the same way off the
radius (24° and 34.83°), so the bisector stands 29.41° off it, and for the same
area the bisector reading is 15% deeper than the radial one.

So the record now reads both depths radially:

- **The wear depth**: a cut square to the radius at depth d takes ½·d²·wpd of
  the wedge, wpd being the club's measured width per radial depth
  (`H.wedge.widthPerDepth`, already held to its flanks' difference). So
  d = √(2A/wpd), and the hardness each corner would need inverts that.
- **The finish's cut-back**: it is the arc's radial setback, read exactly. The
  inscribed arc's centre sits ρ/sin a in from the apex along the bisector, and
  its outermost point is |centre| + ρ from the wheel's axis. The bisector's
  angle off the radius is published on the wedge (`bisectorRad`, the flanks'
  mean).

| | before (bisector) | after (radial) |
|---|---|---|
| finish's cut-back | 1.55 µm | 1.33 µm |
| allowance for the century | 12.50 µm | 12.72 µm |
| best case (K 1e-8) | 1.93 µm, ×0.154 | 1.68 µm, ×0.132 |
| nominal (K 1e-7) | 6.09 µm, ×0.488 | 5.30 µm, ×0.417 |
| worst case (K 1e-6) | 19.27 µm, ×1.542 | 16.76 µm, ×1.318 |
| hardness to hold the worst case | 1552 HV | 1134 HV |

The conclusion does not move: the century holds at the best and nominal
corners and not at the worst, still past any steel. What moves is when the
worst case runs out: because the depth goes as √t, that is year 58, not the
year 42 the bisector reading gave. (In conversation it had been quoted as
"around year 65", a linear reading of the ×1.54; both are superseded.)

Held where the formula is re-derived:

- the `equalisation` gate (row 22's Archard identity and inverted hardness,
  row 21's cut-back identity, and a new clause holding `bisectorRad` to the
  flanks' mean);
- `probe-296-escape-tooth.mjs` (its own recession and W6 ceiling);
- `probe-296-tooth-gate.mjs`, with three new sabotages that restore each old
  reading whole and must be refused by the identity it moved. S16b is the
  bisector recession, S16c the bisector cut-back, S16d a bisector off its
  flanks' mean. It now refuses 24 of 24.

### What it leaves

- **Oil ageing** would move something the simulation really drives: friction
  rises, so the balance's swing falls across an interval and recovers at
  service. But the repo has no degradation curve to drive it with. Moving the
  `FRICTION` band from favourable to adverse across an interval would be a
  posed law, and would have to be labelled as one until a source is found.
  (Landed that way as §346: the oiled rows nominal → adverse, linear, posed.)
- **Debris** stays the service model's assumption (cleaned out at every
  service). Wear that compounds with debris would need a three-body law the
  repo does not have.
- The other members' lives (the pallet stones, the pivots) are TODO 299's own
  next landings.
