## §334 — The crown stands still while the watch runs: the keyless fusee's rocking bar, designed in a line — PARTIAL: landings 1 (the line spec), 2 (the metal), 3a (the bar's true centre distance), 3b (the fold's plan, red on corner K) and 3c (the two-corner setting traverse, designed in a line) shipped

### §334 landing 1 — why a one-way cannot do it, and the rocking bar in free space

**Why.** On a crown-wound watch the crown does not turn while the watch runs. On this movement it turns 4.2 times
backward over the 30 h reserve (TODO 318, measured for the roadmap entry by stepping `setPose({ tension })` from 1 to
0 and unwrapping the `windStem` mesh's world angle). Every winding wheel is posed from the banked wind, and the
`restoring` audit declares the keyless works `two-way`: the running fusee back-drives the winding train through its
spur. That is true of a KEY-wound fusee, whose winding square turns with the cone. A crown-wound one has to break
the path. The owner asked for the fix on 2026-11-18.

**The plan that was refuted.** The entry as filed (2026-11-05) proposed a second one-way in the winding train,
"placed so that winding drives through it and running overruns it". No one-way can be placed that way. The chain
pulls the fusee arbor in the pay-out sense always: while winding the crown overpowers it, and while running the
arbor turns that same way at the train's rate. So the torque at every mesh of the winding train has one sign in both
regimes, and only the motion reverses. A one-way that carries winding stops its crown side running ahead of its
fusee side in the winding sense, which is the same constraint as stopping the fusee side running ahead in the
pay-out sense, and that is exactly the running motion. So it locks and carries the crown wherever it sits: at the
stem (TODO 50's saw coupling, which the entry itself had found does this), at the transfer or crown wheel, or
between the spur and its arbor. A part that senses torque rather than motion fails for the same reason: the torque
does not change sign. The probe below measures that last point (RB5).

**What a keyless fusee does.** It disengages. Search snippets read on 2026-11-18 (the pages themselves were not
reachable from that session; TODO 318 quotes them and they still owe a full read) describe the winding wheels of a
keyless fusee carried on a rocking bar, "held in a neutral position by a spring", and say that keyless fusees "need
a disengagement device". The owner chose the rocking bar over a separate winding position for the crown, on
2026-11-18.

**What landing 1 builds: the bar, in a line.** `src/keyless-rocking-bar.js` is pure and nothing in the movement
imports it. It is CLAUDE.md's "design in a line": a driver D (the transfer wheel, 20 teeth), the wheels the bar
carries (the keyless family's 18-tooth idler, one or two of them), and a follower F (the fusee's 24-tooth winding
spur) on a straight centre line. The fold into the movement is a later landing.

- **Geometry.** The bar turns about D's axis, so its first wheel stays in mesh with D at every angle. Its last wheel
  meets F at full depth at the ENGAGED angle, where a depth banking stops it. The NEUTRAL angle is the nearest one
  beyond it at which the tips stand `CLEAR_MARGIN` apart: a 4.25° swing for the one-wheel bar and 5.02° for the
  two-wheel bar, at the default build's transfer-to-spur distance. The two-wheel bar's bend between its arms is the
  one that keeps its non-meshing members farthest apart (85.3°, worst clearance 2.28 u).
- **The carry.** A friction spring between D's arbor and the bar turns the bar whichever way D turns relative to it.
  The bar's side of the centre line is chosen so that turning the crown to wind carries it from neutral into mesh.
  Released, the crown side is back-driven the other way and the same friction carries the bar out.
- **The teeth.** Under winding load the meshes press the bar onto its depth banking, at every pressure-angle corner
  of the cycloidal faces (k from 0.70 to 1.57 per unit torque at D for one wheel, 1.13 to 2.29 for two). On the
  mirrored side they would kick it out, which is the control.
- **The sense at the crown.** Every mesh reverses. The default build meshes the transfer straight onto the spur (one
  mesh), so only the two-wheel bar (three meshes) keeps the crown's winding direction. The one-wheel bar suits the
  builds whose routing already carries an idler. The idlers cancel from the 20:24 ratio either way.
- **The spring's window**, in TODO 16's format, on the winding torque read off the default build's record
  (`GOING_POWER.fuseeTorque_Nm`, 1.7603 N·mm at the fusee arbor; 1.467 N·mm at D):
  - the return spring must beat the bar's own weight at its worst attitude;
  - it must lose to the friction carry, or turning the crown cannot carry the bar in;
  - the carry must cost no more than one more plain pivot under the winding load would (`FRICTION.muPlain`, adverse
    corner).

  Each sits at the ratio middle of its window, so the margins are equal ratios: weight 0.0003, spring 0.0017, carry
  0.0102, ceiling 0.0604 N·mm for one wheel, each ×5.9; for two wheels 0.0007 / 0.0032 / 0.0138 / 0.0604, each ×4.4.
- **Release.** The bar lets go of any crown-side drag up to about 0.0075 N·mm at D, 0.5% of the winding torque there.
  That is REPORTED, not gated: the movement models the crown side's friction only as load-proportional, and no
  load-independent drag (a case-tube gasket, a finger resting on the crown).

**Instrument.** `tools/probe-keyless-rocking-bar.mjs` (acceptance, pure Node through the native loader) holds every
row for both variants, each rule with a control that must fail: full depth and the neutral margin (half the swing
is short), the two-wheel bar's clearances (the straight bar fouls), the carry's direction and the teeth's (the
mirrored bar), the same force system winding and back-driven (reversing the torque reverses it), sense and ratio,
the window (a bar thick enough to outweigh its span has none), and a fork of the centre distance re-deriving every
row (out of reach, the bar refuses).

**What this landing does not claim.** It is MODELLED, not simulated: the bar is placed at its two bankings and
nothing steps it. The bar's own section is a placeholder plate in the weight row only, and the two springs are
moments, not cut metal (P1 is the next landing's). Tip-on-tip arrival as the last wheel swings in, and shock, are
not covered. The crown still turns in the watch: TODO 318 stays open until the fold lands.

### §334 landing 2 — the bar as metal, still in the line (P1)

**Why.** Landing 1's bar was moments and placeholder plate. CLAUDE.md's P1 asks that every member can do the job as
matter, its section derived from its load path, before anything is folded. The owner asked for this step on
2026-11-19.

**The load.** Every part is sized to the torque at the transfer wheel while winding, the fusee's (1.7603 N·mm)
through the bar's 20:24, lossless: 1.467 N·mm. It is the load the watch already sizes its winding train to (the
geared stop-work takes the fusee's torque as its stall). What a hand can add at the winding stop is declared nowhere
in the movement, and is not priced here either.

**What is cut, and from what.**
- **The bar.** Its in-plane width solves σ = 6M/(t·w²) + N/(t·w) at `SPRING_SIGMA_Y_PA`, with M and N the worst
  bending moment and push along its centre line at any pressure-angle corner. It is floored at §50. One wheel:
  120 µm, the floor (the banking at the wheel leaves the arm in pure push, 42 MPa). Two wheels: 349 µm, load-governed,
  at 800 MPa.
- **The pins.** Each carried wheel turns on a pin in the bar sized by TODO 193's pivot law, loaded at the wheel's
  mid-plane: 222 µm for one wheel; 204 and 233 µm for two.
- **The bankings: one pin.** The last wheel's arbor runs on through the bar into an arc slot in the plate about the
  transfer arbor. The slot's two ends are the depth and neutral bankings, so the angle between them is the swing by
  construction (182 and 308 µm of travel), and the last pin also carries the banking's push (0.94 and 0.96 N).
- **The return blade.** A uniform cantilever of the ribbons' alloy (`MAINSPRING_E_PA`, held to
  `MAINSPRING_SIGMA_Y_PA`), as thin as the movement's flat-blade floor (`SPRING_FLAT_U`, 0.05 mm, which every blade
  here is cut to). It is as long as the bar reaches, and bears on the bar's own boss beside the pivot. It works at
  130 and 175 MPa against 1,800.
- **The friction carry.** A two-jaw clip in the bar's boss gripping the transfer arbor at the bore. Its grip
  (0.29 and 0.35 N) gives the carry at BOTH ends of `FRICTION.muPlain`'s band, and each jaw is as thin as its own
  grip lets it work under the alloy's low end.

**The window, re-solved with the metal.** The chain runs: the bar's weight < the spring at neutral < the spring at
engaged < the carry at the band's low corner < the carry at its high corner < the carry's ceiling. The spring's rise
and the band's spread (×1.67) are fixed ratios inside it, and the three margins left are made equal: ×3.94 for one
wheel, ×3.21 for two. The weight now comes from the cut metal, not the placeholder plate.

**The first draft closed the window, and is kept as the control.** It bore the blade on the first wheel's boss, cut
at §50's plate floor (0.12 mm). Swinging the bar in bent it so far that its push at full mesh rose 7,400 to 15,900
times over its preload, past anything the carry could beat: q 0.20 and 0.21, a bar that would never engage. A spring
whose moment rises by k·swing·r² wants the smallest lever and the softest blade: the boss edge (0.39 and 0.61 mm
from the pivot) and the flat-blade floor, as long as the bar reaches. A second defect was caught the same way: the
bar's width solved from bending alone left the axial push to carry the two-wheel bar to 811 MPa, over its 800. The
width is now the root of both together.

**Tip on tip.** If the last wheel's tips land on the spur's as the bar swings in, the wheel is still being turned (the
transfer drives it while the crown winds), so it slides off within one pitch. That needs the winding torque to beat
the tips' friction under the carry's push. It does so 820 and 780 times over, and costs at worst 36° more turn of the
crown before the wheel drops in.

**Instrument.** `tools/probe-keyless-rocking-bar.mjs` gains RB10–RB15, and RB7 is re-cut to the new chain:
- RB10: the bar's section; its control, a thousandfold load, must be load-governed at exactly yield;
- RB11: the pins, the same control;
- RB12: the blade's preload and rate against the window's spring at both bankings, as rate × deflection × lever,
  under the alloy;
- RB13: the clip at both ends of the band, under the alloy;
- RB14: tip on tip;
- RB15: the slot.

RB7's control is the first draft, which must have no window.

**What this does not claim.** Modelled, not simulated: the bar is placed at its two bankings and nothing steps it.
The clip grips through about 6 µm of interference a jaw (5.5 and 6.8). That is tight to hold, and is REPORTED, not
judged, for want of a tolerance in the repository to judge it by; a longer jaw would ease it, and is the fold's to
find. Shock is not covered. The crown still turns in the watch: TODO 318 stays open until the fold lands.

### §334 landing 3a — the transfer stands clear of the spur, and the stratum the fold has to find

**A defect in landings 1 and 2.** Both took the default build's transfer-to-spur distance, module·(20 + 24)/2 =
7.48 u. That is the DIRECT routing's: at it the transfer and the spur still mesh (their tips 0.47 u into each other),
so the bar would be bypassed and the crown would go on turning. `pairClear` skipped that pair as "fixed by the
routing", which is how it passed. The centre distance is now DERIVED: the nearest the transfer can stand to the spur
with their tips `CLEAR_MARGIN` apart, 8.10 u, so the fold moves the transfer (and the crown wheel on its arbor) 0.62 u
(0.24 mm) out along the stem. RB16 holds it, its control the direct distance. Every row re-derives: the two-wheel
bar swings 4.68°, margins ×3.27.

**What a survey of the default build found** (a report, by native boot, 2026-11-19):
- **Winding sense:** winding turns the transfer clockwise seen from the dial's far side (−z), so the bar sits on the
  line frame's mirrored side.
- **Plan:** in plan the bar's wheels, at both bankings, lie within 14 u of the fusee axis.
- **Height:** the winding band they must share with the spur is 0.875 u tall: the plate's top below, the great wheel
  (radius 15.6 u, z 0.95–3.05) directly above. The bar's own plate (0.725 u) has no stratum there: not beside its
  wheels, and not above them, because the great wheel is.

A first pass that tested mesh VERTICES against the bar's footprint missed the great wheel entirely (its vertices sit
at its hub and rim), the trap `.claude/skills/instruments` names; the radius test about the fusee axis is the one to
trust. So the fold needs a new stratum for the bar, which is a layout decision put to the owner.

### §334 landing 3b — the fold's plan, and the corner it could not clear (its z frame corrected under landing 3c)

**What is built.** `foldRockingBar` in `src/keyless-rocking-bar.js` places the bar in the movement:

- **The pocket** is a set of convex pieces: each member over the whole swing, grown by `CLEAR_MARGIN`, with its floor
  the bar's stock and a margin below the plate's top face.
- **Arms** are bowed round any opening their pocket would crowd.

`tools/probe-keyless-rocking-bar-fold.mjs` holds the plan to the boot:

  openings' true outlines.

- F3: no metal in the pocket's volume.
- F4: the wheels `CLEAR_MARGIN` off everything over the swing.
- F5: the moved transfer clear of the band.

F3–F5 are exact mesh distances (`meshClearance`), each with a control.

**A correction to landing 2.** The return blade cannot bear on the boss's round edge: there its push is radial and
turns nothing. It presses a PIN set in the boss's edge, and lies along the radius through that pin; where round the
boss the pin goes is free, and the plan takes the azimuth whose blade stands farthest from everything else.

**Why the probe is red.** The motion works' corner fold rises through the plate at (−19.25, 11.95), under the place

### §334 landing 3c (re-filed) — the two-corner setting traverse, designed in a line

**What is built.** `src/setting-traverse-line.js` (pure, imported by nothing) designs the traverse A → K1 → K2 → B
that takes the motion works' corner K out from under the rocking bar. `tools/probe-setting-traverse-line.mjs` reads its

  `STOCK_MIN_U`, else the least whose cut blanks do). The opening is `foldOpeningFor`'s (the blanks' footprint above

  publishes `FOLD_DEFLECT_MIN` in its fold record, and the probe re-derives it by the module's own law.
- **Legs.** Each leg is turnable (L/D at most `TURN_LD_TARGET`) and borne by two blocks under TODO 289's law

### Landing 5b — the crown stands still while the watch runs

**The law.** The winding train is two sides now, and the bar is the joint between them.
- **The crown side** (stem, winding pinion, crown wheel, the bar's driver) poses from `barrelWindTurns + kbLead`. The
  spur side (follower, transfer wheel) poses from the bank.
- **While the bar is OUT** (the running watch) the run-down moves the spur side only. `kbLead` grows by exactly what
  drained, and nothing on the crown side moves, not even the stem clutch's parked gap.
- **Winding swings the bar IN.** The carried wheel can only drop into the follower at a whole follower tooth
  (`KB_PITCH_TURNS`, 1/9 of a follower turn, 0.0926 barrel turns), so the first crown input turns the crown side
  alone, up to that tooth. While it does, the carried wheel rides the follower's tips and the bar is shown out, never
  at full depth through teeth it has not met. Then the bar drops in and the bank follows turn for turn, the lead held
  at a whole tooth.
- **The bar, its pin and the carried wheel** swing as one group about the driver's axis, through the line's own
  engaged and neutral angles. The carried wheel rolls on the driver as it goes: in the bar's frame its angle is
  (nD/nC)·(ψ − θ_D) past its index.
- **The run-down is one function** (`drainBarrelBy`): the live loop calls it, and so does `__clock.runDown`, which runs
  hours of reserve down without stepping the balance at its own rate.
- **Persisted:** `kbLead`, because it is where the crown stands and no pose re-derives it.

**The swing is MODELLED, not simulated.** The bar is in on a tick whose crown input reaches the follower and out on
every other tick, quasi-static (the real swing takes milliseconds against a frame). No force moves it: its return
blade, its banking and its friction clip are not cut yet, which is landing 5c. The line's blade lies along the boss's
radius in the bar's own plane, the line's banking is the carried wheel's pin in a slot under the bar, which is where
the stem passes, and the clip's jaws are the bar's own stock high around an arbor with no free height. Each needs its
own placement search against the movement.

**The instruments.**
- **A conditional mesh.** A declared mesh row may name the state it holds in (`engagedBy: 'kbSwing'` on the carried
  wheel ⇄ follower). `measureMeshNow` reports such a mesh NOT ENGAGED outside that state. `meshPhase` judges it only
  where it meshes, and fails a row no pose puts in mesh.
- **Both bar meshes are driven by `wind` alone.** The `wind` axis poses the bar in. The `reserve` axis holds the crown
  side still, as a running watch does: its lead grows by what drained.
- **A new axis, `barSwing`,** cycles the bar out → in → out at half wind, so the sweeps see its path.
