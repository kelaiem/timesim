## §334 — The crown stands still while the watch runs: the keyless fusee's rocking bar, designed in a line — PARTIAL: landing 1 (the line spec) shipped

### §334 landing 1 — why a one-way cannot do it, and the rocking bar in free space

**Why.** On a crown-wound watch the crown does not turn while the watch runs. On this movement it turns 4.2 times
backward over the 30 h reserve (TODO 318, measured for the roadmap entry by stepping `setPose({ tension })` from 1 to
0 and unwrapping the `windStem` mesh's world angle). Every winding wheel is posed from the banked wind, and the
`restoring` audit declares the keyless works `two-way`: the running fusee back-drives the winding train through its
spur. That is true of a KEY-wound fusee, whose winding square turns with the cone. A crown-wound one has to break
the path. The owner asked for the fix on 2026-11-18.

**The plan that was refuted.** The entry as filed (2026-11-05) proposed a second one-way in the winding train,
"placed so that winding drives through it and running overruns it". No one-way can be placed that way. The chain
pulls the fusee arbor in the pay-out sense always: while winding the crown overpowers it, and while running the
arbor turns that same way at the train's rate. So the torque at every mesh of the winding train has one sign in both
regimes, and only the motion reverses. A one-way that carries winding stops its crown side running ahead of its
fusee side in the winding sense, which is the same constraint as stopping the fusee side running ahead in the
pay-out sense, and that is exactly the running motion. So it locks and carries the crown wherever it sits: at the
stem (TODO 50's saw coupling, which the entry itself had found does this), at the transfer or crown wheel, or
between the spur and its arbor. A part that senses torque rather than motion fails for the same reason: the torque
does not change sign. The probe below measures that last point (RB5).

**What a keyless fusee does.** It disengages. Search snippets read on 2026-11-18 (the pages themselves were not
reachable from that session; TODO 318 quotes them and they still owe a full read) describe the winding wheels of a
keyless fusee carried on a rocking bar, "held in a neutral position by a spring", and say that keyless fusees "need
a disengagement device". The owner chose the rocking bar over a separate winding position for the crown, on
2026-11-18.

**What landing 1 builds: the bar, in a line.** `src/keyless-rocking-bar.js` is pure and nothing in the movement
imports it. It is CLAUDE.md's "design in a line": a driver D (the transfer wheel, 20 teeth), the wheels the bar
carries (the keyless family's 18-tooth idler, one or two of them), and a follower F (the fusee's 24-tooth winding
spur) on a straight centre line. The fold into the movement is a later landing.

- **Geometry.** The bar turns about D's axis, so its first wheel stays in mesh with D at every angle. Its last wheel
  meets F at full depth at the ENGAGED angle, where a depth banking stops it. The NEUTRAL angle is the nearest one
  beyond it at which the tips stand `CLEAR_MARGIN` apart: a 4.25° swing for the one-wheel bar and 5.02° for the
  two-wheel bar, at the default build's transfer-to-spur distance. The two-wheel bar's bend between its arms is the
  one that keeps its non-meshing members farthest apart (85.3°, worst clearance 2.28 u).
- **The carry.** A friction spring between D's arbor and the bar turns the bar whichever way D turns relative to it.
  The bar's side of the centre line is chosen so that turning the crown to wind carries it from neutral into mesh.
  Released, the crown side is back-driven the other way and the same friction carries the bar out.
- **The teeth.** Under winding load the meshes press the bar onto its depth banking, at every pressure-angle corner
  of the cycloidal faces (k from 0.70 to 1.57 per unit torque at D for one wheel, 1.13 to 2.29 for two). On the
  mirrored side they would kick it out, which is the control.
- **The sense at the crown.** Every mesh reverses. The default build meshes the transfer straight onto the spur (one
  mesh), so only the two-wheel bar (three meshes) keeps the crown's winding direction. The one-wheel bar suits the
  builds whose routing already carries an idler. The idlers cancel from the 20:24 ratio either way.
- **The spring's window**, in TODO 16's format, on the winding torque read off the default build's record
  (`GOING_POWER.fuseeTorque_Nm`, 1.7603 N·mm at the fusee arbor; 1.467 N·mm at D):
  - the return spring must beat the bar's own weight at its worst attitude;
  - it must lose to the friction carry, or turning the crown cannot carry the bar in;
  - the carry must cost no more than one more plain pivot under the winding load would (`FRICTION.muPlain`, adverse
    corner).

  Each sits at the ratio middle of its window, so the margins are equal ratios: weight 0.0003, spring 0.0017, carry
  0.0102, ceiling 0.0604 N·mm for one wheel, each ×5.9; for two wheels 0.0007 / 0.0032 / 0.0138 / 0.0604, each ×4.4.
- **Release.** The bar lets go of any crown-side drag up to about 0.0075 N·mm at D, 0.5% of the winding torque there.
  That is REPORTED, not gated: the movement models the crown side's friction only as load-proportional, and no
  load-independent drag (a case-tube gasket, a finger resting on the crown).

**Instrument.** `tools/probe-keyless-rocking-bar.mjs` (acceptance, pure Node through the native loader) holds every
row for both variants, each rule with a control that must fail: full depth and the neutral margin (half the swing
is short), the two-wheel bar's clearances (the straight bar fouls), the carry's direction and the teeth's (the
mirrored bar), the same force system winding and back-driven (reversing the torque reverses it), sense and ratio,
the window (a bar thick enough to outweigh its span has none), and a fork of the centre distance re-deriving every
row (out of reach, the bar refuses).

**What this landing does not claim.** It is MODELLED, not simulated: the bar is placed at its two bankings and
nothing steps it. The bar's own section is a placeholder plate in the weight row only, and the two springs are
moments, not cut metal (P1 is the next landing's). Tip-on-tip arrival as the last wheel swings in, and shock, are
not covered. The crown still turns in the watch: TODO 318 stays open until the fold lands.
