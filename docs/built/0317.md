## §317 — The fitted tonneau: a case archetype derived from the solved tonneau, form-fitted to least plan area and least interior volume

Out of the owner's request, in their words: "come up with a new case archetype derived from a solved tonneau
that is then form fitted to minimize the area and volume inside the case", and their follow-up mid-landing: "I'm
assuming the tonneau is the smallest area but we can also try to fit other shapes right?" (answered below, by
measurement: it is not, and the answer is the table). `?case=fitted` is the fifth kind of §308's shape library,
"Fitted tonneau" in both case-shape selects. The round build does not move: fingerprint 566656213 (64 units,
13 poses) on a virgin boot of the base tree and of this one, both boots silent.

### The shape (`caseShapeOf('fitted', box, { fitted: { k, tau } })`, layout.js)

The tonneau's construction with the two freedoms the barrel holds fixed set free. Each long side is the circle
tangent to x = cx ± a at its APEX (cx ± a, yA); `k = a/R` is its curvature against the half-width (the
tonneau's is 2·bow/(bow² + (b/a)²), so the barrel's 0.12 bow is one value of it), and `tau` puts the apex
tau·b above the box's centre (the tonneau's is 0). With tau ≠ 0 the two straight ends cut the side circle at
different heights and the barrel TAPERS, the end nearer the apex the wider. Both ends stay straight, each at its
own bound (the box's yMin/yMax carry them independently), because the flat lugs weld to them; corners round
tangent to side and end at the rim margin, the tonneau's rule. It is exact (arcs and segments), mirror-symmetric
about x = cx (the hands' line: the strap is centred on it and the dial's Y-flip maps the shape onto itself),
and closed under offsetting like the tonneau — grow g adds g to R, to the corner round and to both ends and
keeps every centre, k and tau re-expressed on the grown box. Under `?formrect=` the fitted kind carries SIX
numbers, the bounds then k and tau (`formShapeOf`/`formShapeOpts` read and re-make it; `FORM_KINDS` is now the
one list of case kinds).

Held by `tools/probe-317-fitted-case.mjs` S1–S3: at the tonneau's curvature and tau 0 every ray's exit equals
the tonneau's to 5.7e-14 (control: tau 0.2 moves it 2.36 u); every vertex of the shape grown g reads inset −g to
6.5e-14 and the loop stays convex; a tau tapers it.

### The area: the fit (`fitFittedCase`, layout.js; `FORM_FOOTPRINT`, main.js)

"Derived from a solved tonneau" is literal. The solve starts where §308 stops — `fitCaseShape('tonneau', …)`
about the hands, the barrel at TONNEAU_BOW and the metal box's aspect, scaled until every hull vertex stands the
rim margin inside — and then frees what that construction typed: the half-width, each end's bound, k and the
apex height. The objective is the plan AREA (`shapeArea`, exact off the primitives: ½∮(x dy − y dx)). For a
given (k, yA) the three bounds are TIGHTENED, each bisected to its least feasible value with the others held
(each bound's feasibility is monotone), and (k, yA) are searched NESTED: for a given k, a golden-section search
over the apex height; over k, a 13-step geometric scan from a quarter to four times the tonneau's curvature, then
golden-section in the best bracket. It was a compass search on the two axes first, and on §314's arrangement that
stalled at 1074.19 mm²: the tightened area over (k, yA) is a V-shaped VALLEY on a DIAGONAL (a larger curvature
wants a lower apex; across it two hull vertices trade the binding), and steps along the axes cannot follow its
floor. Measured, a 9 × 9 grid read 1067.3 at (0.5, 5) beside the solve's 1074.2; the nested search finds
1063.02. Two constraints, both laws the tonneau was already held to:

- every hull vertex of the counted metal stands ≥ PLATE_RIM_MARGIN inside (exact on the hull, the kind being
  convex);
- each straight end runs ≥ the lug pair's outboard reach either side of the centreline — makeFormCase's own
  `need`, (CASE_LUG_INNER + CASE_LUG_T)/2 + CASE_LUG_T/2 = 11.000 mm — so a taper that narrowed an end under its
  lugs is refused in the solve rather than warned at the build. **It binds**: the fitted 6-o'clock end runs
  11.000009 mm.

The x centre stays on the hands; the ends do not, so the hands may stand off the 12–6 middle by what the metal
asks — 0.088 mm here. The search is a generator (`fitFittedCaseSteps`, §266's shape for one call longer than the
boot's budget: ~1,000 evaluations, ~1.9 s here) that `FORM_FOOTPRINT` resumes between its evaluations, handing the thread
back on `breathe()`'s own 40 ms budget and keeping its held-time mark — but through no NUMBERED seam: the boot
bar's table (§267) is measured on the default build, which never reaches it, and `boot-progress.mjs --write` was
refused on this landing's host twice by an existing seam whose visits are timed (337: 80/82, then 83/84/80), so
the table is untouched and `--check` passes. Each probe of feasibility asks the vertex that refused last time
first.

**Is the tonneau the smallest? No — and once freed, the fitted tonneau is, among cases whose lugs weld flat.**
Every kind on the same measured hull (tools/data/probe-317-hull.json: the fitted build at its seated default on
§318's movement), hands centred across the width; the freed rows were measured with a tighten-and-compass law
on each kind's own parameters (a report, not the probe):

| shape | plan area | its ends carry flat lugs |
|---|---|---|
| rect, §308 fit | 1162.1 mm² | yes |
| tonneau, §308 fit | 1124.4 mm² | yes |
| rect, its corner radius freed, lugs held | 1124.8 mm² | yes (the corner may not eat the ends) |
| rect, corner freed, lugs NOT held | 1094.5 mm² | no — its 22.9 u corners leave 8.7 mm of straight end for a 22 mm lug pair |
| cushion, §308 fit | 1109.5 mm² | no — the curved end, §308's open finding |
| cushion, its exponent freed (n ≈ 4.1) | 1092.0 mm² | no |
| **fitted tonneau** | **1068.4 mm²** (34.65 × 33.45 mm) | **yes** |
| the hull grown by the rim margin | 923.6 mm² | — (the bound: no convex case is smaller) |

The plain cushion already beats the plain tonneau. With every kind's own parameters freed the fitted tonneau is
the least of them all — 5.0% under the tonneau it started from and the best alternative whose ends stay flat,
2.2% under the freed cushion, which has none. (On §314's movement, before §318 re-seated the gongs: 1063.0 mm²,
5.5% under the tonneau.) (On the movement before §314 the order was the same but for a
near-stadium rect, lugs dropped, that tied it to 1.2 mm²: the measured price of the lugs there.) What remains to
the bound is the convex hull's own corners, which a case with straight lug ends cannot follow. The probe gates
F1–F6: the margin held, the lugs held, the fit TIGHT (each bound pulled in 0.01 u refuses the metal — F1's
control), a local minimum (k and yA nudged and re-tightened enclose ≥ 0.9 mm² more), the ranking against the
tonneau and every §308 fit, and the fit GLOBAL over a 25 × 25 grid of (k, yA) whose own best (1070.74 on this hull) must beat
the stalled compass's 1074.19 — the control that the grid can see the defect it exists for.

### The volume: the fitted back (`solveFittedBack`, layout.js; `FITTED_BACK`, main.js; makeFormCase)

**Retired by §321.** The owner removed the stepped back for cost and complexity, and because the islands crowded the view. The fitted case's back is flat again, as every form case's is: 11.9624 mm deep, over the cap, with §261's warning back. `solveFittedBack` and the grid it read are deleted. What follows is what §317 shipped.

A §307 form back is one flat pane one margin over the TALLEST metal anywhere in the plan, so every square
millimetre of the case was as deep as the alarm tower: 11.9624 mm cased, over CASE_HEIGHT_MAX_MM — §308's open
"form case too deep" finding. The round case has, since §187, a raised glass STEP over the tall metal only. The
fitted back is that step on a form: the pane at a LOW level over the whole bore and raised panes over ISLANDS of
tall metal, the level chosen to make the interior volume least —

  V(T) = A_bore · (zLow − zFront) + Σ A_island · (zHigh − zLow),   zLow = T + CLEAR_MARGIN + float guard

over every distinct score T of the metal (z plus its unit's swept allowance, the back envelope's own score, to
0.01 u). The flat back is the first candidate, so the answer is never worse than it. The scores come off the
SAME walk as `BACK_ENVELOPE`'s bins, kept on an XY grid of one CLEAR_MARGIN a cell on the fitted build only; each
island is the convex hull of its samples grown by the margin plus the cell's half-diagonal, two samples closer
than two walls and the margin are one island (stamped on a raster, so linear in the samples), and islands whose
hulls come that close are merged. A pane under the pusher window's top plus the margin (§187's own assert,
stated as a floor) and an island whose outer wall does not stand a margin inside the back's window are refused.

The profile that decides it, measured on the fitted build before §314: above 10.5 u only **3.6 mm²** of metal stands (the
alarm striking wheel, the hammer, the fusee's top); above 10.0 u, 235 mm² — the three-quarter plate's top is at
10.31. **The declared swept rows were the trap.** `BACK_SWEPT_REGIONS` are radial bands with no azimuth; read in
plan, the hammer's row is a full ring at r 45.4 that covers the movement, and the first solve could step only
0.2 mm² for a 1.1% saving. A row is carried instead over its OWN UNIT's plan, which is that unit's swept plan
for both rows the table holds: the striking wheel turns on its own arbor, and the hammer's row is its own
build-pose top (§198: every hammer bin reads Δ 0). Answer on the fitted build at its default (§318's movement):

| | flat back (§307) | fitted back |
|---|---|---|
| pane (inner face) | 12.191 u | 10.464 u (0.65 mm lower) |
| raised islands | — | 2, 165.9 mm² together, inner face 12.191 u |
| interior volume | 11,831 mm³ | 11,159 mm³ (−5.7%) |
| §261 cased depth | 11.9624 mm, over the 11.367 cap | 11.3624 mm, under it (the warning is gone) |

makeFormCase builds it as the round case's box-stepped glass on the form: the pane holed by each island's outer
wall, each island's wall one glass thick up to the raised pane, and the raised pane over it (a step lower than the
glass is thick would be a pocket under a flush face; the top is the higher of the two). §187's one round step and
its two asserts, and §198's skirt assert (the form case builds no skirt — §307's rule for the skirt's other
asserts), no longer read the fitted build.

### The gong seat

`tools/gong-seats.mjs` takes the fitted kind (`FORM_KINDS`): the shape numbers ride every URL and every shape it
builds, taken from the kind's first fit. Its fixed point compared BOXES, and a fitted re-fit can keep its box
while k and tau move — measured: the seated hammer moved them 0.5873 → 0.5796 inside an unchanged box, the
first run closed on that, and the seated build then warned its metal 4.776 u inside its cut. So the build's own
reading (`formFootprint.margin`) now decides whether a fitted cut holds, and a re-fit that does not carries its
shape numbers into the next round. The candidate seats one run refused carried §198's skirt assert ("the ring is
paying for case thickness of its own"), which reads the round case's threaded-ring skirt; the form case builds
none, and it now holds the round build only (§307's rule for the skirt's other asserts — the fitted back is the
first form back low enough for that band to read the hammer).

**The fitted tonneau takes the BAR** (the owner's call when it was measured on §314's arrangement). §314 made the
spiral every form case's default seat; for the fitted outline the tool finds NO spiral seat the lever reaches —
at the fit, and with the cut stretched 4 and 8 u lug to lug. So the default is read off the generated table
(`defaultSeatMode`, src/case-shape.js): the spiral where one closed, else the bar, for the build, both
case-shape reloads, the bench and probe-form-track alike; the tool writes a kind's rows when either closes and
fails only when neither does.

**A fitted cut GROWS by being re-solved, never by scaling its box.** The seat tool held a seat's wire by scaling
the cut's box about its centre and, when the seated metal outgrew the cut, cut the UNION of the two boxes. For a
box-defined shape that is the whole story; for the fitted tonneau a box at fixed shape numbers is a different
outline, and measured it was a worse one twice over: the stretched cut's −y end fell 1.700 u off its lug plane,
and the union of a stretched y with the seated re-fit's curvature stood the case 40.59 mm wide. On §314's
movement the bar closed anyway at a cut 11 mm² over the solve's; on §318's it did not close at all. Now
`fitFittedCase` takes PINS — points held at their own margin — and the tool re-solves the fitted shape about the
seated metal's hull (`formFootprint.fitted` publishes it with the lug law, `FITTED_FLAT_MIN`, and the hands) with
the wire's stations pinned at the wire's need; a fitted re-fit that does not hold its cut is the next cut itself,
not a union. On §318's movement the bar — an arc of radius 60 u, the hammer pivot moved 2.68 u — closes at
`-45.72,45.72,-43.91,44.38,0.445876,0.162288`, **1068.5 mm² against the seated metal's own least fit of 1068.4**:
the gong now costs the outline only the spec's rounding. Booted there (no `?gong=`), the build takes the bar and
prints ONE warning, §186's plate seat, which every form build carries. Against the tonneau at its own committed
spiral seat (`case=tonneau&formrect=-45.87,45.87,-44.52,44.52`, which prints that warning and §261's depth):

### The gong bench

`gong-bench.html` offers the fitted tonneau ("Fitted tonneau"). `tools/gong-bench-data.mjs` walks `FORM_KINDS`
and reads each fit through `formShapeOf`, so the six-number fit cuts the bench's plate to the fitted outline, and
it requires each kind's DEFAULT seat rather than the spiral (`defaultSeatMode`: the fitted tonneau has only its
bar). The page falls back to a kind's default seat wherever it used to assume the spiral (its space and its fit
selector); the spiral-seat preset on the fitted case says that no spiral seat is generated, which is the table's
own answer. `tools/smoke-gong-bench.mjs` checks every `FORM_KINDS` entry is offered and that each committed seat,
the fitted bar included, rings its generated f₁ on the bench.Regenerated, the three §308 kinds' entries are byte-identical and the fitted
bar's is new (58,627 of 146,653 cells free at the wire's height, on §318's movement, its fit the seat's six numbers);
smoke-gong-bench: every check PASS at 1280 and 390 wide, the fitted bar ringing 2000 Hz against the build's
2000.01, and the solver on the fitted case (bar along a flat edge and anywhere, the hammer on its lever) returns 4
seats.
