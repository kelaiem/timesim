### Why a SECOND window, not a bigger first one

The obvious cut is to grow the cone window's reveal to the great wheel, and
the first build did exactly that: one window, reveal 15.79, every gate silent
but one. It was wrong for a reason no gate measures. §47's winding arrest hangs
its BRACKET from the plate's underside, its foot on plate body one margin plus a
foot's width outside the fusee window's largest rim (`BRK_R_OUT`), reaching in
over the cone to the stud. Grown with the window, that foot moved from 6.9 to
16.4 off the axis: a 0.62-wide arm about 13 long, carrying the stop's load, to
buy a view. That is P1's forbidden currency, a member stretched to pay for
packaging.

So the cone window stands exactly as it was, bit for bit (same reveal, r0,
webs, sectors, and so the same bracket, stud and arrest), and the plate keeps
one `TQ_LAND_MIN` of RING outside it for the bracket's foot to land on. A new
intent, `'fusee gears'`, is the annulus from that ring to the great wheel:

- **Reveal**: the great wheel's silhouette about the barrel axis, measured off
  the built `greatWheel` (`xyRadiusAbout`, exact for a revolver: teeth,
  crossings and the maintaining blade it carries), plus `CLEAR_MARGIN`.
  15.793 today. It is a WANT; step 1 pulls each bearing back against what the
  plate carries. 71 bearings are bitten (the centre and third chatons, a pillar
  seat, the kidney cut), down to 11.02 at worst.
- **Inner edge** (the intent's new `r0`): the cone window's reveal
  (`fuseeConeReveal`, the one law both read) plus `TQ_LAND_MIN`, plus twice the
  sagitta a half-degree chord sags by at that radius, so the land holds
  between the two EMITTED polygons and not only between the circles they
  sample. 8.236 today.
- **Webs**: the cone window's, carried on out (`websFrom: 'fusee'`). The open
  runs are CUT AT THOSE WEBS' AXES rather than split evenly. An even split
  agrees with the inner window only while both open all the way round; on the
  form and arranged builds (`probe-308-barrel-swing`'s A, cushion and tonneau)
  the gear window is pinched, and its even split put the webs at 80/190/331°
  against the cone's 35/155/275°. Cut at the axes, every web of this window is
  an inner web continued, and a pinch only leaves more plate between the ring
  and the plate body. The web width is the same `tqWebWidthFor` floor (span
  7.6, under the 21.6 where §54's ceiling would take over). `TQ_WEBS_MIN` stays
  the inner window's to meet. A boot warning names any gear sector that spans
  a cone web's axis.

### The walk, for an early window

The solve's end test assumes a ray is clear from the centre until it is blocked
once and for good, so a clear ray END means a clear ray. At 6.3 nothing the
plate is cut for stood inside the disc; at 15.8 the kidney cut stands beside
the rays' middles, and the first build left a **1.204** land against the cut
(need 1.900) on a web edge whose ray ended clear: `checkPlateWindows` check 2,
at all three stages. Late windows already WALK their rays (§201); early ones
cannot walk mesh keeps, because §47's stud stands in the cone window's air by
reading it. So an intent may declare `walkOpenings`: its rays are first cut back
where they meet the plate's own OPENINGS or PIVOT BOSSES (which belong to the
plate and can never be a window's tenants), and the old end test runs on what
is left. Absent the flag the solve is as it was, so the cone, governor and
arrest windows solve bit-identically.
