## §321 — The fitted back's metal bosses and the rounded, polished caseback

The owner's two asks, after §317 shipped:

1. **Make §317's raised glass islands metal bosses on the caseback.** A
   stepped sapphire is ground from one blank and polished on every wall,
   or assembled from bonded boxes whose glue lines must seal. A watchmaker
   would put the bumps in steel and keep the sapphire flat.
2. **Round the case back with the bezel's finish**, on every form case.

### The bosses (`makeFormCase`, form-case.js)

Each §317 island is now a **boss milled in the caseback**:

- **Its pocket** is the island's clearance loop. It runs from the back's
  inner face up to `zStepUnder`, the same ceiling the raised glass had, so
  §317's volume solve and its answer do not move. The fitted build still
  measures 11,159 mm³ against the flat back's 11,831, and 11.3624 mm deep.
- **Its cap** is the back's own lip, `LIP_T`, over the pocket. Where the
  pocket leaves less than a lip under the face, the cap stands proud of the
  back. On the fitted build that is 0.143 u (0.054 mm).
- **Its wall** is the lip's land (one glass thickness,
  `formGlassGrow − formApertureGrow`) plus §50's floor (main.js `bossWall`).
  This replaces the glass thickness §317's islands used, as `glassT` and in
  `join`. The pane tucks under a boss exactly as far as it tucks under the
  rim, and §50's floor of metal stands between the pocket and the glass. The
  wider wall merges no islands on the fitted build: two before, two now.

**A boss inside the window has to be carried, so each is joined to the rim
by the shortest route.** Its PAD is the convex hull of the boss's outer wall
and one point, the TIP:

- The tip lies on the line from the boss's vertex nearest the window to the
  window's vertex nearest it.
- It runs out from the window one `CLEAR_MARGIN` at a time until the pad,
  less the land, leaves the glass's edge by the margin. On the fitted build
  that is 4.50 u and 4.95 u.

The arm is a wedge, as wide as the hull makes it; no width is chosen.

**The outlines.** The window is the form's window with each pad bitten out
(`biteConvex`). The glass is the form's pane with each pad, less the land
(`insetConvex`), bitten out. It stays **one flat pane cut to an outline**,
which is ordinary sapphire work; nothing on it is stepped. `biteConvex`
refuses anything but a bite: it requires exactly two boundary crossings and
returns null otherwise, and the build warns that the boss could not be
joined.

On the fitted build, the cost is view:

|---|---|---|
| Window | 1075.5 mm² | 824.3 mm² (−23%) |
| Pane | 1150.0 mm² | 944.8 mm² |

The pads are 81.4 mm² and 174.0 mm².

The back is cut in LAYERS between every plane a wall starts or stops at:

- under the glass's outer face, the hole is the pane;
- over it, the hole is the window;
- under `zStepUnder`, each pocket is also a hole;
- past `z0`, only the bosses' proud caps stand.

### The rounded back (every form case)

The bezel's §289/§292 quarter ellipse cannot be copied: its reach, 5.46 u,
is wider than the whole back face, which runs from the outer wall to the
window, `G − formApertureGrow` (5.13 u on the fitted build). So the round is
derived the bezel's way, on the back's own face:

- **Reach**: the face less the bezel's own flat, `dims.bezelFlat`. The same
  0.8 mm flat stands round the window. Fitted build: 3.017 u (1.14 mm).
- **Height**: the back's own thickness, `z0 − zMidBack` (1.2 mm). The round
  ends tangent to the band's flank exactly at the band's back face. The
  bezel's round ends on its seat plane by the same rule.

The band now ends at `zMidBack`, where the round begins. Before, it ran on
to `z0` inside the back's metal.

The window keeps its square bore, because it is the lip that holds the
glass.

**The thinnest metal the round leaves over the glass's channel is measured
and held to §50's floor.** On the fitted build it is 1.669 u, 0.63 mm.

**The finish.** The rim and the lip layer (the face's flat, the pads' faces
and the window's bore) carry the bezel's polish (`CASE_POLISH_ATTR`, tagged
per part before the merge). The channel and the bosses' proud caps keep the
slider's brush.

### The drawing

`formCaseLines` reads the built back (`g.userData.formBack`):

- the outer wall's ring and its generators end at the band's back face;
- `backRound` is the ring where the round meets the flat;
- the window and glass rings are the bitten outlines;
- `boss` is each pocket's mouth and ceiling, plus the proud cap's two edges.

`probe-schematic-form-case.mjs` holds every one of them on the metal, on
every form build. `backRound` joins the kinds every form drawing must carry.
Its six tube lines fail on every form build, on `main` too (fitted 0.232920
before and after): debt that predates this landing.
