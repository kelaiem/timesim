## §321 — The rounded, polished caseback, and the fitted back made flat

The owner's asks after §317, in order:

1. **§317's raised glass islands.** A stepped sapphire is ground from one blank
   and polished on every wall, or assembled from bonded boxes whose glue lines
   must seal. First ask: make them metal bosses on the caseback. Then, having
   seen the bosses: **remove them and the islands altogether**, for cost and
   complexity, and because the bosses crowded the view into the case.
2. **Round the case back with the bezel's finish**, on every form case.

### The fitted back is flat again

§317's volume half is deleted:

- `solveFittedBack` (layout.js);
- the XY grid and unit plans the back envelope's walk kept for it (main.js
  `BACK_ENVELOPE.fittedGrid`);
- `FITTED_BACK`, `CASE_DIMS.formStep` and `fittedBack`;
- the volume block in `formFootprint.fitted`;
- probe-317's B tests.

The fitted case's back is §307's flat form back, like every form case's: one
pane, one margin over the tallest metal in the plan. §317's guards on §187's
round step and §198's skirt assert are reverted with it: they existed only
because the fitted build lowered its pane.

**What that gives back is depth.** The fitted build is 11.9624 mm deep again,
the tonneau's depth, over CASE_HEIGHT_MAX_MM's 11.367. §261's warning returns
on it, the second warning of the fitted default build, beside §186's.

The plan area is untouched: the fitted outline is still 1068.5 mm² against the
tonneau's 1124.6 (−5.0%). The interior volume is the flat back's 11,831 mm³.

**The bosses, for the record.** They were built (commit 48310d96) and removed
before landing:

- a pocket over each island's clearance loop, with the back's lip as its cap;
- a wedge PAD joining each boss to the rim by the shortest route;
- the window and one flat pane cut round the pads.

They cost the fitted window 23% of its area (1075.5 to 824.3 mm²).

### The rounded back (every form case)

The bezel's §289/§292 quarter ellipse cannot be copied. Its reach, 5.46 u, is
wider than the whole back face, which runs from the outer wall to the window,
`G − formApertureGrow` (5.13 u on the fitted build). So the round is derived
the bezel's way, on the back's own face:

- **Reach**: the face less the bezel's own flat, `dims.bezelFlat`, so the same
  0.8 mm flat stands round the window. Fitted build: 3.017 u (1.14 mm).
- **Height**: the back's own thickness, `z0 − zMidBack` (1.2 mm). The round
  ends tangent to the band's flank exactly at the band's back face, by the
  same rule that ends the bezel's round on its seat plane.

The band now ends at `zMidBack`, where the round begins; before, it ran on to
`z0` inside the back's metal.

The window keeps its square bore, because that bore is the lip that holds the
glass.

The thinnest metal the round leaves over the glass's channel is measured and
held to §50's floor. It is 1.669 u (0.63 mm), the same on every form build,
since the channel and the round both stand off the outer wall.

**The finish.** The rim and the lip layer (the flat and the window's bore)
carry the bezel's polish (`CASE_POLISH_ATTR`, tagged per part before the
merge). The channel under the glass keeps the slider's brush.

### The drawing

`formCaseLines` reads the built round (`g.userData.formBack.round`):

- The outer wall's ring and its generators end at the band's back face.
- The new `backRound` ring is where the round meets the flat.

`probe-schematic-form-case.mjs` holds both on the metal on every form build,
and `backRound` joins the kinds every form drawing must carry.

The probe's six tube lines fail on every form build, on `main` too (fitted:
0.232920 before and after). That debt predates this landing.
