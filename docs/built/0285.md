## §285 — LEGO fusee: a third step surveyed and refused, the centre pinion built

§219 tier three (b), after §282. The owner asked to "add another gear to the
fusee if possible". The first reading was another STEP on the cone, so the
staircase would level the spring better. Landing H's catalogue had five grooved
parts, and with them the solve answered two steps. A third step needed a part
nobody had catalogued, so this landing surveyed the whole LDraw library for
one. A third step loses everywhere the build can mount a groove. The survey did
find a better TWO-step cone on landing H's own barrel, and the solve now takes
it. Because the cone step is impossible, the "other gear" is the going train's
next wheel: a centre pinion on the great wheel. Everything here is MODELLED.
The spring is still the POSED stand-in, and nothing is integrated.

### The survey

`tools/ldraw-groove-survey.mjs` is a report, on the pattern of
`ldraw-notch-survey.mjs`. It reads an unpacked LDraw library from disk. It takes
every part keyed on an axle through its origin. The hole primitives it accepts
are `axlehole`, `axl2hole`, `axl3hole` and their variants: the half bushes
draw `axl2hole`, and a first regex that took only `axlehole` missed them.
Stickers, patterns, minifig, Duplo, electric, baseplate, third-party and
shortcut parts are skipped. For each part it reads the outer radius per 0.1 LDU
along the axis. A groove is a local least of the cord's seat with a wall either
side standing above the cord's centre. Each groove is then measured by
`grooveSeat` itself, windowed to it.

A groove is USABLE when four things hold:

- it is on the part's own Z;
- it is round to a quarter of the cord's diameter (0.3 mm), all the way round;
- both walls stand above the cord's centre at every angle, in 2° bins;
- its floor's middle is within half a cord radius of the part's origin.

The last rule exists because the build places a part by its origin. The offset
inside that half radius is carried exactly, as `grooveOff`.

Its controls are printed first. Every catalogued seat must re-read to 1e-3 mm,
and the 3×3 disc (2723) must read no groove. The controls caught two real
errors. The first sampled every part at 1 LDU, and the pulley wheel 5D read
18.13 mm against its tabled 18.18, so parts under 20 mm of radius are now read
at 0.25 LDU. The second was the regex above. Two more defects showed in
mid-run. A large wheel's samples filled the heap: the first library run was
killed at 4.9 GB and the second by the OOM killer. Samples are now streamed
(`forEachSurfaceSample`), and each part's profile is memoised once per
library. And the walls rule first let a cross block's arms count as walls.

The survey also found a defect in landing H's own `grooveSeat`. The floor was
taken as the outermost of ALL cord positions at the seat. A crowned tyre has a
gutter either side of its crown, and the two gutters read as one 15 mm channel
holding 13 cords, centred on the part. The 62 mm motorcycle wheel 84772 entered
the catalogue that way. The solve then answered a three-step cone at 22.98% on
it as the barrel, and the wrap probe caught the cord standing 0.4 mm from the
surface instead of 0.6. The floor is now the run CONTIGUOUS with the lowest
position. Landing H's five read exactly as before.

### The solve

The usable fourteen joined `FUSEE_PULLEYS`. Each has its seat, capacity,
outermost radius, half width, floor and `grooveOff` in `FUSEE_DIM_MM`, and its
survey window in `FUSEE_DIM_MM.read`. `probe-technic-fusee.mjs` re-reads every
one off the pack to 1e-4 mm, and its offset to 0.006 mm. The `.mpd` pack grew
from 849 to 1212 KiB, re-vendored through `ldraw-vendor.mjs`, which reads the
catalogue.

`solveFusee` no longer enumerates 2^n masks. It grows descending sets a step at
a time and cuts a set once its upper wraps alone need more cord than any barrel
lays. Larger sets only add cord, so the cut loses nothing. It solves the
19-part catalogue in about 150 ms, from 1212 runs that fit. Ties break by
catalogue order, because the four half bushes tie exactly. `fuseeRun` refuses a
run before sampling it when the base wrap falls outside (0, 1].

Landing H's two-step was 40.13%. The answer, `TECHNIC_FUSEE`, is now the thin
tread sprocket's hub groove (base, 0.801 of a wrap) over the 8 × 18 rim's deep
centre groove (one wrap), on the pulley wheel 5D. The solve's rule ranks it
first. Nothing was hand-picked.

**Why no third step wins.** The barrel turns 0.6663 over the spring's band, so
it takes 2π·18.18·0.6663 = 76.1 mm of cord. Every step above the base takes a
WHOLE wrap of that, so a third step crowds the cone onto small, close seats.
The probe searches every seat FREE, on a 0.2 mm grid from 3 to 14 mm. On the
answer's barrel, two steps reach 27.78% (7.6 > 5.8 mm) and three only 28.35%
(6.4 > 5.0 > 3.8 mm). No part anyone makes could change that. On a 29.25 mm
barrel (122.5 mm of cord), three win, 20.97% against 27.70%. That is the
control, and it is the survey's CEILING: if the build could mount a part by an
off-centre groove, a three-step cone on 84772's gutter would reach 22.33%
against that barrel's best two-step at 28.00%. That needs two things. One is a
mounting law that chooses which of a part's grooves to use and which way round
to mount it. The other is a 62 mm motorcycle wheel as the barrel. It is
reported here and not built.

Two rules moved with the new cone. The barrel's centre distance must pass
EVERY step's rim, not just the base's, and the CLEAR floor is now part of the
rule: cd ≥ rim(barrel) + the largest step rim + AX_CLEAR. The sprocket's teeth
stand 13.10 mm out, so cd = ⌈(18.68 + 13.10 + 0.4)/4⌉·4 = 36. At landing H's
32, CLEAR caught the barrel and the sprocket 0.316 mm apart. The barrel's
placement also carries `grooveOff`: its origin stands at the groove plane less
the offset. `stepPlanes` adds each step's offset to its wrap plane.

The cord is now the 21L string (75924), 7.88 mm dead on the barrel. The
maintaining solve answered as before: the detent at (−28, −16), click band −1.

### The centre pinion

Landing H stopped at the great wheel, the last thing that turned. The steel
train's next member is the centre pinion the great wheel drives, `TRAIN.barrel`
120:7, a step-up of 17.14. One LEGO mesh off the 40T cannot reach that.
`solveCentrePinion` takes the classic catalogue's gears that mesh the 40T in
parallel on the lattice (`catalogueMesh`, the rule every fork row is held to)
and ranks them by |log(40/n ÷ 120/7)|:

| mesh | centre distance | step-up |
|---|---|---|
| **40:8** | **24 mm** | **5.000** |
| 40:16 | 28 mm | 2.500 |
| 40:24 | 32 mm | 1.667 |
| 40:40 | 40 mm | 1.000 |
| 40:56 | 48 mm | 0.714 |

The 8T (3647) stands in the great wheel's slab, 24 mm out from the main line on
the far side from the barrel, with its own 5L axle and two columns on the great
wheel's two stud slots. Its angle travels the mesh (standing rule 2):
`centrePhase` meets a tooth of each part on the line of centres, read off each
part's own tooth phase (`toothPhase`, the law `ldrawBuild` meshes every row by).
In the loop, `loopPose` turns it −40/8 of the great wheel's turn: `phiC`, and
`technic.html` turns its body by it. It drives nothing. The rest of the steel's
step-up belongs to the next mesh, which this module does not build.

### Reported, not gated

§282's free-span report moved with the cone. On landing H's cone the cord left
the base step 13.93° off the open belt's tangent. On this one it leaves the
sprocket 51.74° off, and the free span runs 23.05–33.86 mm over the run where
it used to run 26.39–30.49. The crossing law in `fuseeRun` is the
simplification §282 named, and the new cone makes it larger. It is not fixed
here: correcting it would re-solve the cone, and the solve's spread rests on it.
The click's ride table now ends 0.09° from where it began over one pitch,
against landing H's 0.00. That is reported, and it is not a seat.

A headless smoke run of `technic.html?focus=fusee` at 1280×800 shows the cone
with the sprocket and the 8 × 18 rim on the 5D barrel. The step the cord is on
is lit: the rim while running down on step 2, the sprocket while winding. The
8T pinion turns beside the great wheel. The console was clean.

### What it does not claim

It claims no torque in N·mm and no rate. The spring is still the POSED
stand-in, and a measured LEGO spring re-solves all of this with no code
change. It does not claim that a cord runs well in a tread sprocket's hub
groove or a tyre seat: those are grooves by the survey's geometry, and whether
the braid rides them under load is a bench question.
