## §316 — LEGO going train, landing 2: the escapement driven by the posed spring law, frictions tabled, and a rating nut

Owner, after §315 merged: "go on landing 2, as you scoped it — the escapement DRIVEN".
Landing 1 joined the fusee, the train and §287's escapement into one train, but the
balance still swung at a POSED ±3° and the train followed it. Here the swing is SOLVED.
The balance's equation of motion is stepped in time: gravity restores it, and the anchor
pushes it with the escape wheel's torque. That torque comes from the fusee's spring law
through the cone and the 2250:1 train. The landing adds a RATING NUT made of real parts
to put the period on the beat. Everything is `src/technic-drive.js`, held by
`tools/probe-technic-drive.mjs`.

It is DRIVEN BY A POSED SPRING LAW, FRICTIONS TABLED: modelled, never measured. Every
number that rests on the posed law says so.

### The drive, link by link

- **The spring.** `TECHNIC_FUSEE_SPRING`, the ONLY spring source. It is POSED (§285, the
  steel ribbon's shape), and it carries a SHAPE, not a size: its torque is a fraction of
  full wind. The size is the one number a measurement of a LEGO band would give. Here it
  is SOLVED (`solveScale`): the largest full-wind torque at which the driven period stays
  inside `RATE_BAND` (0.5%) of the free pendulum's at the same swing, over the whole run,
  at the nominal friction corner. A spring driven harder makes the escapement keep its own
  time, not the pendulum's. The answer is **9.529e-5 N·mm at the escape arbor** at the run's
  highest level product (`TECHNIC_DRIVE.escapeTorqueHi`), at the pallets' driven setting
  (below). At the barrel that is **0.757 N·mm at full wind (classic, 7 meshes)** or
  **0.720 N·mm (2026, 6 meshes)**. A measured band should not exceed this. Over the run the
  cone's residual spread (27.55%, on the posed law) carries the escape torque from 7.12e-5
  to 9.53e-5 N·mm at the nominal corner.
- **The cone.** Tension M/ρ_b at the barrel's seat, torque P/ρ_b at the fusee, where
  P = M × arm is the run's level product (fuseeRun's own samples, §286). The maintaining
  work is lossless (posed; it carries the great wheel only in the wind).
- **The train.** Divide by the ratio read off the build and multiply by each mesh's
  efficiency, `TECHNIC_DRIVE_FRICTION.mesh`. That is a TABLED band (favourable 0.98, nominal
  0.95, adverse 0.90; a band for plastic spur gearing as general references quote it).
  Nothing here measures a LEGO mesh.

The probe holds the chain as an identity at every run sample: Munit·M·arm/ρ_b·η^n/ratio
at float noise. Doubling the spring table doubles every escape torque exactly. Its
control, a typed torque that ignores the table, fails that check.

### The anchor's contact, tabled

The escape wheel's angle along the anchor's swing is §287's quasi-static settle (the parts'
outlines). It is traced once (`contactTablesSteps`), at a hundredth of a degree of the
anchor a step, over the widest swing whose drops are the posed swing's own:
**8.75°** (`tableSwing`). At the pallets' driven setting (37.25°, below) the wheel has no
free place at 9°: the anchor jams. At §287's 38.75° a pallet first slips near the swing's
end (a drop at −8.76° the small swing never makes) and the anchor jams at 9.25°. Inside
8.75° the contact along each leg is a function of the anchor's angle alone, so one trace
serves for every swing up to it. A swing that grows past it BANKS.

The wheel and the train behind it are MASSLESS here (landing 3 gives them their inertia).
Where a tooth touches a pallet the work balances, τ dθ = Q dα, so the anchor feels
Q = τ dθ/dα. A drop does no work on the balance. Pallet friction is the tabled band
`TECHNIC_DRIVE_FRICTION.pallet` (favourable 0.80, nominal 0.70, adverse 0.60). It is a guess
bracketed wide: two ABS teeth sliding with no lubricant. The wheel driving the anchor
delivers η·τ dθ/dα; the anchor driving the wheel back (recoil) costs τ dθ/dα / η.

### The equation of motion

I α'' = −m g d sin α + Q(α, sign α'), stepped by semi-implicit Euler at a five-thousandth
of the period. I and m g d come from §287's `partSolid` integrals of the very parts the
build places, rating nut included, at ESC_DENSITY's mean. With no drive it is the
pendulum: at 0.1° its period is 2π √(I/mgd) to 1e-7, and at 3°, 5° and 8° it is the
elliptic integral's to 1e-7. Its control, a restoring torque 1% stiffer, misses. Driven, the
energy books close to the step's own error: 0.48 of its bound (ω·h of the energy), and half
that again at half the step. Counted without the pallets' friction they miss by 5.1 times
the work: the control.

**What the drive's size does not set.** No τ-independent loss is modelled: neither the knife
edge's rolling nor the air. So the swing's amplitude is set by the pallets' friction and the
anchor's geometry, not by the drive's size. Twice the torque moves the nominal swing from
2.166° to 2.170°. That is the solve's first finding, and the reason a measurement of the
knife's and the air's losses is landing 3's first need.

**Period against amplitude**, measured off the driven cycle at the nominal corner and the
ceiling's torque (every period of the swing's approach from starts of 1.5° to 8°), RISES:
1.5° 0.99807 s, 2° 0.99996 s, 3° 1.00198 s, 5° 1.00409 s, 8° 1.00523 s. There are two
reasons, and they push the same way:

- the pendulum's own circular error grows with the swing (T₀ (1 + A²/16 + …));
- the anchor's recoil, a push back against the swing just after each catch that the
  balance feels as a stiffer spring, is a smaller share of a wider swing, so the
  escapement's negative error shrinks.

The probe holds the rise. Its control, a hardening restoring torque, makes the period fall.

### The small swing and the friction: the energy a beat, and the pallets' setting

The owner, on this landing's first draft: "Address the small swing issue and the friction
problem." The draft drove §287's pallets at their middle setting, 38.75°. Its nominal swing
was 3.93°. The adverse corner swung 2.30°, and the favourable corner 8.39–8.47°, which is
0.27° under the first slip (8.76°). At the run's most torque the classic train BANKED.

**The budget first.** `beatBudget` books a beat's energy. The spring's work a beat
(τ_e·pitch/2 ÷ η_mesh^n) equals:

- the meshes' loss,
- plus the DROP (the wheel's free turn, its work spent as the tooth lands),
- plus the pallets' two frictions (on the impulse and on the recoil),
- plus the balance's net (the impulse it takes, less the recoil it gives back).

The probe holds the identities. The balance's net by the rows equals the equation of
motion's own work at float noise: the first run missed by 4e-6 to 6e-4, because the rows
classified a reversing step by the wheel's motion and the torque by the swing's direction,
and they now share one branch. The drop the books infer equals the anchor trace's own drops
to 1%. Its control, books that forget the recoil, misses.

The draft's budget, in µJ a beat at the run's mean torque (38.75°, a ceiling of 3.198e-4 N·mm):

| corner | spring | meshes | drop | pallet friction (impulse + recoil) | balance takes / gives back |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| favourable | 0.165 | 0.022 (13%) | 0.110 (67%) | 0.018 + 0.015 | 0.074 / 0.073 |
| nominal | 0.165 | 0.050 (30%) | 0.088 (54%) | 0.016 + 0.011 | 0.037 / 0.036 |
| adverse | 0.165 | 0.086 (52%) | 0.061 (37%) | 0.011 + 0.007 | 0.017 / 0.017 |

**What it says.** The dominant losses are the DROP (54% at nominal: 17.3° of the beat's
22.5° the wheel turns free) and, at the adverse corner, the MESHES (52%). Neither one sets
the swing. The balance keeps none of the work: with no τ-independent loss, it settles where
the recoil it pays back costs exactly the impulse it takes in, recoil / impulse = η². That
is a property of the anchor's geometry and the pallets' friction alone. The probe shows
it. Twice the torque, which is everything upstream of the escape arbor (the spring, a
lower-friction train, a drive point nearer the wheel), moves the swing by 0.2%, while the
pallets' corner moves it by 30%. The balance's inertia at the same period scales I and mgd
together, which is the same as scaling the torque, so it cannot move the swing either. The
knife's losses are not modelled, and adding them would only lower the swing. So of the
owner's candidate levers, only the pallets' geometry is a cause, and its one free number
is the SETTING.

**The setting.** Every setting of the escaping run on a quarter-degree grid is read
(`settingMargins`, `solveSetting`): the lock against F2's 0.1 mm play, the release and the
first slip on the setting's own trace, and each extreme corner's swing. The adverse corner
is taken at the run's least torque and adverse meshes; the favourable corner at its most
torque and favourable meshes. Settings 37.75–38.25° lock under the play. From 39° up, the
favourable corner banks inside the band. The two margins are then read in the quantity
nobody has measured, the pallets' friction (`frictionWindow`). The window is the range of
pallet efficiencies at which the adverse swing still clears the release, and the
favourable swing still stands under the slip, each by a table grid step:

| setting | lock | adverse over release | favourable under slip | friction window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 37° | 0.144 mm | 0.88° | 4.26° | 0.40–0.86 |
| **37.25°** | 0.165 mm | 0.91° | 4.04° | **0.35–0.85** |
| 37.5° | 0.192 mm | 0.53° | 5.40° | 0.51–0.88 |
| 38.5° | 0.124 mm | 0.97° | 2.16° | 0.42–0.82 |
| 38.75° (§287) | 0.159 mm | 1.28° | 0.43° | 0.38–0.80 |

The answer is the setting whose window is widest, **37.25°** (`TECHNIC_DRIVE.palletDeg`).
The band is a guess bracketed wide, and nothing says where in it LEGO's real pallet friction
lies, so the robust setting is the one that survives the widest range of it. 38.5° has the
largest worse margin in degrees, by 0.06°, but it slips at η 0.82. §287's cell keeps 38.75°.
A pallet's setting is its friction pin turned in the liftarm's round hole. Measuring the
steps found that the pin had been placed unturned under its keyed 8T, its cross across the
8T's. The flag only appeared at 38.5°, so the pin now carries the pallet's angle.

**After**, in µJ a beat (37.25°, the ceiling re-solved to 9.529e-5 N·mm):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| favourable | 4.65° | 0.049 | 0.007 (13%) | 0.036 (74%) | 0.004 + 0.003 | 0.014 / 0.014 |
| nominal | 2.17° | 0.049 | 0.015 (30%) | 0.029 (59%) | 0.003 + 0.002 | 0.007 / 0.007 |

**The margins, both directions.** In degrees, the adverse corner's least swing over the
whole run (1.509°) clears the release (0.60°) by 0.91°, which is 2.5 times the release. The
favourable corner's greatest swing (4.673°) stands 4.08° under the slip (8.75°). In friction,
the pallets may run from η 0.346 to 0.863 with both margins kept. That is 0.25 under the
band's adverse end and 0.06 over its favourable end. The favourable side is the narrow one:
pallets freer than about 0.86 make a swing that reaches the slip.

**What it costs, said plainly.** The nominal swing is SMALLER, 2.17° against the draft's
3.93°. A swing this small is the escapement's own and not a defect: what a swing must do is
pass the release, and it passes it by 2.5 times at the worst corner. The larger swing could
only be had by standing the favourable corner on the slip. The drive's ceiling also falls,
from 3.198e-4 to 9.529e-5 N·mm (0.76 N·mm at the barrel against 2.54), because the
escapement's error per unit torque is larger at the smaller swing. The lock against a hand
falls to 34× (35× in 2026), still holding the train it is driven by.

**What stays POSED or TABLED:**

- the spring law's shape (`TECHNIC_FUSEE_SPRING`), with its size solved, not measured;
- both friction bands, the meshes' and the pallets' (the window above says how wrong the
  pallets' band may be);
- the knife edge's and the air's losses, which are not modelled at all (zero in the budget);
- the wheel's and the train's inertia (massless here), the maintaining work's loss and the
  cord's stretch;
- ESC_DENSITY's mean, and F2's 0.1 mm play.

No arrangement of these parts was found that keeps a large swing AND the favourable corner
clear of the slip: the one escaping lattice geometry for an 8T wheel (§287's) offers only
its setting, and the table above is that whole choice.

### The lock against a hand

With the balance at rest and the wheel on a pallet, §287's trip torque, **4.03e-3 N·mm**
at the escape arbor (frictionless), is **34×** the most the drive delivers (classic; 35× in
2026). The escapement holds the train it is driven by. A hand on the great wheel trips it at
about 13.0 N·mm there (12.3 N·mm in 2026, nominal meshes).

### The rating nut

Every axle on this balance is parallel to the knife, so a nut slid along one moves no mass
about it. The probe measures the period moving by 0.0 s for a nut slid a stud along the
bob's axle. A RATING NUT needs a radial member. It is the angle connector #6 (**32014**,
two axle holes at 90°, vendored by this landing: `RATING_PARTS`) on the bob axle's front
end, its other arm up. A 4L axle stands in it, and a **bush with two flanges (3713)** slides
on that axle, held by its own friction. In the parts list the nut is a collar (as the alarm
pusher's bush is) and the connector a joiner. `frameEscapement`'s `rating` option builds
it, and §315's `escapementFor` gives the going train's balance one.

- **The rod** (`solveRatingRod`): of the 2L to 8L axles, only the 4L's nut travel spans
  the beat. Shorter rods leave the balance fast whatever the nut does (2L 0.969 s,
  3L 0.975–0.981 s). Longer ones are slow throughout (5L 1.005–1.029 s), because the rod
  itself stands above the knife on a balance whose centre of mass is 3 mm below it.
- **Its place** (`solveNut`, declared `TECHNIC_DRIVE.nutV`): **17.17 mm above the knife's
  apex**, the beat at the nominal corner and the run's mean torque, 0.8 mm short of the rod's
  top.
- **Its sensitivity, DERIVED** (`nutSensitivity`) from the inertias.
  T₀ = 2π √(I/(gρF)), with F = Σ V v the balance's first moment down the plumb. The nut
  (128 mm³) moved dv changes I by 2V_n v dv and F by V_n dv. So
  dT₀/dv = T₀ · ½ (2 V_n v / I − V_n / F). That is **85.7 s/day per millimetre** up the rod,
  **686 s/day per stud**. It agrees with the free period's finite difference to 1%. On the
  DRIVEN rate it measures 84.8 s/day a millimetre. Its control, a nut whose mass is ignored,
  derives nothing and misses.
- **Its range** at the nominal corner: from +1169 s/day (bottom) to −73 s/day (top). It is
  a coarse trim, as a bush on a pendulum this short must be.

### Landing 3 (scope only)

Landing 3 is the integrated dynamics of the whole train's run-down, still to be built. It
needs:

- **The losses this landing leaves out:** the knife edge's rolling loss and the air's drag
  on the balance. These are the τ-independent losses that make the swing's amplitude depend
  on the drive's size; today it depends only on the pallets.
- **The inertias:** the escape wheel's, and the train's reflected through the teeth. Then a
  drop is a run the wheel takes and a catch is an impact.
- **The maintaining work's loss and the cord's stretch.**
- **The run-down itself:** the barrel's wind falling as the escape wheel's teeth go by. The
  going time and the rate across a wind then come out of one integration in time, rather
  than this landing's per-torque swings at three points of the run.

Gates it would carry:

- landing 2's swing and period at the matching torque, in the limit of those losses and
  inertias going to zero;
- energy books across a whole wind.

And always, before any of it is a claim: a MEASURED band (torque against barrel turns) and a
measured pallet friction. The two bands this landing tables are what its answers turn on.
No Swiss lever, no hairspring, no wrist scale: the analogue stays an analogue.
