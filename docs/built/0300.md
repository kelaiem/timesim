## §300 — LEGO build parts lists: a visual bill of materials per build (landing 1 of the instructions scoping)

Owner: "Start in the instructions and parts lists." This is the first landing of
the private roadmap's §297 scoping ("LEGO build instructions and a visual parts
list for each build"; that number is the scoping's, this one is the landing's):
each LEGO cell on `technic.html` now carries a **parts list** — what to buy for
that build, in what quantity and colour, with a picture of every part — and a
CSV of it. Step order, the swept-insertion validator, the MPD export and the
print booklet are NOT here (landings 2 and 3 of that scoping). Display only: no
placement, `.ldr` line or build probe moves except the colour column of
`LDRAW_COLOUR`.

**MODELLED, not built.** Every list says so on the page and in the CSV: the
placements are checked against the part geometry (seated, through, meshed), no
one has assembled any of these builds, and the forces behind the fusee, the
alarm and the escapement are POSED. The word "simulated" is not on the page.

### What the list says (measured, both generations, `tools/probe-technic-bom.mjs`)

The list is `placedParts` folded to a quantity per (LDraw id, colour code).
Three honest changes sit between the placements and the shopping list:

- **The fusee's cord is one string.** It is placed as 66 braided segments and 2
  end studs (68 pieces); the list shows ONE `75924` ("String Braided 21L with
  End Studs", title read from the pack), 21 studs, 160 mm between the end
  studs, of which 33.6 mm lies dead on the barrel. That is `cordChoice`'s pick,
  re-derived in the probe by calling it on the cord's own path length. 140
  placements are 73 parts.
- **A keyless cell is folded at every mode** (wind, set, neutral) and the three
  folds are the same multiset; the motion works is part of that build, so the
  `motion` stop shows the keyless cell's list.
- **The alarm's parts are the same in armed, ringing and off.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| row 1, centre to fourth | 59 (classic 64) | 59 (64) | 11 | 4 |
| row 2, fourth to escape | 38 | 38 | 11 | 4 |
| row 3, motion works | 44 (classic 43) | 44 (43) | 10 (classic 9) | 4 |
| row 4, reserve reduction | 26 | 26 | 11 | 5 |

Ten stops, eight builds, 51 distinct LDraw ids across both generations (the
scoping's 52, less the cord's two sub-parts, plus the string). Every figure
matches the scoping's census except the line and colour counts, which moved
with the colour table (the scoping counted 6, 8 and 6 colours for the fusee,
alarm and escapement while those roles still fell to colour 16).

### Colour by role, complete

`LDRAW_COLOUR` was ten roles; the fusee, alarm and escapement place 25 more
that fell to colour 16 ("the placer's colour": a viewer's default), so a
picture-led list would have shown a gear and the brick beside it as one grey.
It is 42 rows now, and every role any build places, in either generation, at
every keyless mode and every alarm state, has one. The probe fails a role with
no row (the fold throws rather than defaulting), a code that is not a real LDraw
colour or is 16, a row naming a role nothing places (stale), and any `.ldr`
line in colour 16. Two role tables that had never been read by anything
(`FUSEE_COLOUR`, `ALARM_COLOUR`) disagreed with the .ldr's on `pawl` and
`joiner` and were deleted: one declaration, not three. `technic.html` still
draws by its own role palette (unchanged); the list's pictures use the .ldr's
colours. **The colour names and RGB are LDraw.org's definitions, typed from its
published list and NOT vendored with the pack** — they are display values, and a
supplier's colour ids are not mapped (the list and the CSV say so).

### Purchase ids: never from memory, always with a basis

`PART_TABLE` is the one hand-maintained table. Each row is a purchase id and a
BASIS, and `auditTable` re-derives every derivable basis from its source, so the
table cannot drift from them and a source that improves on a row fails it:

| basis | rows | source |
|---|---|---|
| `catalogue` | 9 | `layout.js` CATALOGUES' `part` column (3647, 69778, 4019, 69779, 3648, 46372, 3649, and the 2026 gears 7785, 7861), read from BrickLink's `Technic, Gear` category by a person on 2026-09-12 |
| `ldraw-keyword` | 6 | the vendored file's own `0 !KEYWORDS BrickLink N` cross-reference: 3023b is 3023, 3070b is 3070, 4032a is 4032, 4185a is 4185, 32123a is 4265c, and 22253 reads 6595 (a different LDraw part's number: the line says "(6595, not 22253)" and is not checked against a catalogue). The string 75924's header names only a printed variant, which is skipped. |
| `unverified` | 36 | none in the repo: the LDraw id is used as the lookup key and the line says "purchase id not yet verified" |
| `person` | 0 | the upgrade path |

Status (official, unofficial, mould-variant) is READ from the pack, never
typed: `Unofficial_*` org is unofficial; a letter suffix or a `~Moved to` stub
(3648 is a stub for 3648b, and the list reads the target's title) is a
mould-variant, whose purchase id may differ. 7785 and 7861 print flagged in
technic_2026 and technic_classic lists none.

### Pictures

`src/technic-bom-view.js` renders each part in its own canvas from the page's
`geoOf` triangles and edges, in a corner view (azimuth 45 degrees, elevation 30
degrees, LDraw's +Y down) fitted to the part's projected bounds plus 12%, as a
160 px PNG data URL shown at 56 px. The camera is pure (`viewBasis`,
`viewBounds` in `src/technic-bom.js`), so the smoke test reads the expected
silhouette from the pack alone. **Measured under SwiftShader, 143 pictures over
the eight 2026 lists, every one non-blank and within 3 px of its part's box in
width and height.** The control swaps a plate and a gear under each other's
labels and the check catches both; a blanked picture is caught too.

### Not in the model

Per build, listed as exactly that: the fusee's rubber bands (the barrel's and
the maintaining band) and the alarm's (column-wheel detent, strike barrel) and
the keyless detent follower's band, all "unspecified"; the alarm's gong ("no
LEGO part rings"; the hammer strikes a tile, which is in the list); a hand to
turn the crown, the winding crank, or the pusher; and the hands the motion works
turns (none placed). No count appears in that block that the model does not hold
(the probe fails a digit there).

### Instruments

`tools/probe-technic-bom.mjs` (pure Node, ~25 s, 2305 checks, 27 controls): the
totals (placements, less the cord's pieces, plus the one string; every mode;
every alarm state); the table audited against the pack's raw headers and the
catalogue (a dropped, stale, over- or under-claimed row all fail); the unofficial
flag read from the header (a pack rewritten so 7785 is official prints no flag);
colour completeness; the CSV parsed back to the lines, the not-in-the-model items
and the honesty text; a word scan (no "works", "tested" or "simulated") over the
prose and the page's code; the picture camera against an independent lookAt;
the page seeding a build per cell. `tools/smoke-technic-bom.mjs` (Chromium, 1280
and 390 wide, ~2 min): every stop's list opens from the bar's "parts" button,
totals and lines are the pure module's, the pictures are their parts, the CSV
download is the list, the classic toggle re-lists a cell, the unofficial gears
are flagged, no overflow. Controls: both tools' above.

**What the battery cannot see:** all of it. `technic.html` and `src/technic-*.js`
import `layout.js` and nothing from the movement, and the battery never loads
them. The fast checks are these two instruments and the twelve existing technic
probes (frame, build, ldraw, keyless, motion, fusee, fusee-loop, alarm,
column-wheel, pusher, escapement, carousel), unchanged by this landing.

**Cost.** The keyless build is ~8 s of Node (its detent survey), so the page
SEEDS `src/technic-bom.js`'s memo with the builds it already made and the list
for the page's own generation is instant; the other generation's keyless list
builds on the first request, after a "reading the build" line.

**Left, named.** The 36 unverified purchase ids and the 6 keyword ids want a
person with Rebrickable and BrickLink access (open question 8). Landings 2 and
3 (step order and validator, MPD export, the viewer, the booklet) are the
roadmap's. A BrickLink wanted-list XML is out of scope until the ids are read.
