## §313 — The arranged train holds the power reserve at 12 o'clock: six idlers, the module at 60°, the arming fixed point per arrangement

Out of the owner's call on §308 landing 5's arrangement (the barrel swung to 180°, the reserve carried round to
36°, the alarm module at 80°): the power reserve stays at 12 o'clock in the new layout, as designed. This landing
solves that on the form cases' default, and on the round case for any URL that asks for it. The ROUND default
keeps the designed train; making the arrangement the round default is its own landing (the battery's reference
build moves).

**Why 12 o'clock refused.** The scan, the reserve azimuth stepped toward 12 at the 80° module (round, booted
whole): 36° is clean, and from 30° down every station prints the setting traverse's cap-bearing refusal
("no cap bearing within ±60° leaves the reserve a swing …") and the link's site refusal. The station at 12 is
22.42 u from the swung barrel against 14.65 designed, so stage two's span is 16.30 u against 8.53. The train's
module is solved from that span (`rsvModule1 = 2·d1 / Σ teeth`), so with two meshes p1 and w2 came out as wheels
of 7.6 u, over the setting route and the link (and w2 at 8.15 against its well's 8.09, TODO 33's ceiling). A
planetary set was considered at the owner's suggestion: it is coaxial, so it changes a ratio, not a distance,
and the ratio here (R = 2.1) is easy; the distance is what fails.

**The idlers.** `?rsvidl=` (`SPEC.rsvIdlers`, an even count 0–6) names the idler pairs §308 L4 option B's
machinery already cuts between p1 and w2 (`rsvFitTrainAt`), until here reachable only on a sector station.
They cancel out of R and come in pairs, so the counts still solve from the reserve and the hand keeps its
sense. A disc well keeps TODO 33's well-wall ceiling on w2 (only a sector trades it for the case shape). The
count is the ARRANGEMENT's, not a solve's, because what binds it — the alarm link's measured site — is cut
long after the train; so the arrangement names the fewest pairs at which its scan found that site held, the
way §308 landing 5 named 80° and 36°. Measured, round, at the 60° module:

**The module's turn.** At 80° the link's measured site runs through 12 o'clock, and the idler line refuses it
(−0.418). Scanned with six idlers, round: 55° and 60° are silent apart from the fixed point below; 65°, 70° and

the dogleg, TODO 174's fork and, from 110°, §163's post. 60° is taken: it carries the strike group off the width
on every form (below), and the pusher stays at the 10 o'clock as worn.

**The arming fixed point is the arrangement's.** `ALARM_PINB_SEAT_MN` is the shared equal margin TODO 222's
solve re-derives at the link (B* = √(250 / (a + K₁))), quoted to its fixed point. Both a and K₁ read the link's
site and the guide triangle about the ring, so an arrangement that moves the link moves it: the designed 5.2780
read 5.1554 against a need of 5.1792 here. It is now `ALARM_PINB_SEAT_FIXED`, keyed by the arrangement (the key
the gong seats use, `ALARM_ARR_KEY`, which `GONG_ARR_KEY` now reads: configKey with the case shape, its fit and
the seat mode dropped). The arrangement's row is 5.1837, found by secant on two boots (5.17 → seat 5.1877, need
5.1843; 5.18 → 5.1848, 5.1838). A spec with no row takes the designed value and the existing assert says so.
