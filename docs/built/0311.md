## §311 — The form dial's railroad track follows the case: the outline's own inward offset, corners held at the case's round, rungs on the hands' rays

Owner: "redesign the railroad track to match the dial and case shape. Avoid sharp
corners that a real railroad can't traverse." Since §307 a form face (`?case=rect`,
`cushion`, `tonneau`) printed the ROUND chemin de fer on its rectangular ground — the
1930s convention, named there as a prototype's placeholder with "a rectangular track"
filed as landing 3's. This landing is that track. The round watch does not move: the
dial's canvas call log and its geometry bytes are identical to the pristine builder's in
Node (1213 calls, same sha256 both; same geometry sha256 both), the round build's
fingerprint is 4180065205 (64 units, 13 poses) on a virgin boot of the base tree and of
this one, the round boot is silent, and the ink readings on the round face are unchanged
(11.02 / 10.79 : 1). The three fitted form builds boot with the warnings they had (2, 4, 2).

### The law (`dialTrack`, geometry.js)

A circle is the one curve that is its own offset, so the round track had never had to say
what it was: two circles at `DIAL_RAIL_OUT_F` and `DIAL_RAIL_IN_F` of the printed disc.
Read as a relation to the EDGE the dial is cut to, those are two gauges — the outer rail
stands `gOut = radius·(1 − 2·DIAL_CANVAS_FILL_F·DIAL_RAIL_OUT_F)` inside the rim (5.613 u
at the shipped dial), the inner `gIn` (8.287 u) — exactly as §307 ran the chapter ring's
gauge round the form loop. The form track is therefore the outline's own INWARD OFFSETS
by those two gauges, through `caseShapeOf`'s `grown` (a true offset: the straights stay
parallel to the case's sides at the round face's distance, the long arcs concentric with
the case's), and the round face is the circle's case of the same law — the probe's first
control holds that at a circle of the dial's radius the two offsets ARE the shipped rails,
to 1e-9, and the hand's reach the shipped fraction.

**No sharp corner.** A rect's and a tonneau's corners are rounded only by the rim margin
(`PLATE_RIM_MARGIN`, 5 u), and both gauges are past it: the exact offset carries the outer
rail round a 0 u corner and the inner one round a −3.3 u one — a cusp, which no rail can
be bent to and no wheel can roll round. So a corner is HELD at the outline's own round:
`grown(g, { cornerR })` (layout.js, new) offsets the straights and the long arcs exactly
and re-sites a corner arc of the held radius tangent to both (the tonneau's corner centre
by the base's own construction, `rc` inside the offset end and `R − rc` from the side's
centre; its straight end shortens, 37.22 → 32.69 u half-run at the outer rail on the shipped tonneau). The
track turns no tighter than the case it follows — the smoothest turn the face has to
offer, and the one number for it that is not typed. At a held corner the rails stand
farther from the rim than on the straights (√2·g at a rect's corner), which is the form
dial's look and the consequence, not an input. The cushion has no corner primitive: its
curvature is the curve's own, both offsets are smooth (least radius 12.58 u on the outer
rail, 9.90 u on the inner, measured), and the hold is a no-op there.

A railway's own rule — a curve of so many gauges' radius — was weighed and refused: the
drawn track's gauge is 2.67 u and a main line's tightest curve is tens of gauges, which
no form dial can carry and still be its case. What the request means on a dial is a
rail with no cusp and a bounded turn, and the bound is the case's.

**The rungs lie on the hands' rays.** Minute i is the ray from the hands' axis at 6°·i,
and its rung runs along that ray from the inner rail's crossing to the outer's (the
shape's own `exit`, which measures from the frame's origin — the hands' axis, since the
shape is built in the dial's frame). The hand that reads it is a radial line, the rung
continues that line, and two rays from one centre never meet, so no two rungs can cross
however tight the corner. A rung square to the rails — a railway's sleeper — is the SAME
line on the round face, where the normal is the radius; the two conventions part exactly
where the hand stops being the rails' normal, and the hand is what the rung is for. (The
sleeper convention was measured too: at a 5 u corner with a 2.67 u gauge the inner ends
bunch but do not cross, so it is refused for the hand's line, not for a crossing.) Along a
straight side the rungs are unevenly spaced along the rail, which every honest form dial
shows. Same ink, same weights, same caps as the round track's; the loops are drawn at
`TRACK_SEGS` = 256 chords per turn, a held 5 u corner's sagitta 3.8e-4 u, under a tenth
of a pixel at the print's scale.

### What reads it

- **main.js** builds the shape in the dial's frame (`FORM_DIAL`: the fitted box through
  dialFace's Y-flip about the hands' axis — exact, every kind being symmetric about its
  box's axes) and passes `dialTrack`'s answer to `makeDial` as `track`. `makeDial` paints
  it in place of the round track, holds the caller's two frames to one outline (every
  outline vertex on the track's base shape within the cushion's polyline accuracy, a boot
  assert), and publishes it as `userData.track`; `__clock.dialTrack` and the dial spec's
  `track` carry it for the instruments.
- **The ink** is solved against the rails' grounds as before, now at the track's two
  radial extremes (the outer rail's farthest and the inner rail's nearest approach to the
  axis, as fractions of the printed disc; past the disc `dialTintAt` reads the rim tone the
  sheet pads with). ONE list, `railFracs`, read by the paint, the §157 gate and
  `inkContrast` alike; on the round face it is the two constants, byte for byte. Measured
  on the rect: 11.22 / 10.99 : 1 (round 11.02 / 10.79).
- **The minute hand** is derived from the track as before ("tip on the rungs"): on a form
  face that is where the mid-rail comes NEAREST the hands' axis — the middle of a side —
  read as the mid-rail shape's own signed distance to the axis (`nearestMid`), so the tip
  lands on the track there and falls inside it everywhere else, as every form watch's
  minute hand does. 36.63 u on the rect, 37.62 on the cushion and on the tonneau,
  against the round 34.57 (the round expression is untouched; a circle gives it exactly).
- **The wells.** The solver bounds both wells by the ROUND inner rail (`printFrame`): that
  is conservative on a form face only while the form inner rail stands outside the round
  one everywhere, which is asserted at boot (`nearestInner` ≥ 33.23 u: 35.29 rect, 36.28
  cushion and tonneau) and held by the probe. And a form build no longer passes the
  track's OUTER rail: §308 L4's two outside-the-chapter well laws grew a well from the
  round outer rail into the free corner the round chapter left, and a track that follows
  the case leaves no such corner — every station is inside the track, the solver takes the
  chapter law for all of them, and a corner station (`?rsvaz=` past the window) is refused
  by that law's own warn rather than cut through the rails. The track is never cut by a
  well, the round face's rule (the seconds well "yields only the railroad").

### Instrument

`tools/probe-form-track.mjs` (acceptance, pure Node): per build — the round circle, then
every fitted form case `gong-seats.js` lists, in the dial's frame — the gauges are the
round face's and the rails never stand nearer the rim (G, exact on the shape's own axes);
each rail is a simple convex loop whose least radius of curvature (the circumradius
through each vertex, which on a sampled arc IS the arc's radius) is at least the case's
corner round, or strictly positive on the cushion (S); sixty rungs on the rays, each
ending on both rails to the shape's accuracy and within the drawn stroke's own sagitta,
no two crossing (R); the hand's reach the mid-rail's nearest approach and under every
rung's mid-point (H); the inner rail outside the round inner rail (W). Controls: C1 the
law at a circle is the round print; C2 the un-held offset fails the smoothness floor on
the rect (corner 0) and the tonneau (−3.287); C3 two rungs laid across each other count.
Measured on landing: 49 PASS, 0 FAIL.
