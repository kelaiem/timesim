## §329 — A trailing-side winding arrest: the fusee stop's beak on the chain-free side, a reversing lever, designed in a line — PARTIAL: landing 1 (the line), landing 2a (the site, measured: no site seats), landing 3 (option B, a Geneva stop-work: the line and its site), landing 4 (the stop-work built behind `?arrest=stopwork`; the alarm-off lands preview up to 5.9% smaller with the drum turned)

Out of §75 step 1. Turning the mainspring drum to 125–135° on the lands preview shrinks the free case's fitted area by 3.3–5.9% with
the drum's own sweeps clean, and §47's winding arrest is all that stands in the way. Its single finger reads the arriving coil with
its pad and throws its beak inward in front of the fusee's lug. With the drum turned, the chain's departure covers every beak station
on the side that finger throws inward (§75's record has the scan). This entry designs the arrest that would not need that side.
Landing 1 is the design alone, in a line, in free space. Nothing in the build reads it yet.

### The premise, measured first

§75's record called the pad's far side "chain-free" from a trace that never tested it there: on that side the throw-sense and
arm-length tests reject a station before the chain test runs. So landing 1 first measured the chain at EVERY beak station on both
sides, at the tab's z band over the whole wind (a throwaway build, not kept), with the drum at the shipped direction, 125° and 135°:

| drum | pad | lever side (§47's inward throw) | far side |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped | 5° | 24 free steps, then chain to the end | all 105 free |
| 135° | 1–4.5° (14 pads) | chain at every step | all 105 free |
| 135° | 316–334° | chain except the last 18–34 | chain for 17–33 steps, then free |
| 125° | 7.5–15.5° | 9–16 free at the pad (inside the arm-too-short zone), then chain | all 105 free |

So the far side is chain-free where the drum's new direction puts the pads, and the premise holds.

### Why two levers, and why these two were refused on paper

- **A mirrored finger** (its stud moved to the far side) throws inward there, but reverses the stop. In radial and tangential
  components the lug's push on the beak gives a moment `a_r·f` about the stud, which a mirror does not change, while the throw's sign
  flips. The lug would then peel the beak out, the "disengaging" failure §47 asserts against.
- **A shorter lever on the inward side** would use the 9–16 free steps at 125°. That shrinks a lever arm to fit, which the design
  priority forbids (P3 is never paid for out of P0–P1), and it gives nothing at 135°.

So the finger keeps its job, reading the coil on its stud, and the beak moves to a **second lever on its own stud**, driven in
reverse by a **pin on the finger's tail in a slot in the beak lever** (the owner's pick, 2026-11-02, over a two-pin link and geared
sectors).

### The line as landing 1 drew it (superseded by landing 2a, below)

> **Landing 2a found two things wrong here.** The levers must turn TOGETHER, not against each other, and the beak lever must turn
> LESS than the finger. The module now holds the corrected line and the probe judges that; the text below is landing 1 as it shipped.

```
       pad (lifts by PAD_LIFT, −x)               beak (throws +x, inward at the lug)
         │                                         ╲  tilted β past the lug's force line
         │ R_PAD_ARM                                ╲ L2
  S1 ────●──── r1 ──── pin ═══[ slot ]═══ r2 ────────● S2
     finger (turns +ψ1)                   beak lever (turns −ψ2)
```

Every row is derived from a constraint, written in the module beside it:

- **The reference is §47's**: `R_PAD_ARM` 1.25, `PAD_LIFT` 0.36 (the probe holds them equal to `main.js`' literals), and the bite §47
  delivers on the default build (`ENGAGE` 0.336), which this arrest must match, not merely clear §47's 0.15 floor.
- **ψ1 = asin(PAD_LIFT / R_PAD_ARM) = 0.2921**: the finger's turn when the pad's face moves `PAD_LIFT` across its arm.
- **The pin rides the slot exactly**: ψ2 = atan2(r1·sin ψ1, d − r1·cos ψ1) with d = r1 + r2, which gives **−0.2636**, a ratio of 0.902.
- **r1 1.0128 and r2 1.0378**, each at its floor: a hub (`PIVOT_MIN_U` bore, 0.01 running clearance, `STOCK_MIN_U` wall), then a
  `STOCK_MIN_U` wall, then the pin (or the slot's flank and half the running clearance).
- **One spring, on the beak lever**, biasing it to rest on its own bank. Through the slot it holds the pin, and the finger with it, on
  the slot's driving flank at every pose. So the slot's running clearance is never lost motion, and the finger needs no bank of its
  own. The first draft hung the spring on the finger instead. The beak lever then floated by 0.0865 and its parked beak could stand
  0.0835 from the lug's orbit, under `CLEAR_MARGIN`. That is why the spring moved.
- **β = −ψ2 − asin(μ·r_pivot / L2) = 0.2445**: at full throw the lug's force (tangential, toward S2) passes outside the beak pivot's
  friction circle (`MU_STEEL`·`PIVOT_MIN_U` = 0.0369) on the side that turns the beak deeper. The stop's load then lifts the slot's
  flank off the pin, and the slot carries the throw, never the stop.
- **L2 = 1.9373**, solved so the throw reaches the parked clearance (`CLEAR_MARGIN` + 0.02, §47's `BEAK_PARKED_R` − `LUG_OUTER`) plus
  the bite: **throw 0.506, bite 0.336, overall gain 1.406**. That is 1.55 pad arms, inside §47's 1.1–2.2 ratio band, inherited here as
  an envelope.
- **The pin's pressure angle** at full throw is 31.8°, inside the friction cone (90° − atan μ = 78.7°, past which the pin would lock in
  the slot). The slot is 0.501 long: the pin's 0.082 of travel along it, plus its diameter, plus the running clearance.

### Landing 2a (2026-11-02): the site, measured — no site seats, and §329 is paused

`?arrest=trailing` (`SPEC.arrest`) asks §47's own solve, for every pad candidate it already finds, where the trailing arrest's beak lever
could stand, publishing the answer on `__clock.windArrest.trailing` (the best site, or the count of why every try failed). It builds no
metal: the key is nulled in every table keyed on the configuration (the alarm arrangement, the gong seats, the case selector, the free
outline), so a build with it fingerprints exactly as one without (default 2990090130, lands preview 1213031771, the same warnings).
The solve reads §47's own laws — the chain's occupancy at the tab band, the arm stop (the wrap's reach, floored by the lug's orbit),
the flying span, the fusee window's sectors, the chain's reach at a stud's head — so it cannot disagree with §47 about where anything is.

It refused the line three times, each time for a measured reason:

1. **The sense.** Landing 1's pin sat BETWEEN the studs, so the levers counter-rotated. On the lands preview at drum 135° every
   far-side station able to throw the full 0.506 (the best reached 0.5092) put the stop's moment in the PEELING sense: 21,874 tries
   refused on the sign, none on the size, none reaching a clearance. In this movement the finger turns with `MOVEMENT_SENSE` and the lug
   pushes the beak away from the pad, so an engaging stop needs the beak lever to turn WITH the finger. Landing 1's row K ("the levers
   counter-rotate") described the line's drawing frame, not the watch.
2. **The short arm.** With the pin beyond S2 (S1 ─ S2 ─ pin) the levers co-rotate, but the beak lever turns more than the finger
   (ratio 2.03), so its arm is short (0.8945). Held near the force line by the engaging condition, that keeps S2 within about 4.5 u of
   the cone: every site's hub rim stood inside the arm stop (49,435 tries at drum 135°, 57,425 at 125°, 58,755 at the shipped
   direction), and no other test was reached.
3. **The radial band.** So the finger's stud went BETWEEN the beak lever's stud and the pin (S2 ═ S1 ─ pin): the levers co-rotate, the
   beak lever turns less, and its arm is long (3.75 at the most compact member). The line became a FAMILY in the arm length, with the
   gain, throw, bite, engaging stop, pressure angle and floors held, and the fold picking the smallest arm that seats (the owner's
   rule). Every turn is then reachable, but nothing seats:

   | why each try failed (lands preview) | drum 135° | drum 125° | shipped direction |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | arm too short for the turn (ψ2 would reach ψ1) | 116,922 | 135,824 | 138,970 |
   | S2's hub rim inside the arm stop | 107,525 | 124,086 | 125,923 |
   | a tail chord (S1→pin, S2→pin) inside the arm stop | 270,223 | 314,730 | 323,057 |
   | pin, span, webs, head | not reached | not reached | not reached |

   At the levers' height the arm stop stands near 4.43 u and §47's stud near 4.96 u. A second lever whose slotted tail must cross the
   finger's stud runs almost tangentially through that half-unit band, and with its half-width it dips inside the stop.

**What is left open, and why §329 is paused here.** Under the bracket there is no room for the beak lever ABOVE the finger (`PIN_GAP`
is less than `STOCK_MIN_U`). The one untried place is BELOW it, in the band the stud heads hang in, where the arm stop is a different
law (the wrap's reach at a lower height, and whatever else stands there). That band is unmeasured. The owner chose to ship the
finding and pause (2026-11-02). The drum stays at its shipped direction, §47's arrest is unchanged, and `?arrest=trailing` stays as
the instrument the next attempt starts from.

**The line as it now stands** (`src/trailing-arrest.js`, held by `tools/probe-329-arrest-line.mjs`, seven rows each with a control):
S2 ═ S1 ─ pin, co-rotating. ψ2 = atan2(r1·sin ψ1, r1·cos ψ1 + e). The thrown arm stands ε = asin(ρ/L2) past the force line, so
ψ2 = ε + asin((throw − ρ)/L2) and e = r1·sin ψ1 / tan ψ2 − r1·cos ψ1. The compact member (e at `D_MIN` 1.1728, r1 at `R1_MIN` 1.0128)
gives ψ2 0.1353 (ratio 0.463), L2 3.749, β −0.1254, throw 0.506, bite 0.336, gain 1.406 (held in §47's 1.1–2.2 band, now on the gain),
and a pin pressure angle of 9.0°. The spring stays on the beak lever: unsprung, the beak would park 0.0833 from the lug's orbit.

### Landing 2b (2026-11-02): the layer below the finger is worse, measured — the second-lever design has no seat in z

The one place landing 2a left untried was the band under the finger. The wrap's reach (the arm stop's law: every chain point within
`CLEAR_MARGIN` of a lever band, the sphere rule `ARM_BAND_REACH` uses, over the whole wind) was measured at the finger's own band and at
bands stepped down from it, a throwaway read of the built chain, nothing kept. It is the same on the default build and the lands preview
(the chain's wrap does not depend on the drum's direction):

| band (lever stock `STOCK_MIN_U` 0.317 thick) | top z | arm stop (wrap reach) | room to §47's stud (~4.96) |
|---|---|---|---|
| the finger's own | 7.469 | 4.429 | 0.53 |
| one `PIN_GAP` (0.215) below it | 6.937 | 4.736 | 0.22 |
| a further 0.5 down | 6.437 | 5.099 | none (stud inside the stop) |
| a further 1.0 down | 5.937 | 5.624 | none |

**Lower is fatter**: the wrap climbs 1/`FUSEE_GROOVE_TURNS` of the band per turn, so the lower the band, the more turns stand within its
reach. The half-unit band between stop and stud that rejected every site at the finger's height narrows to 0.22 one gap down and closes
two layers down. A second lever's slotted tail must cross the finger's stud, so it needs MORE room than the finger's own, not less. So no
layer, above or below, seats the line: §329's design, a second lever on its own stud driven through a pin in a slot, does not exist in this
movement's z stack. That settles the open question 2a left, and it settles it against the mechanism rather than against a layer.

**What would be a different arrest, not a different layer** (none designed): a beak on the finger's own stud and lever (no second
stud to cross) with a reversing member that does not cross the stud, a stop on the cone's far side that does not use the pad at all (a
spring-held pawl on the lug, reading the cone's wind by its own cam), or moving the coil's departure instead (the span's corridor is what
fills the beak's arc). Each is a new mechanism designed in a line first.

### Landing 3 (2026-11-02): option B, a count and not a cam — a Geneva stop-work, designed in a line (no site yet)

The owner chose to try a pawl that reads the cone itself before moving the chain's departure. **The first sketch of it was wrong.** A cam
on the cone repeats every turn and cannot tell turn one from turn two, and the state the arrest has to read is the wind, which is
`FUSEE_WRAP_TURNS` of the cone's own rotation (1.75 at the 30 h default). So the arrest has to COUNT turns, and the classic count is a
Geneva stop-work: a finger pin on the cone carries a star one slot per revolution, and a star with closed slots stops the finger at
either end. It never touches the chain, so the chain's departure corridor that fills the beak's arc is not a constraint on it.

`src/stopwork-arrest.js` (pure) and `tools/probe-329b-stopwork-line.mjs` (acceptance, each row with a control):

- **Geometry (G).** With the pin at radius r1 on the cone's axis and the star's pivot at a = r1 / sin(π/n), the pin enters every slot
  along it (alignment 1 to 4e-16) and the star turns ±π/n across the engagement, E = π − 2π/n of finger angle. Control: a pin radius 5%
  over the law enters at 0.99875.
- **The count (Q).** With m open and n − m ≥ 1 closed slots in one run, the finger travels (m + 1) revolutions less E between the two
  stops. A discrete simulation of the star (forward and backward passages, a closed slot stopping) agrees with that closed form for five
  (m, n) pairs. Control: a star with no closed slot never stops.
- **The default wind (T).** 30 h reserve is 1.75 turns, and **n = 4, m = 1 (three slots closed) travels exactly 1.75 turns with zero
  trim** (a = 5.959 and star tip 4.549 for the lug's orbit, 4.214 on the lands preview). That is a property of the default reserve, not a design
  choice: 2 − E/2π is 1.75 at n = 4.
- **Floors (F).** The lock disc has a window (reaches the star's tip circle, clears the slots' pin circle), 1.41–3.88 at n = 4, and
  the slots' inner end leaves a hub (0.991 against 0.501). n = 3 fails that floor (0.160), which removes the 48 h reserve.

**What this does not do, and the limits it found:**

1. **It covers few reserves.** The travel is quantised, (m + 1) − E/2π, and the stop face can come earlier than the mouth by only 0.033
   revolution. Of the 13 reserves the spec allows (12–48 h) it reaches 30 h (n = 4), 45 h (n = 8) and 27 h (n = 12, a star 12.4 from
   the axis, not buildable); the other ten have no stop-work in this family. A reserve outside the covered set would keep §47's arrest.
   Getting the rest needs an unequal star or a finger ratio, which is not designed.
2. **It adds a run-down stop that §47 does not have.** The star stops the finger at BOTH ends, so the cone also locks at the end of
   the reserve, with the going spring's residual torque held by the star. The fusee equalises that end's torque, so this is a new load
   case, not an extension of the wind-end one.
3. **No site** (landing 3 held the line only; landing 3b, below, measures one).

**But the probe left the cone out, and the cone is in the way.** It excluded the fusee as the lock disc's seat. The star's tip circle

**Open, in order:** the relief (which of the two, and that the cone's top keeps its strength), then the star's post (a seat hung from
the three-quarter plate, as §47's bracket is), the pin and star at the going spring's stall torque, the run-down stop, replacing §47's
finger, beak, lug and bracket behind a key, and the battery at the moved point. Nothing is built.

### Landing 4 (2026-11-03): the stop-work, built behind `?arrest=stopwork` — the alarm-off case up to 5.9% smaller

**The owner's pick changed with a measurement.** Landing 3b's two ways out were a relief pocket in the cone's top or the top band as a
separate finger wheel. Measured on the lands preview: the cone's top face is at z 7.945 and the three-quarter plate's underside at 8.404,
with nothing between them but the fusee's top staff (radius 0.5); the chain's top coil at full wind reaches 7.902. So the star does not
need the cone's band at all: it turns in that gap, over a finger wheel fixed on the cone's top face, and the cone is not cut. The gap
(0.459) is less than the star's §50 stock (0.317) plus two `CLEAR_MARGIN`s, so the star rides on two **running gaps** of 0.071, one
above and one below. Those are its own mechanism's contacts: it rides its driver's face and hangs from its own support. The owner
accepted that (2026-11-03).

**What `?arrest=stopwork` builds** (`src/main.js`, a branch inside §47's block, which it leaves before §47's solve; none of §47's
finger, beak, lug or bracket is built). Every dimension is derived where it is built:

- **The pin** stands in the cone's top face at the largest radius whose root (a §50 stock of cone metal round it, a stock deep) is
  still cone metal at its own azimuth. The groove runs out through that face, so this rejects about 300 of 720 azimuths. Its floor
  `R1_LO` keeps the star's teeth (they come to a − rTip from the axis) a margin off the top staff. On the lands preview r1 = 2.652.
- **The star** is the line's choice for this wind at that orbit: n = 4, m = 1, travel 1.75 turns = `FUSEE_WRAP_TURNS`, trim 0. Its
  pivot is at a = 3.750 from the cone's axis and its tip at 2.986. It has one open slot, three closed notches whose floors are the stops,
  and four lock arcs at the lock disc's radius plus the running fit. Its stud is **planted in plate metal**: a web of the fusee
  window, at the azimuth whose disc and head stand clearest of the chain over the whole wind: 21.5°, the best of the 54 of
  720 azimuths that both root the pin and stand on plate metal (the chain refused none), 2.16 clear on the drum-130° build.
- **The lock disc** sits on the cone's top face. Its radius is the line's `lockHi` (2.317), the largest that clears the pin's
  circle by the margin, and its relief is carved from the star's own sweep through the engagement, at the running fit. Two
  measurements shaped it. A relief cut at `CLEAR_MARGIN` ate the disc under the lock arcs at both ends of the passage and left the star
  unlocked over 228° of the finger's 540° of dwell, so it is cut at the running fit. And the star's teeth come to 0.764 from the axis,
  inside the 0.817 a hub ring round the staff would need, so the disc has no hub: it is fast on the cone's face and **open 41°** where
  the sweep reaches the bore (a C), one opening, the sliver the open slot's shadow left standing between two cuts merged into it.
- **The pose** is the Geneva law, from the cone's own wind: finger angle from the tension, the star's turn summed over the passages.

**Held at boot** (derivations over the pose law, since boot has no pose): through the passage the pin's centre lies on the open slot's
centreline, and at both stops it sits on a closed notch's centreline at its floor plus its radius (each within 1e-9). The disc holds
the star through every dwell: there is disc metal under the lock arc on both sides of the azimuth it faces. A concave arc spans ±52.8°
about the cone's axis here, and the measured unlocked arc is 0°. The default spec with `?arrest=stopwork` boots **silent**.

**The inspector** (`src/inspect.js`) swaps §47's unit for the stop-work's under the same key, on `FRAME_LANDS`' pattern. In
`MECH_GRAPH` the stud is supported by the three-quarter plate and the fusee drives the star. `EXPECTED_PAIRS` gains star ⇄ plate and
star ⇄ fusee, and every table naming 'Winding arrest' drops those rows. `UNIT_GROUPS` and the tour stop name the new unit. §47's A2
chain-to-plate measurement moved up the block, unchanged, so it runs on both builds.
