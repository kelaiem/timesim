## §328 — Integrated lands for the default round build, and sinks with lands on both faces — PARTIAL: step 1 shipped, lands the default (step 1b)

Out of the owner's request (2026-11-01): the free-outline case supports the back plate on integral screw columns
raised from the base plate (§325 landing 6, `?frame=lands`), and the owner wanted the same on the **default round
build**, with raised metal eventually on **both faces** of the base plate holding parts inset into it, as a CNC
machine makes a movement. **Applied pillars stay**, as the default and as a supported construction; this is a second
style, selectable. Of TODO 271 and 272 only the CNC construction carries over: the outline-inset scan and the
strike-footprint yield are form-case work and do not apply to a circle. The plan has three steps: lands on the round
build (step 1, then step 1b: lands the default on every case kind), lands on the dial face, and sinks in a thick base
plate. Steps 2 and 3 are still in the roadmap.

### Step 1: the round build on integrated lands (2026-11-01)

`?frame=lands` now builds on the round case as well as the free one. The rect and the other form kinds still read
it to null, because their seat scans are TODO 271's and their lands were never measured. With the key absent nothing
moves: the default fingerprint is **1995847421** before and after, and the change is gated on the key everywhere.

- **The gate.** Two conditions: `layout.js` reads `frame` on `caseShape === 'free' || !caseShape`, and `main.js`'s
  `FRAME_LANDS` on `FORM?.kind === 'free' || !FORM`. `inspect.js` needs nothing. Every table landing 6 conditioned
  (the `MECH_GRAPH` support rows, `FASTENED_EDGES`, `EXPECTED_PAIRS`, `PLATE_SEATS`) already keys on `SPEC.frame`, which
  layout.js nulls wherever the build does not read it.
- **The seats: the pillar's own.** Landing 6's lands take the outermost seat that holds on the nearest bearing,
  because the free outline has room on its rim that a pillar, owed the case's margin, could not use. On the round
  ring that rule moved the 45° seat 3.76 round the ring. A round land therefore takes the scan's own deepest-seat choice:
  it stands exactly on the pillar's axis with the pillar's cap disc (`PILLAR_CAP_R`), so every later siting solve
  reads the same `pillarSeats` and the frame moves nothing else. Seats: (28.739, 24.115), (−34.306, 9.192),
  (−25.114, −25.114), (25.696, −4.070).
- **Finding 1: `PLATE_RIM` read a land as the rim, and boot died.** The walk measures the rim's back face as the
  highest base-plate metal within 1 mm of `plateR` (`rSeat`). That face is where the case seats and where the gong's
  annulus floor stands (`GONG_RIM_Z`). A round seat plus `PILLAR_CAP_R` reaches inside that band, so the land's top
  (z 8.40, `TQ_BOT_Z`) became the rim face. `GONG_POST_LEN` then went negative, the gong's post radius came out NaN, and
  `buildAlarm` threw in `GONG_DESIGN`. On the free case the outline's inset had kept lands out of the band.
  - **The fix is a measurement scope, not a move:** a land carries `userData.rimLand` and the rim walk skips it, because
    a land is the plate's metal but not its rim.
  - **The other fix, refused.** Holding lands inside the band (`plateR − 1 mm − CLEAR_MARGIN`) also boots, but it moved
    two seats and with them the alarm's siting.
  - **What the band is for is held directly.** The case clamp screws stand 16.457 off the nearest land.
- **Finding 2: the arming fixed point's key carried `frame`.** `ALARM_ARR_KEY` dropped `caseShape`, `hands` and
  `strapMm` but not `frame`, so the lands build missed the default arrangement's row (5.2248 mN), took the designed
  5.2780, and the link's assert warned (`TODO 206 arming spring … need 5.2223`). With `frame: null` in the key the boot
  is silent. That silence is the evidence: the assert re-derives the margin at the link off this build's metal. The
  same drop reaches `GONG_ARR_KEY`, which reads `ALARM_ARR_KEY`. The only form build carrying `frame` is the free lands
  preview, which runs `alarm=0`.
- **The fitted numerals stay a form-face feature.** `fitMarkers` reads `FRAME_LANDS && FORM`, so the round dial keeps
  its band.
- **`__clock.plateRim`** publishes the rim as measured (front, back, reach), so the probe reads it directly.

**The instrument.** `tools/probe-328-round-lands.mjs` (acceptance) exists because a land is a child of the HELD base
plate, in no pair sweep. The one gate that measures foreign metal against it, `plateSeats`, excuses any mesh with a
`PLATE_SEATS` row, the case clamp screws among them. The probe checks:

- the roster: `pillars` on the default and four lands without it;
- each land on a pillar's axis, to 1e-9, read across two boots;
- `PLATE_RIM` equal to the default's, with the land tops standing 8.40 above its back face as the control that a walk
  reading them would move it;
- every labelled mesh against each land over §152's 52-pose net, no excuse but the back plate's own screw set. The
  must-hit control is the screw shanks in contact with all four lands; the must-fire control carries land 0 its gap
  plus `CLEAR_MARGIN` toward its nearest foreign mesh, which must then read under the margin and read its gap again
  when restored.

Measured: ALL PASS. The nearest foreign metal is `Alarm striking wheel / alarmGovWheel` at 0.275 from land 0, then
`Alarm hammer / alarmHammerHead` at 0.307.

**The spec point.** `round-lands` (`frame=lands`, `expect: 'silent'`, `sweep: true`) joins `SPEC_POINTS`, so TODO 186's

**The explainer** has a new compact entry, *The frame — applied pillars, or lands raised from the plate*. It is the
page's first account of the frame, and it is untranslated until a locale landing picks it up; the page check reports

### Step 1b: lands are the default frame on every case kind (2026-11-02)

The owner asked for the lands to become the default for all case shapes once the probe gated in CI (#857). `frame` null
now builds the back plate on the base plate's lands on every kind, and `?frame=pillars` builds the four applied pillars,
kept as a supported style. Null rather than a name keeps every default build's configKey and URL the same string; an old
`?frame=lands` link reads the default it asked for. `?frame=pillars` boots the old default's fingerprint, **1995847421**,
bit for bit, so the movement every gate measured until now is still one URL away and is swept (below).

- **The seat rule, per kind.** Every kind but the free outline scans its lands on the pillar's terms (the rim term the
  pillar owes, `PLATE_RIM_MARGIN` on a form case; the scan's deepest-seat choice), so on round, rect, cushion, tonneau and
  fitted a land stands on the pillar's seat and nothing downstream moves. The free outline keeps §325 landing 6's rule
  (the outermost seat on the nearest bearing, owed only `TQ_LAND_MIN`), which it needed: a pillar there had no 315° seat.
  The cushion and tonneau defaults find no 315° seat for a land either, exactly as their pillars find none today (the
  same pre-existing warning), so they stand on three.
- **Boots, measured** on thirteen spec points against `main`: the round default, the designed train, `reconf=1`, a stud
  variant, the two-leg route, both rect points, and plain cushion, tonneau, fitted and free each boot exactly the warning
  set `main` does. Fingerprints move (the `pillars` unit is gone), the warnings do not.
- **The free outlines** (`src/free-outlines.js`, `tools/free-outline.mjs`). Re-solved from scratch (`--fresh`), the two
  default free rows (alarm on and off) came back byte-identical: the pillars never bound those outlines. The −140°
  lands preview's row was keyed with `"frame":"lands"`, a key the build no longer forms; it moves to the null key with
  its outline byte-identical (re-solved seeded from the committed row; a `--fresh` solve settles on a second fixed point,
  877.58 mm² against 884.39, and the committed row passes `--check` on `main`, so the table keeps the outline the page
  already cut). Net diff: one line.
- **Finding: `?frame=pillars` lost its gong seat.** `GONG_SPEC_KEY` (the key the form cases' generated seats are looked up
  by) kept `frame`, so `?frame=pillars` was refused its seat on every form case, lost the seat's `keep`, and the rect's
  315° pillar stood at (25.680, −4.067) in the strike group's corner where the lands default stands at (5.010, −35.650).
  `tools/gong-seats.mjs` solves the seat with the frame out of its map (pillars and lands alike) and the frame yields to
  the keep, so `frame` is out of the key (and out of the case selector's copy of it): the twins now share every seat, and
  the rect twin boots `main`'s own form-rect fingerprint, 2800210639.
- **Finding: the chain was never an obstacle to a support.** The seat scan read the train's arbors and the drum as boxes
  and never the fusee chain between them. Pillars got away with it (far from the run on round and form cases, interior
  seats on the free outline, and in every pair sweep as a unit); the free outline's lands, taking the outermost seat in
  no pair sweep, did not: the probe found land 1 inside the chain's run (0.0000 at the build pose). The scan now samples
  the run the way the beak arms' fold scan does (`chainLayoutAt` over the wind at a quarter pitch, the link plate's
  envelope per sample) and holds every support off it. Measured: no seat moves on round, rect, cushion, tonneau or fitted
  (`?frame=pillars` still boots 1995847421); the free lands re-seat to (28.638, 28.638), (−22.500, 38.971),
  (−23.688, −23.688), (25.680, −4.067), and the free outlines still hold the metal. The sampling loop carries one new boot
  seam (374), and `src/boot-progress.js` is regenerated.
- **Spec points.** Step 1's `round-lands` (`frame=lands`) became the identity and is retired; `pillars` (`frame=pillars`,
  silent, swept) keeps the applied pillars validated. Its `POINT_COSTS` row (587 s) is unmeasured until a run writes it.
- **Instruments.** `tools/probe-328-round-lands.mjs` becomes `tools/probe-328-lands.mjs`: the round, rect (the `form-rect`
  point) and free builds, the first two against their `?frame=pillars` twins (roster, seats to 1e-9, `PLATE_RIM` to 1e-9,
  no boot warning beyond the twin's), every land held off every labelled mesh over the 52-pose net with its must-hit and
  must-fire controls (the must-fire now stepped toward the nearest mesh with metal in the land's own height band: a part above
  it, the case's back crystal, closes no gap), and the round case's clamp screws by name. ALL PASS, 226 s uncontended;
  the `battery probes` job's cap, and the battery's wait on it, move 9 → 13 min by its stated rule (26 + 194 + 226 s ×
  1.66 = 12.3). `tools/probe-184-frame-joints.mjs` boots both frames
  and holds each roster (lands: four plate screws into the lands and the three cocks' screws; pillars: those plus the
  four riveted tenons). `gong-bench-data.mjs` and `probe-310-gong-seat.mjs` read a land as a support where they read a
  pillar (same seat, same cap); `gong-seats.mjs` already leaves the frame out of its strike map.
