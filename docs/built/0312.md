## §312 — A LEGO alarm clock cell, landings L1 and L1b: the modules on one base, each shared axle one keyed body; the escapement driven through a transfer

**Why.** `technic.html` showed the alarm COMPLICATION in LEGO and, beside it, a going
train, an escapement, a fusee and the keyless and motion works, each on its own
frame. It was not an alarm CLOCK: nothing joined one module's output to the next
module's input. §312 is that clock as one more cell, in five landings (the plan is
in the private roadmap; the scoping note is its `docs/LEGO_ALARM_CLOCK_FEASIBILITY.md`).
This landing, L1, is the joining: one base, and every axle that passes from one
module into the next ONE keyed body. Nothing is driven yet. The cell is MODELLED and
DISPLAY ONLY, and every place it is described says so.

**The declaration.** `TECHNIC_CLOCK` in `src/technic-clock.js` names each LINE as the
two bodies it joins, by the names the modules' own builds use:

- **centre**: the fusee's centre pinion IS the centre→fourth row's first arbor (the
  steel's centre wheel, 1 rev/h);
- **fourth**: that row's output IS the fourth→escape row's input (1 rev/min);
- **escape**: that row's output IS the escapement's escape arbor;
- **minute**: the keyless works' minute axle (the setting branch's output, the hour
  wheel's bearing) IS the alarm's hour axle (the release chain's input). The two
  names differ because landing I named the axle for what it feeds. It turns once an
  hour, the minute hand's rate.

The modules are their own cells' builds, read through the same functions
(`clockModules` takes what the page already built, so the page builds nothing twice).
The clock adds nothing to any of them.

**Three quantities are derived, none chosen.**

- **The height.** Each module carries its axles at the lowest height its own frame
  admits, and the heights differ: the fusee and the fourth→escape row at 24.8 mm, the
  escapement at 28.0, the rest at 34.4. A module can be LIFTED on fillers but never
  lowered below its own frame. So every module of a joined group is lifted to the
  group's highest line. Every frame hole stands at 5.6 + 3.2n mm, so a lift is a whole
  number of plates and fillers can make it. The probe holds each lift to that rule.
- **The half stud.** A line's x is fixed in both its modules. Moving a module by whole
  studs keeps every axle in its class: on the grid, or half a stud off it (each frame
  takes the half stud with a jumper). Where a line pairs the two classes, one module
  stands HALF A STUD over. Its 1×1 feet (3024) become jumpers (3794b) and its jumpers
  become 1×1 plates: the frames' own rule, applied in the clock's coordinates. Where
  NEITHER module of such a line can re-foot (the keyless works and the alarm, both
  framed round a stem or a column wheel), the line runs through a derived
  **half-stud adapter** (`solveAdapter`). It is a row of two 1:1 meshes whose centre
  distances sum to an odd number of half studs, so its output stands half a stud off
  its input and turns the same way. The surveyed answer, the fewest teeth that frame
  under `frameFindings`, is 8:8 × 12:12, 20 mm across, in both generations.
- **The place.** `solveClockLayout` slides the modules apart along their lines a stud
  at a time until no part of one comes within an LDU of another in PLAN, using the
  parts' boxes with every turning body swept through a whole turn. A plan gap is a 3D
  gap, so this is sufficient, and the probe then measures the parts themselves. Each
  module may be turned 0° or 180° about the vertical and each group may stand on either
  side. Of every arrangement that frames, the one on the fewest baseplate studs is the
  answer. The search takes half a minute, which the page does not spend, so the answer
  is DECLARED (`CLOCK_PLACEMENT`). The page reads it through `layoutFrom`, the solve's
  own judge, and the probe re-solves both generations and fails if either differs.

**The joint.** Where a line crosses from one module into the next, the joint is one
stock axle through both modules' columns when a stock length spans them (20N − 1 LDU,
the library's half-LDU trim at each end). Otherwise it is two axles keyed in a ridged
joiner (18948, the keyless stem's own) standing in the gap, each entering the joiner's
bore short of its web. Either way the line is one body. **The phase**: merging two
bodies forces one angle on both, so the modules are re-phased with `ldrawBuild`'s own
mesh law, each part's tooth phase MEASURED. The going train is phased from the
escapement at its rest back up to the fusee's great wheel, and the great wheel is set
through the fusee's own law (its ratchet angle, which turns the great wheel with it) to
the nearest angle that meshes. The minute line is phased from the alarm's state, and
the keyless works from it per mode, each clutch gear idle or locked to the stem as the
mode says.

**The escape line is REFUSED, by measurement.** `lineExits` walks each line's corridor
out of its module's bricks. The escapement's escape arbor is closed at both ends: in
front by its balance's pallet (3647), 3.60 mm past the arbor's bricks, and behind by
its frame's brick (3004), 8.00 mm past them. Freeing it would mean re-framing the
escapement with a driven gear on its arbor. That is a change to the escapement, not to
the clock, so it is not absorbed here (the design priority's P3 rule). It is filed as
a landing of its own. The cell carries no escapement and says so in its caption. The
probe's control takes the back knife away and watches the back end read open.

**A coupling the plan did not list.** The going train does not yet turn the hands. The
centre arbor and the minute axle are two lines here, because setting the hands through
a KEYED centre arbor would have to turn the escapement backwards. Real clocks put a
friction (the cannon pinion's) between the two, and that slip belongs with L2's alarm
set, which is where the roadmap now carries it.

### Landing L1b — the escapement driven, the escape line joined

**Why.** L1 refused the escape line by measurement, and filed the fix as the
escapement's own: "extend the escape arbor past its back brick, or move the back
knife's support off its line". Measured again, neither can work. The arbor runs at the
knife's apex less vW (28.0 mm against 38.4), and the back knife's 3044b and the top
of its stack fill the 9.6 mm under the apex, 1.6 mm into the arbor's section. So the
arbor cannot pass the knife whatever stands under it. The knife cannot leave the line
either, because the knife IS the balance's pivot. The front is the pallets'. So the
train cannot reach the arbor along its axis at all.

**The drive** (`solveDrive`, `frameEscapement(spec, bal, { drive: true })`). The
going train reaches the arbor through a TRANSFER: a gear keyed on the escape arbor,
meshing an equal gear on an INPUT axle beside it. The input axle runs back past the
back knife and out of its own two Technic bricks, open behind. Each figure is derived:

- **The ratio** is 1:1, so the arbor keeps the rate the row delivers (RATE_TABLE's law).
- **The reach.** The input axle passes the knife's 1×2 stack (±8 mm about the line) with
  its own 1×1 columns standing beside it. That needs a half stud more than the span:
  12 mm, more than the axle's own corridor (10.8).
- **The pair.** Of the parallel 1:1 meshes in both catalogues, the fewest teeth at that
  reach is 12:12 (69778) at 12 mm. The 8:8's 8 mm puts the input axle in the knife,
  which is the control.
- **Where it stands.** The transfer slab stands an AX_CLEAR behind the mass gears, which
  sweep the slab behind the wheel out to 57.2 mm. The arbor grows from 4L to 6L, the
  back knife moves two studs back (y 64 → 80 mm), and the crossbar grows from 10L
  to 12L.
- **The sense.** One mesh, so the input turns the arbor's opposite way. The clock takes
  that by the module's turn (below).

**The balance re-solved.** The longer crossbar moves the cell's balance (TECHNIC_BALANCE)
to 1.00504 s, 0.504% off its 1 s and past RATE_BAND's 0.5%. The beat is not re-targeted.
The same `solveBalance`, run on the drive's frame, answers `TECHNIC_BALANCE_DRIVE`: the
11478 mass arms carry a 24T, a 24T and a 16T a side, and the 6632 bob carries one 40T.
It beats 1.00191 s (0.191%), with d 2.535 mm. It is the only arrangement inside the band
on that frame, and the record says so: a builder's tolerance on that frame has less room
than on the cell's (five arrangements inside the band there).

**The cell is unchanged.** The escapement on `technic.html` stays undriven. Its frame,
build, probe, parts list, steps, booklet and substitutes are all as they were. The drive is
the clock's variant, and `tools/probe-technic-escapement-drive.mjs` holds it apart from
the cell's probe for a measured reason. The cell's probe is the escapement cell's judge
for its parts list's substitutes (§309). With the drive section inside it, three of the
five swaps that reach it (the 8T pallet 10928, the anchor axle 3704, the friction pin 3749)
failed at the drive's balance, because the drive's band holds one arrangement and the
cell's own balance sits on its edge. That is no fact about the escapement cell, so the
section moved. All five swaps re-judged against the restored cell probe still pass.

**The clock** (`src/technic-clock.js`):

- **The escape line** now runs from the fourth→escape row's output to the escapement's
  input axle (`TECHNIC_CLOCK.lines`). No line is refused. The going group is phased from
  the escapement at its rest, as L1's record said it was. That was untrue in L1, because
  the escapement was not in the cell, so the fusee stood as built. The phase walks back up
  the train, and the fusee is BUILT at the ratchet angle whose great wheel meshes the
  angle its centre pinion is handed (`fuseePhases`: the great wheel takes the meshing
  angle nearest its own, and its ratchet stands BAND_LAG_DEG ahead in the going sense,
  so the click and the band's pegs stand where the loop would stand them).
- **THE SENSE.** The great wheel runs its going sense, and the escape wheel must turn the
  way its escapement escapes (`goingSenseRule`). A ratio inside a module does not depend
  on its turn, so the world ratio is the product of the modules' own, read off their
  parts (any two gears at their centre distance in one slab: the fusee lists no mesh for
  its centre pinion). The rule is therefore one on the TURNS of the fusee and the
  escapement. The layout search prunes on it and the judge (`layoutFrom`) finds it. The
  probe's control turns the escapement the other way and the judge refuses it.
- **THE HOLD, and an L1 defect it fixes.** L1 laid each joint between the far faces of
  its line's bricks. The probe's new CARRIED check, which holds every part keyed on a
  line body to one of its axles, found two gears on no axle in L1 as shipped, in both
  generations, under a green probe. One was the fusee's centre pinion (z 250.5–270.5 LDU,
  its joint axle from 290.5). The other was the alarm's hour-axle 8T on the adapted
  minute line (z 138–158, the axle to 129.5). A joint's far ends are now the line's HOLD:
  its bricks and every part keyed on its body. The control cuts the joints back to the
  bricks' faces and finds the floating parts again. L1's centre and adapted-minute joints
  change with it (each now 5L + 5L in a joiner), and the escapement's transfer gear,
  in front of its input's bricks, is carried the same way.
