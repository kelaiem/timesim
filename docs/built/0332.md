## §332 — The free case's dial print follows the case: scaled to the smallest seconds face that reads

**Shipped 2026-11-05 (branch `print-follows-case`).** On the free case (`?case=free`), the dial's round print is
now a function of the case being minimised, not a fixed input to it. Before, the outline had to enclose the hands'
swept disc at their round-face length (§325 landing 9). That length came from the plate's round face, so the case
could never come in past it whatever the metal allowed.

### Why the print needs a floor

The owner asked for the dial print and the hand length to follow the case. With nothing else holding it, that
degenerates. Measured on the stop-work arrangement (cone −140°, drum 140°, alarm off), the fit with no hands
keep-in comes to 714.4 mm², but the hands' axis then stands 16.6 u (6.3 mm) from the opening's nearest side. A
track that follows that outline gives a minute hand of about 4 mm, over a seconds sub-dial still 5.56 mm in
radius. So the minimisation needs a floor, and the owner chose to scale the print as one piece (2026-11-04): the
minute hand, the marker band and the sub-dial wells come in together, so the minute hand always reaches past the
seconds sub-dial's ring. The floor is the smallest seconds sub-dial that still reads.

### The floor, derived

The seconds face is the most demanding thing printed on the dial. The fourth wheel fixes its station (D4, 18.21 u
below the hands' axis), and its well grows out to the railroad's inner rail (§125). §293 already sized its ink
by acuity at the wrist's far end (400 mm): the quarter figures' cap at the glance floor (8′), the caption's em at
the identify floor (5′), and a resolvable gap (1′) at each end of the run. Under the radius where those all fit, the paint scales
the ink below its floors (the `fit` factor). `geometry.js secondsFaceFloorMm()` solves that run for the radius
where `fit` reaches 1:

    (SECONDS_TICK_OUTER_F − SECONDS_MAJOR_LEN_F − SECONDS_CAPTION_DY_F) · sr = cap + captionEm·CAP_PER_EM/2 + 2·gap

which gives **sr = 4.04 mm** (10.66 u).

**The floor reads only the arcmin floors, never the font.** The paint also raises the caption's em to the one where
the installed monospace's heaviest weight strokes a resolvable line (`resolvableEm`, a canvas measurement). The first
version of the floor took that term too, and it differs by platform. On this landing's first CI run, Linux's
monospace moved the scale, and the case and hands with it, so `stopwork-140` held its metal 4.9835 u inside the outline
its row was cut to (need 5). The print scale sets METAL, so `secondsFaceInk` is font-free. Where a platform's
monospace needs a hair more em than the identify floor (0.8129 against 0.8115 mm on the Mac that cut the rows), the
paint's one `fit` factor absorbs it, a fraction of a percent and far above the half where it warns. The face's radial fractions moved from locals in the paint to exports
beside it (`SECONDS_TICK_OUTER_F` and its neighbours), so the paint and the floor read the same numbers. The
paint keeps its own order of operations, so the round build's print is bit-identical.

### The scale

`layout.js solveKeyless` takes the floor through `printFrame.secondsFloor` (main.js passes it on the free case
only, and only when no `?subdialr=` pins the well). It scales the round print's marker band and railroad by

    printScale = min(1, (D4 + secondsFloor + DIAL_WALL_HALF + CLEAR_MARGIN) / (dialRadius · railInnerF))

which is the print whose seconds well, grown to the rail less the ring wall and margin, is exactly the floor.
That comes to 0.88 on the free builds. It never goes over 1, since a print larger than the round face would grow
the case to suit the print. Everything the round print sized follows by the same factor: the hour hand
(`HOUR_HAND_LEN`, the marker band's inner edge), the minute hand's round length in the free fit's hands keep-in
disc (§325 landing 9's L), the telescoping hands' round minute length, and the §311 check that reads the rail the
solver read. On a form face the minute hand is still cut to the track (`DIAL_TRACK.nearestMid`), and the track
still follows the outline. Every other build passes no floor, so `printScale` is exactly 1 and nothing moves.

Sizes on the free builds:

| | round print | scaled (×0.88) |
|---|---|---|
| seconds well radius | 5.56 mm | 4.04 mm |
| minute hand (round length) | 13.04 mm | 11.47 mm |
| hour hand | 9.21 mm | 8.10 mm |

§125's assert that D4 maximises the seconds well is skipped when `printScale < 1`. D4 is the round print's
maximum, and a scaled print moves the outboard bound in on purpose.

### What sets the floor now, and what is left

Before this landing, on the stop-work row: the minute-hand disc plus the two strap flats came to 732.6 mm², the
sub-dial wells added 39.4, the movement metal 35, and the stem's port faces about 5. The strap flats are a large
share by themselves: the same row with no flats fits at 724 mm², about 8.7 mm² per mm of strap. Curved ends with
longer lugs would spend some of that, at a cost in lug length and lug stress. That is a case-design question for
the roadmap, not part of this landing. In the movement metal, the hack rod's eye on the setting lever's post is
the one part worth about 10 mm².
