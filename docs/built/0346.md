## §346 — Oil ageing between services, as a posed law: the oiled rows' friction from fresh to aged, and the balance's swing it leaves

**The ask** (the owner, after §344): model oil ageing as a posed law. §344 named
it as the one effect of the service interval that would move something the
movement really computes. Friction rises as the oil ages, so the balance swings
less across an interval and recovers at the next service. But the repository
has no degradation curve to drive it with. This lands it the only honest way
there is: as a law stated on purpose, with every part of it that CAN be derived
derived, and the part that cannot labelled.

### The law (`OIL_AGEING`, `layout.js`)

- **Which rows age** is not declared: it is read off the lubrication map.
  Every FRICTION row a lubricated regime prices (`REGIME_INFO`'s `priced`, over
  every regime but `dry`) ages: `muJewel`, `muPlain`, `muChain`, `springInt`
  and `escEff`. The rest hold their fresh values: teeth run dry by design
  (`muTooth`), and `qOther` and `endContactMm` are not a lubricant's rows.
- **The pallet oil** (the owner's second ask: it is the oil that ages first in a
  real watch) ages through `escEff`. The map's `escapement` regime used to price
  nothing; it now prices `escEff`, and the pallet row (`Escape wheel > Pallet
  fork`) names it, so the ageing law picks it up from the map like every other
  row. But `escEff` is the whole escapement's efficiency band: the drop, the
  draw, the impulse sliding and the unlocking. Only the sliding is the oil's,
  so ageing the band from nominal to adverse lays its whole spread on the oil.
  The pallet's share of the fall is an UPPER bound, and the page says so.
- **The two ends are band corners**, never new numbers. FRESH is the NOMINAL
  corner, the serviced watch, where TODO 207's design swing is held. AGED, at
  the end of an interval, is the ADVERSE corner, which `muJewel`'s row already
  calls "a thinned or aged oil". So aged oil cannot be worse than the band
  allows, and the claimed amplitude (the band's minimum) still bounds it.
- **The shape is the posed part**: each ageing row moves linearly from fresh to
  aged in the fraction of the interval since the last service, and a service
  puts it back. Linear is the shape with no parameter to choose. A real oil
  probably holds up and then fails late, which would keep the swing up for
  longer and drop it harder at the end. A measured curve would replace `shape`
  and nothing else.

### The record (`going.energy.oilAgeing`)

The energy column's per-corner walk now takes coefficients
(`GOING_POWER.at(A)`), so a corner is one reading of it (`corner(c)` =
`at(coeffs(c))`) and aged oil, between two corners, is another. The swing at
each sample is the column's own closed form, not a second copy of it. Each
stage also names the FRICTION row it is priced with (`row`), which is what lets
the gate re-derive an aged walk.

The samples are what the service-life page joins by straight lines, so their
spacing is DERIVED from the page's reading. The page quotes a swing to 0.1°
(`READ_DEG`). The spacing halves from two intervals until the swing midway
between every pair of samples reads within half of that of the line between
them, in both positions. It lands at eight intervals (nine samples), within
0.025°. `N_MAX` (1024) is a guard on the halving, and hitting it warns.

At the shipped build:

| | fresh (nominal) | aged, 5 years | fall |
|---|---|---|---|
| swing held upright | 201.65° | 144.57° | 57.08° |
| swing dial up | 293.98° | 236.77° | 57.21° |

Without the pallet oil (the first draft of this section) the aged swing was
164.37° upright, so the pallet oil's upper-bound share is 19.8° of the 57.1°.
The aged swing stays over the 126° claim. It is above the adverse corner's
(126.95°), because the teeth and the balance's other losses do not age. The swing falls under TODO 207's
200° design swing almost at once: the design swing is held at the nominal
corner, which is fresh oil, and leaves 1.65° of headroom.

Boot asserts (rule 6) warn if:

- fresh oil is not the fresh corner to 1e-9;
- the swing does not fall;
- aged oil sustains less than the claim;
- the spacing misses its reading.

### Held

- **The `equalisation` gate's row 23** re-derives every sample from the two
  corners, never reading one back. Each stage's η is linear in the one row it
  names, so an aged stage is its fresh η plus f of the way to its aged η, and
  a held row's stage keeps its fresh η. The delivery, the pivot torques (scaled
  by `muJewel`) and the swing (the quadratic's root) follow. The row also holds:
  - the rows are the map's;
  - the coefficients are the declared corners';
  - the law is declared posed and linear;
  - the first sample IS the fresh corner, exactly;
  - the swing falls, and ends at or over both the claim and the aged corner;
  - the spacing meets the reading it states;
  - the summary agrees with the samples.
- **`tools/probe-346-oil-ageing.mjs`** watches the row refuse nine sabotages
  on a cloned record. The two that matter most are whole alternative laws,
  re-walked consistently so that only the law is wrong: every row aged (the
  adverse corner whole at the interval's end), and the late-failing shape (f²
  for f). The others:
  - the grease left out of the ageing rows;
  - an aged coefficient read at the favourable corner;
  - the law not declared posed;
  - one sample's swing raised a degree;
  - a stage naming no row;
  - samples coarser than the reading;
  - a summary that disagrees with its samples.
- **`tools/service-life-data.mjs`** copies the record into
  `src/service-life-data.json` (refusing a build with none), and `--check`
  holds it on both architectures, as for the wear.
- **`tools/smoke-service-life.mjs`** holds the page, at 1280 and 390 wide:
  - between two samples the page reads the line between them;
  - a service puts the swing back to the fresh corner's;
  - just before a service it reads the record's aged swing;
  - the page calls the law posed;
  - the new drawing stands.

### The page

`service-life.html` gains:

- a readout beside the wear: the oil's age, and the swing upright and dial up
  against fresh;
- a chart of the swing over the century: a sawtooth across the twenty
  intervals, with the design swing and the claim drawn;
- a panel stating the law, with the rows that age and their two ends.

Both charts' margins now follow the label size they are drawn at. At 390 wide,
§344's depth chart had its axis title on top of the year figures.

### What it does not do

- **The oil does not feed back into the wear.** The wear rate is constant
  through each interval: the K band is meant to cover a serviced, oiled
  contact, and coupling the two would be a second posed law on top of this one.
- **It does not move the rate.** The record has no law for how the rate
  changes with the swing; §245's probe measures isochronism at one swing only.
- **It is not in the live loop.** The balance the page shows is the energy
  column's sustained swing at the aged coefficients: MODELLED, posed against
  time, not simulated. Driving the live balance (`BALANCE_DRIVE`) through an
  oil-age input would need a pose axis, and the battery would have to sweep it.
- **The pallet oil's share is an upper bound**, because `escEff` also carries
  losses no oil causes (above). Splitting the band into the faces' sliding
  (a coefficient the oil sets) and the drop and draw (which it does not) would
  make the share exact; FRICTION carries no such row yet.
