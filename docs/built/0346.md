## §346 — Oil ageing between services, as a posed law: the oiled rows' friction from fresh to aged, and the balance's swing it leaves

**The ask** (the owner, after §344): model oil ageing as a posed law. §344 named
it as the one effect of the service interval that would move something the
movement really computes. Friction rises as the oil ages, so the balance swings
less across an interval and recovers at the next service. But the repository
has no degradation curve to drive it with. This lands it the only honest way
there is: as a law stated on purpose, with every part of it that CAN be derived
derived, and the part that cannot labelled.

### The law (`OIL_AGEING`, `layout.js`)

- **Which rows age** is not declared: it is read off the lubrication map.
  Every FRICTION row a lubricated regime prices (`REGIME_INFO`'s `priced`, over
  every regime but `dry`) ages: `muJewel`, `muPlain`, `muChain`, `springInt`
  and `escEff` (since landing 2, `muPallet` in `escEff`'s place). The rest hold
  their fresh values: teeth run dry by design (`muTooth`), and `qOther` and
  `endContactMm` are not a lubricant's rows.
- **The pallet oil** (the owner's second ask: it is the oil that ages first in a
  real watch) ages through `escEff`. The map's `escapement` regime used to price
  nothing; it now prices `escEff`, and the pallet row (`Escape wheel > Pallet
  fork`) names it, so the ageing law picks it up from the map like every other
  row. But `escEff` is the whole escapement's efficiency band: the drop, the
  draw, the impulse sliding and the unlocking. Only the sliding is the oil's,
  so ageing the band from nominal to adverse lays its whole spread on the oil.
  The pallet's share of the fall was an UPPER bound, and the page said so.
  Landing 2 (below) split the band and made the share exact.
- **The two ends are band corners**, never new numbers. FRESH is the NOMINAL
  corner, the serviced watch, where TODO 207's design swing is held. AGED, at
  the end of an interval, is the ADVERSE corner, which `muJewel`'s row already
  calls "a thinned or aged oil". So aged oil cannot be worse than the band
  allows, and the claimed amplitude (the band's minimum) still bounds it.
- **The shape is the posed part**: each ageing row moves linearly from fresh to
  aged in the fraction of the interval since the last service, and a service
  puts it back. Linear is the shape with no parameter to choose. A real oil
  probably holds up and then fails late, which would keep the swing up for
  longer and drop it harder at the end. A measured curve would replace `shape`
  and nothing else.

### The record (`going.energy.oilAgeing`)

The energy column's per-corner walk now takes coefficients
(`GOING_POWER.at(A)`), so a corner is one reading of it (`corner(c)` =
`at(coeffs(c))`) and aged oil, between two corners, is another. The swing at
each sample is the column's own closed form, not a second copy of it. Each
stage also names the FRICTION row it is priced with (`row`), which is what lets
the gate re-derive an aged walk.

The samples are what the service-life page joins by straight lines, so their
spacing is DERIVED from the page's reading. The page quotes a swing to 0.1°
(`READ_DEG`). The spacing halves from two intervals until the swing midway
between every pair of samples reads within half of that of the line between
them, in both positions. It lands at eight intervals (nine samples), within
0.023°. `N_MAX` (1024) is a guard on the halving, and hitting it warns.

At the shipped build (the 14,400 movement since §343 landing 3), as landing 1
left it (landing 2's figures are below):

| | fresh (nominal) | aged, 5 years | fall |
|---|---|---|---|
| swing held upright | 200.09° | 144.31° | 55.78° |
| swing dial up | 288.04° | 232.91° | 55.13° |

Without the pallet oil (the first draft of this section) the aged swing was
163.88° upright, so the pallet oil's upper-bound share is 19.57° of the 55.78°.
The aged swing stays over the 126° claim. It is above the adverse corner's
(126.42°), because the teeth and the balance's other losses do not age. The swing falls under TODO 207's
200° design swing almost at once: the design swing is held at the nominal
corner, which is fresh oil, and leaves 0.09° of headroom.

Boot asserts (rule 6) warn if:

- fresh oil is not the fresh corner to 1e-9;
- the swing does not fall;
- aged oil sustains less than the aged corner (the bound the law guarantees; whether a build reaches the claim is TODO 192's assert, which `geared-36` already declares);
- the spacing misses its reading.

### Held

- **The `equalisation` gate's row 23** re-derives every sample from the two
  corners, never reading one back. Each stage's η is linear in the one row it
  names, so an aged stage is its fresh η plus f of the way to its aged η, and
  a held row's stage keeps its fresh η. The delivery, the pivot torques (scaled
  by `muJewel`) and the swing (the quadratic's root) follow. The row also holds:
  - the rows are the map's;
  - the coefficients are the declared corners';
  - the law is declared posed and linear;
  - the first sample IS the fresh corner, exactly;
  - the swing falls, and ends at or over both the claim and the aged corner;
  - the spacing meets the reading it states;
  - the summary agrees with the samples.
- **`tools/probe-346-oil-ageing.mjs`** watches the row refuse nine sabotages
  on a cloned record. The two that matter most are whole alternative laws,
  re-walked consistently so that only the law is wrong: every row aged (the
  adverse corner whole at the interval's end), and the late-failing shape (f²
  for f). The others:
  - the grease left out of the ageing rows;
  - an aged coefficient read at the favourable corner;
  - the law not declared posed;
  - one sample's swing raised a degree;
  - a stage naming no row;
  - samples coarser than the reading;
  - a summary that disagrees with its samples.
- **`tools/service-life-data.mjs`** copies the record into
  `src/service-life-data.json` (refusing a build with none), and `--check`
  holds it on both architectures, as for the wear.
- **`tools/smoke-service-life.mjs`** holds the page, at 1280 and 390 wide:
  - between two samples the page reads the line between them;
  - a service puts the swing back to the fresh corner's;
  - just before a service it reads the record's aged swing;
  - the page calls the law posed;
  - the new drawing stands.

### The page

`service-life.html` gains:

- a readout beside the wear: the oil's age, and the swing upright and dial up
  against fresh;
- a chart of the swing over the century: a sawtooth across the twenty
  intervals, with the design swing and the claim drawn;
- a panel stating the law, with the rows that age and their two ends.

Both charts' margins now follow the label size they are drawn at. At 390 wide,
§344's depth chart had its axis title on top of the year figures.

### What it does not do

- **The oil does not feed back into the wear.** The wear rate is constant
  through each interval: the K band is meant to cover a serviced, oiled
  contact, and coupling the two would be a second posed law on top of this one.
- **It does not move the rate.** The record has no law for how the rate
  changes with the swing; §245's probe measures isochronism at one swing only.
- **It is not in the live loop.** The balance the page shows is the energy
  column's sustained swing at the aged coefficients: MODELLED, posed against
  time, not simulated. Driving the live balance (`BALANCE_DRIVE`) through an
  oil-age input would need a pose axis, and the battery would have to sweep it.
- **The pallet oil's share was an upper bound**, because `escEff` also
  carries losses no oil causes (above). Landing 2 split the band into the
  faces' sliding (a coefficient the oil sets) and the rest (which it does not).

## Landing 2 — the escapement's band split, so the pallet oil's share is exact

**The ask** (the owner): split the escapement band so the pallet share is
exact. Landing 1 aged the whole of `FRICTION.escEff`, which also carries the
drop, the run, the pin in the fork and the fork's own momentum, so it laid the
band's whole spread on the pallet oil.

### The split

- **The faces' sliding is a LAW, not a row.** `palletFaces` in
  `src/escapement.js` reads the escapement solve's own contacts: the tooth's
  tip at P on each stone's locking face through the unlock and on its impulse
  face through the impulse. The model is quasi-static: the wheel and the fork
  are massless, as the energy column already treats the train. The tip bears
  with a normal force N into the stone and a friction μN along the face,
  against the tip's slide. The train holds the wheel, which fixes N from the
  escape torque, so where the wheel advances dδ the fork takes
  dW = T·dδ·(1 + μa)/(1 + μb), with a and b the friction's moment over the
  normal's about the fork's pivot and about the wheel's centre. The faces'
  efficiency η_faces(μ) is the beat's delivery at μ over its delivery at μ = 0.
- **Its friction is a new row, `muPallet`**: the tooth tip on the ruby stone
  under a 9415-class oil. That is the jewel pairing, so the row takes
  `muJewel`'s band by reference (`MU_RUBY_ON_STEEL_OILED` in `layout.js`, named
  once): 0.10, 0.12 and 0.15. The `escapement` regime prices `muPallet` now,
  and the pallet row names it.
- **The REST is the band over the law at the same corner**: escEff ÷
  η_faces(μ_pallet). `GOING_POWER.coeffs` derives it per corner (`escRest`), so
  each corner's escapement total is exactly `escEff`'s and no corner moved.
  The two stages are 'lever escapement: the pallet faces' and 'lever
  escapement: drop, run and pin'; the consumers that took the train path to the
  wheel (the escape tooth's rows, the drive's impulse table) exclude both.
- **The quadrature is not chosen.** Each run is Gauss–Legendre in the fork's
  angle, with the gearing dδ/dφ taken off the same moments (virtual work). The
  node count doubles until η at the harshest μ stops moving by 1e-12: 32 nodes
  a run. A first draft stepped at the midpoint and needed 16,384 steps a run
  for the same tolerance; the two agree to 1e-11.

| corner | μ_pallet | η_faces | the rest | escEff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| favourable | 0.10 | 0.7568 | 0.5285 | 0.40 |
| nominal | 0.12 | 0.7163 | 0.4886 | 0.35 |
| adverse | 0.15 | 0.6595 | 0.4549 | 0.30 |

Frictionless, the faces deliver 0.8557 of the wheel's advance: the impulse
less the recoil, the drop and the run being what they leave. So of the rest at
nominal, 0.8557 is that geometry, and 0.571 is what the band leaves for the
pin, the fork's momentum at the banking and the drop's impact, which nothing
here models.

### The share

At the shipped build, five years from a service:

| | fresh | aged | fall | the pallet oil's share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| upright | 200.09° | 153.12° | 46.97° | 10.76° |
| dial up | 288.04° | 244.04° | 44.00° | 13.42° |

The share is measured as the end of the interval with every other oil aged and
the pallet faces held fresh (163.88° upright, 257.46° dial up). Landing 1's
upper bound was 19.57° upright. The shares do not add: each stage multiplies the
others, so this is the pallet oil's share given everything else aged. The
spacing stays at eight intervals (0.022°).

### Held

- **Boot asserts** (rule 6) warn if the law has not converged; if, frictionless,
  it is not the solve's δ_E − δ_rest to 1e-9; if the gearing departs from the
  solve's own δ by central difference (1e-5), a run's normal flips side or its
  slide reverses; if a face self-locks at the band's harshest μ; or if η_faces or
  the rest leaves (0, 1] or rises with μ.
- **The `equalisation` gate's row 24** holds the split from the published nodes
  alone, without `escapement.js`: frictionless the solve's angles, the controls
  passed, each corner's faces the law at its own μ and its rest escEff over
  them, the corner's two escapement stages those and their product escEff, η
  falling and no face self-locking. **Row 23** reads the faces through that law
  at μ(f), the one stage not linear in its row, and re-derives the share.
- **`tools/probe-346-oil-ageing.mjs`** now refuses fifteen sabotages. The six
  new ones are: one node of the law moved; the frictionless law off the
  solve's angles; a corner's rest not escEff over its faces; the faces aged
  linearly in η instead of through their law (re-walked); the pallet oil not
  ageing at all, the rest cancelling it (re-walked); and landing 1's bound
  quoted as the share. Its new control re-walks the TRUE law and must pass, so
  a re-walked sabotage cannot be refused for the re-walk's own error.
- **`tools/smoke-service-life.mjs`** holds the page's quoted share and the
  faces' two efficiencies to the record, and that nothing on it calls the
  share a bound.

### What it does not do

- **The rest is still a band's remainder**, not a model: the pin in the
  fork, the fork's and wheel's momentum and the drop's impact are what
  `escEff` leaves once the faces are priced. That remainder holds through an
  interval, which is right for the pin (it runs dry) and the momentum, and
  leaves in it the fork's own jewelled pivots, whose oil (9010) does not age
  here. They are small beside the faces.
- **Quasi-static**: the fork's and the wheel's inertia do not enter the faces'
  law.
- **The power-budget probe was already stale** before this landing:
  `tools/probe-power-budget.mjs` quotes `DRUM_R_ACTUAL` as a literal, which §339
  made an expression, so it throws before it reaches the escapement. It still
  prices the escapement as one `escEff` stage, whose total is unchanged.
