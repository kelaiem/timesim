## §280 — Downturned welded lugs: a narrower section sized by the strap load, a rounded end, a shorter lug-to-lug

Commissioned by the owner (2026-10-15) to restyle the case for wearability:
- narrower lugs, with a section that stays the same along their length and
  the strap kept at 20 mm;
- the lugs angled away from the dial, so lug-to-lug comes down;
- welded to the band;
- rounded at the end, with no sharp corners.

[§190](0190.md#190--lugs-re-derived-from-the-strap-the-wrap-gap-becomes-the-source-and-the-lug-stock-becomes-one-declaration)
had already priced a downturned tip and left it for a new entry.

### What scoping got wrong, and what the owner decided

The scope assumed the lug was a 2.75 mm lever (the bar's reach past the band
wall). It is not:
- **The lever is 5.8 mm.** The lugs stand 10.6 mm off the case's centreline
  to frame a 20 mm strap, where the round band has curved back to 16.96 mm
  from centre, and the bar sits at 22.75 mm.
- **§190's lug was already over yield.** At ISO 6425's strap-attachment pull
  (200 N on the bar, 100 N per lug), its 1.2 × 3.0 mm section carried
  **322 MPa**. That is over annealed 316L's guaranteed 170 MPa and its
  typical 290 MPa, and annealed is the honest grade because the weld anneals
  the root. Nothing measured it.
- **So "narrower" has a price.** Bending strength goes as T·W², so a lug can
  only get narrower seen from the dial (T) by getting taller seen from the
  side (W).

The owner chose the load and the yield off a priced ladder: **200 N against
the typical 290 MPa**. That is narrower from the dial and slightly taller,
not narrower everywhere.

### The law: one outline, `caseLugPlan` (`layout.js`)

The lug is a SIDE PROFILE in its own (u, z) plane, read by the solid, the
schematic line tier and the probe. Every quantity in it is solved once:

- **T, the width seen from the dial**: 1.0 mm (from 1.2). It is the owner's
  pick, in the strap width's own standing: the look is chosen, and the section
  that carries it is derived.
- **The load**: `CASE_STRAP_PULL_N` 200, so `CASE_LUG_LOAD_N` is 100 per lug.
  It is a diver's standard and the only published strap load found, which makes
  it the conservative figure for a dress alarm watch.
- **The yield**: `CASE_STEEL_SIGMA_Y_BAND` (316L annealed: A240 minimum
  170 MPa, typical 290 MPa). `CASE_LUG_SIGMA_Y_PA` is the typical, by the
  owner's pick.
- **W, the height seen from the side**: **3.565 mm** (from 3.0). It is the
  lightest section whose root carries the load at that yield.
  - The section modulus is integrated over the section with its edge breaks
    rounded.
  - The critical section is where the lug leaves the band at its OUTBOARD
    face, which gives the longest arm (6.047 mm).
  - The strap pulls anywhere from radially out to straight down at the
    wrist: it wraps the wrist under the case and cannot pull through it. So the
    worst arm is the larger of the bar's two offsets from that section. Here
    the radial offset governs, which `caseLugPlan` asserts.
- **The drilled hole.** The lug is drilled through for the bar's Ø0.8 mm tip
  pin (`CASE_SPRING_BAR_TIP_D`, the catalogue figure for a Ø1.5 mm bar),
  with one `CLEAR_MARGIN` each side. §190 had sized the walls round the
  1.5 mm body, which no drilled lug carries.
- **The tip wall is the tear-out**: e = F / (2·τ_y·(T − 2·edge)), which comes
  to 0.427 mm (from §190's 0.5 beyond a 1.5 mm bore). The tip stands that
  far beyond the hole. Bearing in the hole is 125 MPa.
- **The bar's station.** It keeps §190's radius (`CASE_BAR_REACH`, the 2.0 mm
  wrap gap) and drops toward the wrist until the lug's lowest metal is **flush
  with the case back's face**.
  - §261's ratchet measures the cased depth WITH the lugs, so any lower would
    make the watch deeper.
  - The band wall stays the governing metal for the wrap gap because the
    bar's centre stays above the band's back edge (asserted).
  - **The bar now sits 1.783 mm above the back plane, from 4.093**: the strap
    leaves the case 2.3 mm nearer the wrist. That is the main wearability
    gain.
- **The path.** The centreline leaves the band sloped toward the wrist
  (44.5°), runs straight, then one arc of radius W returns it horizontal at
  the end's centre.
  - The curve is cut (forged and machined), not bent, so no forming strain
    limits it. The one limit is geometric, and R = W leaves the inside of the
    bend a radius of W/2, the same as the end.
  - The root is centred on the band's outer wall, with equal metal beyond
    each weld foot (0.238 mm).
- **The end** is a full round of radius W/2, centred so it stands the tear-out
  wall beyond the hole.
- **The weld.** Concave fillets where both edges meet the band, with radius
  surfIn − surfOut (0.617 mm), the band's own sag across the lug's width. The
  foot lands on the wall at the outboard face and is buried at the inboard
  one, so the fillet's toe is the first section wholly clear of the band.
- **The embed.** Inboard of the wall the outline closes at `caseLugRootR`
  (TODO 209's rule, unchanged).

**Lug-to-lug: 47.267 mm, from 48.000.** The whole 0.73 mm comes from the
end: a hole drilled for the tip pin plus its tear-out wall. The slope buys
none of it, because once the bar drops the wrap gap still holds it at §190's
radius. What the slope buys is the bar's height.

`CASE_LUG_W`, `CASE_LUG_Z_OFF` and §190's `CASE_LUG_TIP_WALL` derivation are
deleted. `CASE_LUG_REACH` is now `CASE_BAR_REACH + CASE_LUG_HOLE_D/2 +
CASE_LUG_TIP_WALL`.

> **Superseded in part by TODO 262 (2026-10-16).** The lug is now sized to the
> WEAKEST alloy the case may be cut from (Pt950, provisional 224.8 MPa), because
> the alloy is a finish and cannot move geometry. At that yield the sloped root
> above could not fit the band's wall at any width. The root now leaves the band
> LEVEL and reaches the bar through an S. W is 4.046 mm, lug-to-lug 47.515 mm,
> and the bar sits 2.023 mm above the case back. The record is TODO 262 in

### The solid: `makeRoundedSlab` (`geometry.js`), not an ExtrudeGeometry bevel

The first cut extruded the outline with three.js's bevel (offset −size, so the
bevel's widest point is the outline). The probe's hole control failed: the tip
pin read the hole wall 0.003 u INSIDE itself.

The cause was the bevel, not the hole. ExtrudeGeometry triangulates the cap
from the UNBEVELLED shape and only then moves its vertices. Where the bevel
pulls the outline toward a hole, the cap's triangles fold across the web.
Behind the pin that web is 0.43 mm, and cap triangles stood inside the drilled
hole. That is a self-overlapping mesh: it rendered plausibly and would have
fooled the parity raycasts.

`makeRoundedSlab` builds the same rounded slab directly:
- each layer of the edge round is the ring inset by the round's own depth
  (holes grow);
- each cap is triangulated from its OWN inset rings;
- the authored rings stand at mid-thickness, so every extreme of the part is
  the outline's own.

It carries `parameters.shapes`, so the `outlines` gate reads it as it reads an
extrude. `CASE_LUG_EDGE_R` (0.15 mm) is the one finish number (P4). It is
priced in the section modulus and in the tear-out's shear planes.

The spring bar is modelled as it is made: a Ø1.5 TUBE between the facing
walls, bored to the pin plus `CLEAR_MARGIN`, and a separate Ø0.8 tip PIN at
each end. Each pin stands out into the drilled lug as deep as the lug less its
edge round, and is guided in the bore over the same length, which is the
travel it must retract by to clear the lug when the bar is fitted. The spring
between the pins is not modelled.

The bar took two cuts to find:
- **One turned rod.** `turning` failed it at L/D 27: a Ø0.8 bar 21.7 mm long,
  which nobody turns because nobody makes one.
- **A solid tube with the pins abutting its ends.** It failed the same way:
  `turning` joins members that touch end to end into one piece of stock
  (§234), which is right for a stepped arbor.
- **A hollow tube with the pins inside its bore.** Each pin was no longer
  joined to the tube, but the two pins were joined to EACH OTHER. A gap
  bridged by a hollow member is how §234 reads a pivot either side of a wheel
  as one arbor through its bore, and across the tube the pair looks exactly
  like that. What actually fills that gap is the spring.

The outline cannot tell a spring from an arbor, so the gate now names the
parts that are FITTED into a bore rather than turned from the bar:
`TURN_FITTED_PARTS` in `inspect.js`, by member, on `TURN_END_PIVOTS`' pattern.
A member there is never joined to another and is judged on its own length.
`caseSpringBarTip` is its only member. `turning` then passes: 260 bars, 0
over L/D 20. The tube's L/D 13.3 is a follower-rest report, the same reading
§190's solid bar had.

Two measurement lessons are recorded in the probe:
- **The tip pins are their own meshes.** Their radius is read about the
  tube's fitted axis, so "the pin is coaxial with the tube" is part of the read,
  and exactly two pins must be found on each bar.
- **A hollow lathe biases the old axis fit two ways.** Its closed profile
  repeats a point, so the vertices crowd one end, and the two ends no longer
  share a seam bias. The axis came out tilted by enough to read every radius
  0.005 u long, the wrap gap 0.004 mm short and the hole control 0.011 u off.
  The direction and the circle fit now take the OUTER ring alone, which is the
  same ring at both ends. The read segment is centred on the vertices' axial
  extent, not their centroid. The reads returned to the plain cylinder's
  values exactly.
- **A drilled lug puts air on the bar's axis.** The control that used to read
  the body's abutment (≈ −bar radius) now reads the tip pin inside its hole
  and must find the drilled clearance.

`tools/probe-261-height-ledger.mjs` passes: cased depth 11.366 mm, the back
still governed by the case-back ring face with 0.000 mm over `caseLug`.
