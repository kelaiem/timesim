### The load, and why the arm is not a bar

The bush's reaction is **sideways**: the chain's pull and the great wheel's mesh
force lie in the plate's plane. So the arm bends in its own plane (its WIDTH is
the section's depth), and the leg is a column with a side load at its top and a
twist from the arm. It is sized at the **stall**, §329's rule (the stop holds the
cone at the going torque, `GOING_POWER.fuseeTorque_Nm`), with every force ADDED as
if collinear, the energy column's upper bound, by statics between the lower bush
(the base plate's mid-plane) and this one (the boss's mid-plane):

| force | N | at z | from |
|---|---|---|---|
| chain | 2.587 | 7.53 | the groove station whose pull × height is largest (r 3.294) |
| great wheel mesh | 0.557 | 2.00 | its pitch radius |
| the stop, bounded | 2.587 | 7.95 | the cone's smallest groove radius as its lever, its top as its height |
| winding input | 2.089 | 0.59 | the winding spur's pitch radius |
| **R at the bush** | **4.647** | 9.90 | (2.190 running) |

The stop is §47's lug, built thousands of lines after the cock, so its force is a
BOUND at the cock and held against the built stop at movement complete: §47 now
publishes the stop's lever (`WIND_ARREST_STOP_ARM`, the thrown beak face's
distance from the axis, the line the stall force runs along), and a lever under
the bound's or a lug over its height warns. The first form of that check read
the lug's innermost VERTEX (2.564) and fired; a vertex is not where the force
acts, and the fix was the lever, not a smaller bound.

**The arm is a fully stressed beam.** The load acts at the bush, so the in-plane
moment at distance s along the arm is R·s: nothing at the boss, R·L at the foot.
Its width is √(6·R·s/(T·σ)) at every station, floored by shear
(1.5·R/(T·b) under σ/√3: 0.611 u), with T = `COCK_T`. The first build carried
the root's width (5.31 u) the whole length and, through `makeEscapeBridge`'s
waisting, forced the boss to match: it covered **more** cone than the webs had
(55.6% clear against 73.7%). Tapered, the arm is 2.99 u wide at the cone window's
rim and the boss is the bush plus a stock floor (r 0.867).

**The yield is new.** No member had asked for the plate metal's strength before.
`NICKEL_SILVER_SIGMA_Y_BAND` (layout.js, beside `STOCK.nickelSilver`) is a band
over CuNi18Zn20's tempers, and a member is held to its low end, the annealed
temper, because nothing specifies the cock's stock as work-hardened: **172–510
MPa**, CDA's C75200 table as recalled. **That pair of figures wants a source
check**; every section here scales as σ^(−1/2) (arm) or σ^(−1/3) (leg, post), so
a 10% error moves them 5% and 3%.
