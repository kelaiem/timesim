## §320 — A volume-adjustable alarm, designed in a line: the strike station as the volume, its line spec, the level law lifted pure

### §320 landing 1 — the line design, free of any size constraint, and the law it reads

**Why.** §319 found the strike point can move a little at almost no cost in level: 0.12 dB of f₁ per percent
of the wire near the tip. The owner's ask was to turn that into a new complication, a volume-adjustable alarm,
and to design it free of any size constraint. CLAUDE.md's rule for a new action group is exactly that: design
it IN A LINE, a straight chain from input to output with no corridors, establish its LINE SPEC, and fold it into
the movement later as position-space moves only. This landing is the line. Nothing is built into the movement.

**What the station does to the sound, measured through the build's law.** The movement prices a blow as a
hand-off and a radiation (§197, §253): the head meets the mass the wire presents at the contact, m₁/φ₁(x)², so
η = 4μ/(1+μ)²·((1+e)/2)² falls as the contact moves inboard; each mode takes a share ∝ φₙ(x)²/mₙ; and each mode
radiates as a line of dipoles. On a straight bar at 2 kHz (the movement's wire, ⌀1.10 mm, 19.71 mm, the head
matched at the tip, 36.8 mg) the A-weighted level is **carried by the second mode**, 12.5 kHz, 22 dB over f₁ at
a tip strike (§262 found the same on the arc). So the level against the contact is not monotonic:

| contact, from the foot | total dBA vs tip | f₁ vs tip | f₂ vs tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.000 (tip) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 0.850 | −6.3 | +2.2 | −6.5 |
| 0.800 | −15.6 | +2.5 | −17.8 |
| 0.783 (mode 2's node) | −19.5 | +2.5 | gone |
| 0.575 (mode 2's antinode) | −1.4 | −4.8 | −1.4 |
| 0.200 | −15.1 | −28.6 | −15.1 |

The control is the run from the tip to the node, where it is monotonic, and the carriage stops at the node
because past it the level rises again. It is honest to call it a brightness-for-level control: the pitch stays
2 kHz and f₁ itself gets slightly LOUDER; what goes is the bright partial.

**The chain.** knob → selector arbor (a jumper-indexed star and a snail cam) → follower pin on a hammer carriage
sliding on a guide parallel to a straight gong bar → the hammer, carried whole, strikes the bar square at the
station the cam holds. A lift rail parallel to the slide takes the lifting lever's stroke to the tail at every
station.

**The line spec** (`src/alarm-volume.js`, every derived row from the constraint beside it):

| row | value | constraint |
|---|---|---|
| settings | 7, 3.18 dB apart | as many as the 19.10 dB range holds at ≥ 3 dB, equal in total level |
| loudest station | 0.498 mm in | the head's whole face on the wire (half its 0.997 mm width) |
| quietest station | 4.270 mm in | the level's minimum on the run: mode 2's node, 0.7834 of the length |
| carriage travel | 3.772 mm | loudest to the node, a hard stop there |
| cam base radius | 8.753 mm | the least whose every cycloidal rise holds the 30° pressure ceiling, the peak found on the profile |
| star pitch, land, rise | 45°, 8°, 37° | 7 settings and one sector for the banking stop; lands concentric |
| follower spring | 0.883 mN | 3× (`ALARM_SPRING_HEADROOM`) the guide's friction, `FRICTION.muPlain` adverse, on a posed 150 mg carriage |
| jumper | 5.00 mN, ×8.9 | inside `SELECTOR_DETENT_WINDOW_MN`, its torque over the worst mid-step back-drive by at least 3× |
| lift rail | 4.882 mm | the travel, the tail's pad (posed at the face width) and one `CLEAR_MARGIN` each end |
| worst level error | 0.160 dB | the carriage's ±0.01 mm (posed) times the level's slope, under half a step |
| blow along the slide | 0 | the hammer rides the carriage, so striking cannot move the setting |

Two P1 properties fall out of the shape rather than out of a spring: a resting setting has **no back-drive**
(the lands are concentric, so the follower's force passes through the arbor), and the blow puts **nothing
along the slide** (it is square to the bar at every station). The jumper only indexes the knob.

**A defect the probe caught.** The first base radius took the pressure angle's peak at mid-rise. It peaks a
little before, where the radius is still the smaller: 30.09° against a 30° ceiling. The probe samples the cam
as a profile, not the formula's peak, so it failed. The solve now finds the true peak by golden section and
bisects the base radius on it (8.719 → 8.753 mm).

**The law, lifted pure** (`src/gong-level.js`). The pieces the prototype needs lived inside `main.js`'s
`GONG_ACOUSTICS` block: the air, A-weighting, η, the contact spectrum, the line contact's stiffness and time,
and the far-field integrals for a planar wire. Re-deriving them in the prototype would be a law written twice,
so they moved verbatim and the build calls them with the same operations in the same order. Booted before and
after, `__clock.acoustics` is identical field for field, with timings stripped, on the round build and on the
rect spiral seat; the reference tree booted against itself is the control.
`gongRadiationFactors` (the arc's series) stays in the build: nothing here reads it.

**Two findings, recorded rather than changed.**
- The build drives every side strike as a TIP strike: `GONG_ACOUSTICS`' `driveOf` returns 1 for any blow
  across the wire, so a seat struck mid-wire would be priced with φₙ(tip). Today's seats are all end-struck
  spirals at s = 0, so nothing is mispriced now; a fold of this complication must read φₙ at the station,
  as `levelAt` does.
- The acoustics block's Poisson's ratio is 0.29 (`CONTACT_NU`, moved as it stood) while `layout.js`' `STEEL_NU`
  is 0.30. Two values for one steel; the contact time it feeds is far from limiting (measured over the run: the
  contact lasts 1.72 µs at its longest and its spectrum is at least 0.9996 for every audible mode), so no level
  here moves on it.

**The prototype page.** A section of `gong-bench.html` ("Volume — a new complication, designed in a line")
draws the chain to scale at each setting, plots the level against the contact with the stations and the stop,
lists the line spec's rows with their reasons, and plays the two partials at their modelled relative pressures
(the decay illustrative, as the bench's own). It reads `solveVolume` and `levelAt`; it restates no number.

**Measured** (local, SwiftShader Chromium for the browser rows):
- `node tools/probe-alarm-volume.mjs`: PASS on every check, under a second. The controls fail as they must:
  past the node the level is back to −1.42 dB; half a millimetre less base radius reads 31.35°; a carriage 30×
  heavier wants a 50.58 mN jumper, outside the window; a 0.2 mm tolerance costs 3.20 dB, past half a step.
- `node tools/smoke-gong-bench.mjs`: PASS on every check, at 1280 and 390: the page offers the solve's 7
  settings, the drawn hammer moves 0.498 → 4.270 mm in and the readout −0.4 → −19.5 dB, 23 spec rows, no
  console error, nothing scrolls sideways.
- The acoustics comparison above, and `tools/offline-check.mjs` with the precache at 112 (the two new modules).

**Not done.**
- The fold. The movement's gong is a spiral struck along its end; the volume group wants a straight bar struck
  across it, a carriage with 3.8 mm of travel beside it and an arbor for an 8.8–12.5 mm cam. Fitting that into a
  case is the next landing, filed in the roadmap with this entry.
- The posed rows (the land's width, the carriage's mass and its tolerance) are budgets the fold must meet.
- No absolute loudness is claimed: the levels are relative, the blow cancels, and the case path (TODO 126),
  which carries most of the real level, is not in them.
- The battery was not run.
