The technic fork

The movement's going, motion and reserve trains re-toothed to LEGO Technic's module-1 catalogue. Each chain below multiplies EXACTLY to the ratio the steel train serves, and each shaft turns by the product of the tooth counts before it.

Tooth counts, kinds, centre distances and ratios come from the declared rows. In the drawn view, part shapes, faces and colours are drawn, not LEGO's CAD, and every pair's teeth are phased to mesh. The LEGO build view assembles every row from the real parts (LDraw.org's library, CC BY 4.0) on a brick frame whose every dimension is measured off those parts. The rows use no turntable: neither could be mounted on the frame, so the solver answers with spur gears on the stud lattice. The escapement, balance and fusee have no LEGO row and are not here.

The LEGO view adds the keyless works: the stem carries two 16T clutch gears and a driving ring that slides between them, LEGO's own changeover standing in for the steel sliding pinion. wind, set and neutral are the PULL: a rack under the selector's 28T pinion, pushed in to wind and drawn out to set, turns the selector on its axle over the stem, and its changeover catch carries the ring in its groove onto the clutch gear's web (the stem stops first, as the ring's lugs need it). A DETENT holds each mode: a half bush on a thin liftarm, banded onto a small gear on the selector axle (a 12T classic, a 14T 2026), rests in one of its gaps, and a mode is one tooth. Each branch's gears are the solver's answer from the clutch gear to the steel ratio. The rests are measured off the parts; the band is drawn posed, its force not measured.
