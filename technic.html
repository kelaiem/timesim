The technic fork

The movement's going, motion and reserve trains re-toothed to LEGO Technic's module-1 catalogue. Each chain below multiplies EXACTLY to the ratio the steel train serves, and each shaft turns by the product of the tooth counts before it.

Tooth counts, kinds, centre distances and ratios come from the declared rows. The build assembles every row from the real parts (LDraw.org's library, CC BY 4.0) on a brick frame whose every dimension is measured off those parts. The rows use no turntable: neither could be mounted on the frame, so the solver answers with spur gears on the stud lattice. The escapement and balance have no LEGO row and are not here.

Beside the trains is the keyless works: the stem carries two 16T clutch gears and a driving ring that slides between them, LEGO's own changeover standing in for the steel sliding pinion. wind, set and neutral are the PULL: a rack under the selector's 28T pinion, pushed in to wind and drawn out to set, turns the selector on its axle over the stem, and its changeover catch carries the ring in its groove onto the clutch gear's web (the stem stops first, as the ring's lugs need it). A DETENT holds each mode: a half bush on a thin liftarm, banded onto a small gear on the selector axle (a 12T classic, a 14T 2026), rests in one of its gaps, and a mode is one tooth. Each branch's gears are the solver's answer from the clutch gear to the steel ratio. The rests are measured off the parts; the band is drawn posed, its force not measured.

The motion works rides the setting branch's output, the minute hand's axle. The hour wheel is a round-bored 16T clutch gear that idles on that same axle, LEGO's own clock idiom. Four meshes bring the train back to the axle it left: two cannot, because no two catalogue pairs with equal centre distances multiply to 12, and three would turn the hour hand backwards. So in set the crown turns the hands through the gears: 3 crown turns per minute-hand turn, and 36 per hour-hand turn. In wind and neutral the hands stand still, because no going train drives them here. No hand part is placed. The two angles are the reading.

The fusee is a cord on a stepped cone of grooved pulleys, climbing one step a wrap, from a barrel the spring turns. The steps are SOLVED so that torque × radius is as level as a staircase allows. The spring's law is POSED: a LEGO spring (a rubber band, a pull-back motor) can only be measured, and until it is the table holds the steel ribbon's law as a stand-in. Its maintaining power is Harrison's: a click pawl rides the ratchet, a detent holds the ratchet, and a band from it drives the great wheel while the fusee is wound back. Which way each pawl locks is read off the teeth. The bands' forces are posed. The great wheel drives the train's next wheel, the centre pinion: an 8T, five times as fast the other way, turned through the tooth counts. It drives nothing yet.

The fusee runs a loop. Running down, the cord leaves the cone one step at a time and the barrel takes it: the fusee turns at the train's steady rate, so the barrel speeds up when the cord comes down onto the larger step. At run-down a crank winds the fusee back. The click's pawl rides its wheel, the detent holds the ratchet, and the maintaining band keeps the great wheel turning forward, because the train must not stop while the watch is wound. At full wind the going spring first re-tensions the band, and the run starts again. The two bars are the measurement this module exists for: the spring's torque, which falls the whole way down, and the torque after the cone, which the steps hold in a band, each against its value at full wind. Both read a POSED spring law, and the train's rate is a display rate. The loop is modelled, not simulated: every angle comes from the cord paid through the parts' own geometry, and no force is integrated.

The alarm is the movement's alarm as LEGO. Its column wheel is two three-blade rotors, one turned over, so six columns stand 60° apart; a finger presses the end of a driver idling on its axle, whose pawl pushes a 12-tooth wheel one tooth, and a banded detent holds each of twelve rests. A press short of the detent's crest falls back; a full press steps the wheel half a column pitch, so on and off alternate. The release is a ring of three pins geared 12:1 off an hour axle, the fourth place empty: no stock disc has a notch, so the notch is built. The release lever rides the pins; where the empty place comes under it, it drops, and its far end lets go of the fly. A pin under the lever reads the column wheel: on a column the lever is held, and the alarm stays silent. The striking train steps the barrel up 4:1 to the fly's arbor, where four pins lift the hammer, which falls onto a brick: no LEGO part rings. The governor is a fly, two beams that the air brakes. It is a different mechanism from the movement's anchor, and it has no period. So the cadence and the strikes per wind are posed until a band is measured; the bands are drawn posed, and the build is shown armed, ringing or off.
