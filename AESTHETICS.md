# Aesthetics Configuration

The visual aesthetics of the clock have been extracted into a centralized configuration file (`src/aesthetics.json`) that can be edited without modifying code.

## What's Configurable

Values that affect visual appearance rather than mechanical behavior — with one exception the Gong section names: two dimensions of a modelled part whose defaults are derived and whose changes are heard as well as seen.

### Dial
- **Face**: `dial.face.color` — the base tone of the dial's silvered radial gradient. The gradient's inner and outer stops are ratios to this tone (see `DIAL_TINT_RATIO_IN`/`DIAL_TINT_RATIO_OUT` in `geometry.js`), so any tone keeps the same soft vignette rather than going flat; the printed inks are SOLVED against it at paint time since §196 — the track and sub-dial print flip between a dark pole (`DIAL_TRACK_INK`) and its mirror-derived light pole (`DIAL_TRACK_INK_LIGHT`, ≈ `#eaeaea`) by whichever holds more contrast on this tone's own grounds, and the maker's mark re-solves to the shipped pair's ~2.8:1 relation from whichever side has room, so a black dial gets light print the way a real dial does. On the shipped tone every solve returns the shipped ink verbatim. **Live** since §157 — `makeDial` exposes `userData.recolourFace`, which repaints the face canvas and every sub-dial well that derives its tone from it, so the picker updates the running scene with no reload and no geometry rebuild. Each well's blend-in tone is derived from *its own* distance off centre, so moving a station (`?d4=`, `?rsvr=`) re-derives it. It carries no `_bounds` deliberately — a colour has no interval to bound; its real constraint is legibility, which is asserted instead as a contrast floor (`DIAL_INK_CONTRAST_MIN`, 3:1, WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.11) against the printed track, at build and on every recolour. **Shareable** since §185 — it is the one finish value that travels, as `?dialcol=rrggbb`, and Copy view sets it whenever the colour differs from this file's. A link OVERRIDES a persisted local colour (§97's rule: the link and the picture must not disagree per machine) but never writes to the override store, so the recipient's own tuning is back on their next visit without the param. The contrast floor is unenforced at the link and since §196 has nothing left to catch there — the ink solve holds every face colour above it (worst pole ≈ 3.8:1), so a linked colour arrives legible and the floor's warn now flags a solve regression, not a viewer's taste.
- **Plate**: `dial.plate.sapphire` — the box sapphire dial (§3's remaining half), **on by default since §222**: the shipped watch wears a smoked sapphire dial, and untick this to get the silvered one back. On, the dial's matter — the plate, its pocket walls and the sheets its print is laid on — takes the case crystal's own glass (`CRYSTAL_GLASS` in `materials.js`, corundum's ior), while the chapter ring, the applied numerals and the feet stay metal; the keyless works, motion works, reserve train and minute jumper read through the print from the front. **Reload-tier** (⟳): the materials are chosen at build and the x-ray map is built over them. Geometry is untouched, so the fingerprint is still. The print's ink is solved against the ground that actually stands behind it — the crystal's tint over the base plate's nickel, read off the materials — and the same 3:1 gate holds it. Not modelled: a box dial's raised rim, or glass depth beyond the plate's own thickness. Browser-local; it does not travel in a link.
- **Smoke**: `dial.plate.smoke` — the sapphire dial's coat, as its visible TRANSMITTANCE T; 1 is §3's clear crystal, byte for byte, and **0.45 is the shipped coat since §222** (§220 built the knob and shipped it at 1; §222 has why 0.45 and not another number in the window). The reference is A. Lange & Söhne's “Lumen” dials, cited in README's Sources: their smoke is a thin-film coating on colourless sapphire, not a tinted crystal, and the shipped glass is already a coating model (a colour at an alpha, no thickness term), so this is a second RECIPE on §3's glass rather than a second material: light crosses the coat twice, in and back out off the nickel plate, so the plate behind contributes T², and `smokedGlass(T)` in `materials.js` folds the clear crystal's own layer and a black coat at 1 − T² into the one alpha material the renderer blends in a single pass (opacity αc + (1 − αc)(1 − T²), colour C·αc/opacity; the identity at T = 1 is asserted at boot). The plate body and the pocket walls carry the coat; the print sheets keep the clear crystal's layer, because a sheet is print ON the coated glass. The ink is solved against the composite the renderer paints — sheet over body over nickel — so the print flips to the light pole on its own as T falls (dark to 0.59, light from 0.58 on that composite, both boots held by `node tools/probe-220-smoke.mjs`) and the same 3:1 gate holds it. **Reload-tier** (⟳) for `sapphire`'s reason, and inert unless `sapphire` is on (the panel disables the slider while the box is off). Under x-ray a smoked plate is swapped for its clear twin — at 0.86 it would hide the works the toggle exists to show — and restored after. **Ships at 0.74** (§224) — a light coat the works read clearly through (works patch ΔL* 15.9 against 6.3 at §222's 0.45), above the ink flip so the print is the DARK pole: the owner's call, outside the Lumen window §222 derived, and the record says so rather than dressing it as a derivation. **And the range has a HOLE in it**: `_bounds` is a [min, max] and cannot express one, but the reserve zone gate WARNS across T 0.75–0.90 and is silent either side (measured, §224). Past the flip the ticks are dark, so a zone tone must be light to clear them; as the ground brightens through mid grey that light tone stops clearing the GROUND by 3:1 and a dark one cannot clear the TICKS, so neither direction has room until 0.91, where the ground is bright enough for a middling tone. 0.74 is therefore the last silent step UNDER the band, with zero margin above it — the face tone, the nickel and §196's solve each move that edge. Both edges are pinned by `node tools/probe-220-smoke.mjs`; `node tools/probe-224-zone-band.mjs` reports where the band is. Its `_bounds` floor, 0.41, is DERIVED, not chosen — and from the print, not the works: the reserve sub-dial's zone gate holds each coloured zone's best tone to 3:1 against both the face and the ticks, on a smoked ground the ticks go light so only black has room, and black holds 3:1 against the face only while the ground's relative luminance is at least 0.10. The rendered composite crosses that between T 0.40 (the zone at 2.99:1, boot warns) and 0.41 (3.09:1, silent), so 0.41 is the first slider step that boots silent; `node tools/probe-220-smoke.mjs` boots both sides and holds it. The works stay readable well below that: `probe-220-smoke.mjs --scan` boots T downward with the floor lifted, reads a works-only patch of the Dial-preset frame (blued steel — hands and screw heads — masked by colour), and finds where the CIELAB lightness separation between the works and the ground they stand on falls to the just-noticeable difference (ΔL* 2.3, the CIE 1976 JND) — T 0.27 at landing — an opaque-dial control must read under the JND and a clear-dial control must clear it by an order — a report beside the derived floor, and the reason the floor is the zones' and not the works'. SC 1.4.11's 3:1 was the first criterion for the works and failed its own clear-dial control at 2.56:1: the works are large shapes the eye reads at far lower contrast than that small-object floor assumes. Browser-local; it does not travel in a link. Not modelled, and said so: the luminous compound the real coating exists to charge (the sim has no lume), a non-neutral or graded smoke, the coat's second pass on specular reflections from the works.
- **Hour Markers**: Stroke width, relief depth, gap between strokes, slant proportions; `subdialMarginFactor` (clearance kept between a clipped numeral and a sub-dial rim); `weightBalanceExponent` (0 = uniform stroke width, 1 = strict equal ink per numeral — heavier numerals like VIII thin toward III's total ink); `minNumeralKeepFrac` / `minLetterKeepFrac` (how much of a numeral/letter must survive sub-dial clipping to render — at 0.3, XII and VI show as outer-half stubs)
- **Hands**: Proportions for hour, minute, and second hands (width, depth, tail, boss size)

### Materials
- **Steel finish**: `materials.steel.brush` — one slider from polished (0) to brushed (1), default brushed (§203 step 2). It reaches the works' steel and the case exterior's (`MATS.steel` and `MATS.caseMetal`, split in §203 step 1) alike. Both ends are constraints rather than tastes: at 0 the roughness sits on the renderer's own floor (0.0525 — three's shader clamps below it, so a smaller number does nothing) and the lobe is isotropic; at 1 the lobe is fully anisotropic and the across-grain roughness is the steel's authored 0.30, which is named as underived in `materials.js` rather than laundered. Between them both mix linearly. `materials.steel.brushAngleDeg` sets the straight grain's direction on FLATS (levers, springs, cocks, the bezel top — a face within cos 45° of the movement axis); everything else (arbors, pinion bodies, the band's flank, the crowns) is grained circumferentially about the movement axis, the way a lathe or a turning brush leaves it. The direction is a world-space law in the shader, never the UV tangent, because procedural geometry's UVs run wherever each builder's parametrisation runs. **Live**: the panel writes both materials with no reload, and the anisotropy define is held on across the whole range so a drag is never a shader recompile. `node tools/probe-203-brush.mjs` (a report) screenshots both ends and a turned grain and diffs them.
- **Case metal**: `materials.caseMetal.alloy` — a PICK, one of `steel` · `yellowGold18k` · `whiteGold18k` · `platinum`, default steel (§203 step 3). It reaches the case exterior only (`MATS.caseMetal`: band, back ring, stem sleeves and collars, both crowns, the pusher cap) and never the works — a spring or a pinion in gold would be a lie about the metal. Every colour but steel's is DERIVED: a metal's base colour is its normal-incidence reflectance F0, and `node tools/derive-203-alloys.mjs` integrates it from measured optical constants (refractiveindex.info's tables; Johnson & Christy for gold, silver and copper, Rakić for platinum, Weaver for rhodium) against the CIE observer, with two controls — the observer fit's integrals and Hoffman's published F0 table, which the shipping metals match to 0.06 or better. 18K white gold reads as its rhodium plate; 18K yellow is 3N (75/12.5/12.5 Au/Ag/Cu) under a named effective-medium approximation, to be replaced by ISO 8654's swatch when the standard is at hand; steel keeps its authored `#d6d9dd`, named as underived in `materials.js`. The finish slider above still governs the grain and polish of whatever metal is picked. **Live**, and it **travels**: `?metal=<key>` under §185's rule (a link overrides a persisted override, never writes it; Copy view sets it whenever the alloy differs from this file's), and the loader refuses any key outside `_options` — a retired alloy in a stale override is reported, never applied. Density is not modelled and is not claimed.
- **Ruby colour**: `materials.ruby.color` — bearing jewels, pallet stones and the impulse pin; live since §23.

### Decoration
Two finishes on the nickel plates, both SHADED rather than modelled: a world-position law in the fragment shader tilts the surface normal across each band or pearl (the shallow scallop a real lap leaves), gated to upward-facing surfaces so walls, legs and bevels stay plain. Because the coordinate comes from WORLD space, not UVs, a pattern continues seamlessly across separate parts that share the material — the stripes run unbroken from the three-quarter plate across the escape bridge by construction. Both are **live**: the panel writes the compiled shaders' uniforms (`applyDecorationFromAesthetics`), and the main panel's "Rib pitch" slider is the same knob as the advanced panel's, bounded by the schema's own `_bounds`.
- **Ribbing** (Glashütte stripes, `decoration.ribbing`): `widthUnits` is the band pitch in model units, so LARGER means FEWER ribs — the default sits at the bound maximum, ribbing at its minimum; `angleDeg` is the direction the bands PROGRESS in (the stripes themselves run perpendicular to it); `tilt` is the scallop strength, 0 for off.
- **Perlage** (circular graining on the base plate, `decoration.perlage`): stamped like the real thing — pearls on a staggered grid, each fragment shaded by the LAST pearl in application order whose radius covers it, so every pearl keeps a clean edge on one side and is shingled away on the other, and the centres are jittered so no boundary reads as a straight row. `pitchUnits` is the grid pitch; `ringFreq` the ring spacing within a pearl; `tilt` the scallop strength; `pearlRadiusUnits` the pearl's radius, which past half the pitch sets the overlap; `shingleFlip` reverses the application order; `jitterFrac` the centre jitter as a fraction of the pitch. All six are live since §203 step 4 — the last three had been compiled in at first compile and never written again while the panel called the domain live, which this file's writing found.

### Gong
The exception named above: `gong.arcDeg` and `gong.wireDiaUnits` are DIMENSIONS of the alarm gong's ringing wire, a modelled part, and both feed the synthesised tone — `f_n = (β_n L)² (d/4) √(E/ρ) / (2π L²)` for a clamped-free steel bar — so the gong is heard at the pitch its dimensions imply rather than at a chosen note. `arcDeg` is measured BACK FROM THE FREE (struck) end, so changing it moves the FOOT and leaves the hammer, its pivot and the strike point where they are; the foot never leaves the plate (a live edit is clamped to the bound §125 derived). Both defaults are DERIVED (§197) and `main.js` warns at boot if either drifts from its derivation: `wireDiaUnits` is the height of the band the ring flies in — floor the tallest metal in its own radial swath, ceiling the movement's existing back envelope, because the radiated power of a struck wire rises with its section and only space caps it; `arcDeg` is the length that puts the fundamental on 2.5 kHz, the A-weighted peak of human hearing, the foot's clear-station walk then lengthening the arc a little. `_bounds` hold `arcDeg` to 25–120° and `wireDiaUnits` to 0.4–2.63. **Live**: the panel rebuilds the wire's torus, re-sites the foot and its post, and re-solves the voice (`gongModes`) on every edit. The achieved pitch is reported by `clock.acoustics` and gated into the alarm band by the battery (see TODO 17 and TODO 127 for what is and is not claimed about loudness).

## How to Use

Edit values in `src/aesthetics.json` and reload the browser. For example:

```json
{
  "dial": {
    "hourMarkers": {
      "strokeWidthFactor": 0.14  // Increase to make markers thicker
    },
    "hands": {
      "hour": {
        "widthFactor": 0.09  // Adjust hand proportions
      }
    }
  },
  "lighting": {
    "keyLight": {
      "intensity": 2.4  // Brighten or dim the key light
    }
  }
}
```

## What's NOT Configurable

Values that are excluded because they affect mechanical calculations or create dependencies:
- Marker positioning and rotation (affects collision detection)
- Camera distance multipliers (tied to plate dimensions)
- Shadow camera bounds (tied to movement scale)
- Gear ratios and tooth spacing
- Kinematic constants

This separation ensures that tweaking the look of the clock won't break its mechanical behavior.

## Files Modified

- `src/geometry.js` - Imports aesthetics for hand and marker styling
- `src/main.js` - Imports aesthetics for lighting, rendering, scene setup, the gong's wire (rebuilt live) and the generated panel's live appliers
- `src/materials.js` - Reads `decoration.*` for the two plate finishes' shaders and `materials.*` for the ruby's colour, the steel finish (its grain law lives there) and the case alloys
- `src/aesthetics.js` - Module that exports the aesthetics configuration
- `src/aesthetics.json` - Central configuration file with all aesthetic values
